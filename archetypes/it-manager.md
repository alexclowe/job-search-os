# IT Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `it-manager` · **Aliases:** IT director, engineering manager
**Also read:** `sysadmin-cloud`, `project-manager`, `operations-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- IT Manager, IT Director, Information Systems Manager, Infrastructure Manager, Service Desk Manager, and at the top Chief Information Officer (CIO) or Chief Technology Officer (CTO)
- Adjacent worth searching: IT Operations Manager, Technical Services Manager, IT Program Manager, Engineering Manager (when the team builds software), Information Security Manager, and IT Business Partner

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and executive search firms for director level and above; school districts, hospitals, and local governments post on their own job sites
- Many IT manager roles are filled internally or through referrals, so being known inside a professional network or user group often matters

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Team size and budget you have managed, described in words you can back up, and the environment you ran (on-premises, cloud, hybrid; number of sites or users)
- Vendor and contract management, security and compliance responsibility, and major projects delivered (migrations, system rollouts, outsourcing changes)
- Certifications are often valued, not usually a gate: ITIL (an IT service management framework, certified through PeopleCert), the Project Management Professional (PMP) from the Project Management Institute, and ISACA's Certified Information Security Manager (CISM) or ISC2's Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) where security is part of the role

## Story types that land
- A major project or migration you led, including what went wrong and how you recovered
- Building or turning around a team: hiring, coaching, or fixing a struggling service desk
- A budget or vendor decision that saved money or cut risk, and how you made the case to leadership
- Handling a serious outage or security incident as the person accountable

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Two pages is normal; a short summary at the top naming scope (team, budget, users, sites), then roles with outcomes rather than duties
- "Oversaw all IT operations" reads as filler; "replaced three help desk vendors with one, and wait times dropped while the contract cost less" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, a hiring-manager or executive interview, then a panel with peers from other departments and sometimes your future direct reports
- Some employers ask for a presentation (your first months' plan, or a response to a sample budget or outage scenario)

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $175,140 a year ($84.20 an hour); most earn between $107,550 and $297,510 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-3021). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 53,500 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 15.8% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-3021). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary, often with a yearly bonus, often tied to company and department goals; ask how the bonus has paid out in recent years, not just the target
- Director-level roles in the public sector often sit on published pay grids; private-sector roles may add a car or phone allowance and a larger training budget

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, bonus target, title (manager vs director), reporting line, team and budget authority, severance terms, and start date tend to move
- Ask who you report to and what budget you control; those often shape the job more than the title

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: an "IT manager" who is also the only technician, responsibility for security with no budget or authority, a long list of hands-on tools for a leadership role, and unclear reporting lines

## Verify before relying on this
- The certification body's own site (PeopleCert, the Project Management Institute, ISACA, ISC2) for current rules; the employer's posting and organization chart; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply

- IT management isn't a regulated profession in Canada, but an engineering manager title touches the "engineer" title, which is protected in every province and territory (per Engineers Canada), so check your provincial engineering regulator if the role expects an engineering licence.
