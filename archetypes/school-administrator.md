# School Principal / Administrator — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `school-administrator` · **Aliases:** principal, assistant principal
**Also read:** `teacher`, `instructional-designer`, `school-counselor`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Principal and Assistant Principal (elementary, middle, high school), Dean of Students, Head of School at independent schools, and district roles such as Director of Curriculum, Special Education Director, Athletic Director, and Superintendent
- Adjacent worth searching: instructional coach, curriculum coordinator, charter network operations roles, and education nonprofit program director

## Where the postings live
- Usually district career pages, state education job boards, and school administrator association boards; independent schools often hire through search firms
- Many assistant-principal roles are filled from within, so leadership roles inside a school (department chair, coach, committee lead) often lead to the first offer

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A state administrator or principal certificate or licence, which usually means teaching experience, a master's in educational leadership or administration, an internship, and a state or national exam
- Evidence of leadership: teams led, data used to improve results, discipline and safety experience, and budget or schedule ownership

## Story types that land
- Raising a measure such as attendance, reading scores, or graduation rates, and how you got teachers on board
- Handling a safety incident, a discipline case, or a community conflict
- Coaching a struggling teacher, including the hard conversations
- Building a master schedule or managing a budget under constraints

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Certification and state near the top, then roles with school size and level described in words, what you led, and results from your own records
- "Transformational leader" reads as filler; "led the school's attendance team and a new advisory program" reads as credible (use your own details)

## How interviews usually run
- Often a screening interview, then one or more panels with teachers, parents and district leaders, sometimes a writing task, a data exercise, or a mock teacher-observation debrief
- Superintendent and head-of-school searches usually add a board interview and community sessions

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $105,870 a year; most earn between $76,280 and $167,770 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-9032). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 20,600 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to shrink 0.3% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a master's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-9032). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Public districts usually pay administrators an annual salary from an administrator salary schedule, often with steps, or through individual contracts, and many work a longer year than teachers
- Common extras: a pension, stipends for doctorates or extra duties, and for senior roles a multi-year contract with performance goals

## What's usually negotiable
- Step placement and contract days tend to move more than the schedule itself; for senior roles, the salary, contract length, performance bonus, car or phone allowance, and severance terms are often discussed
- Ask whether the role is covered by an administrators' union or bargaining unit

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a role posted repeatedly, "turnaround" language with no added support, one-year contracts for senior roles, and principals expected to cover large vacancies themselves

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state department of education for administrator certification; the district HR page or board minutes for the administrator salary schedule; a lawyer or association adviser for senior contracts
