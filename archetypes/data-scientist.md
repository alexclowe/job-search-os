# Data Scientist / ML Engineer — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `data-scientist` · **Aliases:** machine learning engineer, applied scientist
**Also read:** `data-analyst`, `software-developer`, `sysadmin-cloud`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Data Scientist, Senior or Staff Data Scientist, Machine Learning (ML) Engineer, Applied Scientist, and Research Scientist at the more academic end
- Adjacent worth searching: Analytics Engineer, Decision Scientist, Quantitative Analyst, Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) Engineer, AI Engineer, and Data Analyst roles that include modelling

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and tech and startup boards; research-heavy roles are also posted through university and conference job pages
- Many roles are filled through referrals and recruiters on professional networks, and public work (a portfolio, open-source code, a paper, or a competition write-up) often gets people noticed before a posting does

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Python and SQL, the libraries named in the posting (for example pandas, scikit-learn, PyTorch), and experience with a cloud platform such as Amazon Web Services or Azure, plus Git
- Whether your models reached real use (in a product, a decision, or a report people relied on) rather than stopping at a notebook
- Degree field and level: many research-leaning roles prefer a master's or doctorate, while product and analytics-leaning roles often weigh shipped work more; cloud certifications are sometimes valued but rarely decide the screen

## Story types that land
- A model or analysis that changed a decision, and how you measured the change
- An experiment (A/B test) you designed or read, including one where the result was not what people hoped
- A model that worked in testing and failed in real use, and how you found and fixed the cause
- Explaining uncertainty or a trade-off to someone who wanted a yes-or-no answer

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page early career, two for experienced; a skills line grouped by languages, libraries, and platforms; links to GitHub, papers, or a portfolio when the work is public
- "Leveraged cutting-edge AI" reads as filler; "built a demand forecast the planning team now uses to set weekly orders, replacing a spreadsheet average" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then a technical screen (SQL, Python, statistics, or a take-home), then a loop covering machine learning concepts, a case or product question, coding, and behavioral rounds
- ML engineer loops usually add software engineering and system design for model serving; research roles may ask you to present a past paper or project

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $120,230 a year ($57.80 an hour); most earn between $67,240 and $199,130 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 15-2051). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Stock or equity:** grants aren't in these pay figures, so compare them separately.
- **Openings:** about 24,800 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 34.6% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 15-2051). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some employers add an annual bonus, and some, especially tech companies, add stock or equity grants that vest over several years plus a sign-on bonus
- Contract data science work tends to pay hourly or by project with fewer benefits, so compare total pay rather than the rate alone

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, level or title, the size of any equity grant, sign-on bonus, start date, remote or hybrid days, and conference or compute budget tend to move
- Ask how the level was decided; the gap between data scientist and ML engineer levels at the same employer can be wide, so check which ladder you are on

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a "data scientist" role that is mostly dashboards, one person expected to build the whole data platform and the models, a wish list of every tool at expert level, and take-home tasks that look like unpaid client work

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's posting and level guide; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** 21211, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- "Engineer" is a protected title in every province and territory (Alberta exempts only the title "software engineer"; per Engineers Canada and APEGA), so a machine learning engineer title without an engineering licence can breach the law; check your provincial engineering regulator, and expect some postings to say data scientist or machine learning developer instead.
