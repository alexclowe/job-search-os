# Project Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `project-manager` · **Aliases:** project coordinator, IT project manager, program manager, scrum master
**Also read:** `construction-manager`, `product-manager`, `operations-manager`, `it-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Project Coordinator, Project Manager, Senior Project Manager, Program Manager, and IT, Implementation, or Technical Project Manager
- Adjacent worth searching: Scrum Master, Delivery Manager, Project Management Office (PMO) Analyst, Project Controls Specialist, and Operations Manager when the work is ongoing rather than time-boxed

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages and the general job boards; many project roles are posted inside a department (IT, construction, marketing, healthcare), so search by industry as well as title
- Contract project work often comes through staffing and consulting firms, and many coordinators move up internally

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The size and type of projects you ran, described in words (budget owned, team size, duration, number of stakeholders), and the method used: traditional plan-driven (waterfall), agile or scrum, or a mix
- Tools named in the posting, such as Microsoft Project, Jira, Smartsheet, or Excel
- Certifications are often valued, not usually a hard gate: the Project Management Professional (PMP) from the Project Management Institute (PMI) asks for documented experience leading projects plus project-management education; its Certified Associate in Project Management (CAPM) is the entry option; the Certified ScrumMaster (CSM) from Scrum Alliance and the Professional Scrum Master from Scrum.org are common for agile teams

## Story types that land
- A project delivered on time and on budget, and what you did when one of the two slipped
- A scope change you managed without derailing the plan, and how you got agreement
- A risk you spotted early, what you did about it, and what would have happened otherwise
- Getting people who don't report to you to do what the project needed

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One to two pages; each role names the project type, budget or team size in words you can back up, and the method used; certifications near the top
- "Managed multiple projects" reads as filler; "ran the move of the billing system to a new vendor, kept both running until cut-over, and closed the old contract on schedule" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, a hiring-manager interview, then a panel with the people you would work with; many use behavioral questions ("tell me about a time") about slipping schedules and difficult stakeholders
- Some employers ask you to walk through a sample project plan, a risk log, or a status report you'd send

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $102,320 a year ($49.19 an hour); most earn between $61,580 and $167,970 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-1082). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 76,500 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 6.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-1082). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some employers add a yearly bonus tied to delivery, and contract project managers are commonly paid hourly or by day through an agency without benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, title (coordinator vs manager), remote or hybrid days, start date, and paid study time or exam fees for a certification tend to move
- Ask whether the role is permanent or funded by one project; the answer often changes how hard to push on salary versus severance or notice terms

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a "project manager" title with coordinator pay and no authority over scope or budget, several unrelated projects with no support, "wear many hats", and a certification listed as essential for a junior role

## Verify before relying on this
- The certification body's own site for current eligibility (pmi.org, scrumalliance.org, scrum.org); the employer's posting for the method and tools; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply
