# Production / Manufacturing Supervisor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `production-supervisor` · **Aliases:** manufacturing supervisor, plant supervisor
**Also read:** `operations-manager`, `manufacturing-worker`, `industrial-engineer`, `warehouse-supervisor`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Production Supervisor, Manufacturing Supervisor, Line Supervisor, Assembly Supervisor, Shift Supervisor, Area or Department Supervisor, Team Leader or Group Lead on the production floor, and Production Manager at the senior end
- Adjacent worth searching: Operations Supervisor, Quality Supervisor, Continuous Improvement Lead, Production Planner or Scheduler, Plant Superintendent, and Value Stream or Area Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually manufacturer career pages, the general job boards, and staffing firms that place plant leadership; many plants promote leads and operators from the floor before posting outside
- Referrals from plant managers and people you have worked with on other lines often surface roles before they are posted, especially for off-shift supervision

## What a recruiter screens for first
- How many people and which shift you have supervised, the kind of production (assembly, food, chemical, machining), and the numbers you were accountable for, such as safety, quality, output, and on-time delivery
- Familiarity with lean manufacturing tools (5S, standard work, root-cause problem solving), the plant's enterprise resource planning or timekeeping system, and safety programs such as lockout/tagout; union-plant experience is commonly screened where the workforce is organized
- Shift availability, especially nights and weekends, and whether you have led through a change such as a new line or a layoff

## Story types that land
- Turning around a shift or line: the problem, what you measured, what you changed, and what happened to safety, scrap, or output
- A safety incident or near miss you handled, and the fix that stuck
- Coaching or disciplining an employee fairly, including under a union contract
- Working with maintenance, quality, and engineering to fix a recurring problem

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Each role names the plant type, shift, team size, and products, with one or two results per role in plain terms; a short skills line lists lean tools, systems, and safety training
- "Results-driven leader who drives excellence" reads as filler; "supervised a night shift of line operators on two packaging lines, cut changeover time and led the plant's first lockout/tagout audit" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then interviews with the plant or operations manager and HR, commonly with behavioral questions about conflict, safety, and missed targets; many plants add a floor tour
- Some employers ask you to walk through a production problem or read a shift report and say what you would do first

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $74,450 a year ($35.79 an hour); most earn between $47,130 and $108,750 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 51-1011). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 61,000 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 1.8% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 51-1011). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Either an annual salary or hourly pay: many supervisors are salaried and classified exempt from overtime, while some, especially lead or working supervisors, are paid hourly and are owed overtime under federal and state rules, so ask how the role is classified
- Off-shift supervisors commonly receive a shift differential, and some plants add a bonus tied to safety, quality, or output; hourly supervisors may be offered guaranteed hours, while salaried ones tend to work long weeks without extra pay

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: the base salary or rate, shift assignment, the bonus target, start date, and relocation help; ask whether a shift differential applies to salaried supervisors too
- Ask how often supervisors are expected to cover extra shifts and whether that time is paid

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "salaried" supervision with mandatory extra shifts every week, supervising several lines or shifts alone, a posting that lists operator duties as much as leadership, and high supervisor turnover the interviewers won't explain

## Verify before relying on this
- The Department of Labor's overtime guidance for how supervisors are classified; the pages of OSHA (the Occupational Safety and Health Administration) for the safety programs the plant names; the employer's own posting for the shift and team size

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** no clear match. No single unit group fits; NOC groups production supervisors by industry in unit groups 92010 to 92015 and 92020 to 92024, for example 92012 for food and beverage processing and 92023 for mechanical and metal products.
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Supervisors in unionized plants usually sit outside the bargaining unit, and overtime rules for managers and supervisors vary by province under employment standards law; check your provincial employment standards office and Job Bank for regional wages.
