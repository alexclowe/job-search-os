# Photographer — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `photographer` · **Aliases:** portrait photographer, commercial photographer, photo editor, studio photographer
**Also read:** `graphic-designer`, `social-media-manager`, `writer-editor`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Photographer with the specialty named: Portrait, Wedding and Events, Commercial or Advertising, Product or E-commerce, Real Estate, Sports, School and Graduation, Photojournalist, and Studio Photographer; plus Photo Editor, Retoucher, and Photo Assistant
- Adjacent worth searching: Content Creator or Visual Content Specialist, Social Media or Marketing Photographer in-house, Videographer, Digital Asset Manager, Photo Studio Manager, and Imaging Technician

## Where the postings live
- Employed roles usually post on company career pages (in-house marketing, e-commerce studios, school portrait companies, news outlets, universities) and the general and creative job boards
- Most photographers are self-employed, and freelance work commonly comes from referrals, a portfolio website, social media, directories, second-shooting for established photographers, and agencies that book shoots

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A portfolio in the style and specialty of the role, shown first and edited tightly; then the camera, lighting, and editing tools you use (such as Adobe Lightroom and Photoshop), and whether you bring your own gear
- For freelance clients, reviews, a clear package and price list, business insurance, and contracts; for drone work in the US, the FAA remote pilot certificate (Part 107)
- Availability for weekends and travel, and turnaround speed for edited images

## Story types that land
- A shoot that went wrong (light, weather, a late client) and how you still delivered
- Working with a nervous subject or a large group and getting a natural result
- A commercial brief where you matched a brand's style and deadline
- Building a repeat client or referral stream from one job

## Resume conventions for this profession
- The portfolio leads and the resume supports it: a short resume listing specialties, clients or employers by type, tools, and credits; freelancers often list recent client types and publications rather than job titles
- "Passionate visual storyteller" reads as filler; "shot and edited weekly product images for an online store, set up the lighting kit, delivered within the week" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a portfolio review with the hiring manager or creative director, sometimes a paid test shoot or an editing test; in-house roles commonly add a panel with marketing
- For freelance work, the "interview" is usually a consultation call where the client asks about style, packages, timeline, and contract terms

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $21.47 an hour ($44,660 a year); most earn between $14.82 and $44.14 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 27-4021). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 10,700 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to shrink 0.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 27-4021). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 67% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Employed photographers, photo assistants, and studio staff are commonly paid hourly or an annual salary; portrait and school photo roles are often seasonal, so ask whether the role has guaranteed hours or rises and falls with the season
- Freelancers usually charge a day rate or half-day rate for commercial work, a package or per-project fee for weddings and portraits, and sometimes a usage or licensing fee for how long and where the images are used
- Hourly employees usually earn overtime under federal and state law for long event weeks; freelancers don't, so they tend to price long days into the rate; rush, weekend, and holiday fees often do the job that shift differentials do in other work
- Freelance income commonly has to cover gear, insurance, software, travel, and taxes, so compare it after expenses against a salaried role with benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- Employed roles often have room on pay rate, gear allowance, editing time, and credit or portfolio rights to the images you make
- Freelance work: day rate, usage rights, number of edited images, turnaround time, deposit, and cancellation terms tend to move; ask for them in a written contract

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "exposure" or credit in place of pay, unpaid test shoots that look like real client work, contracts that take all rights for a small fee, clients who won't sign a contract or pay a deposit, and employed roles asking you to bring your own gear without an allowance

## Verify before relying on this
- The FAA for drone pilot rules; the U.S. Copyright Office for image copyright and registration; your state and city for business licences and sales tax on prints or packages

## In Canada

- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Photography isn't a regulated profession, but self-employed photographers commonly need to register for GST or HST with the CRA once revenue passes the small-supplier threshold, drone work needs a drone pilot certificate from Transport Canada, and in Quebec, bilingual English and French client work is common; check your province and the federal drone rules before flying.
