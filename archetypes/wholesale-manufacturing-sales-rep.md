# Wholesale / Manufacturing Sales Rep — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `wholesale-manufacturing-sales-rep` · **Aliases:** territory sales rep, technical sales, medical device sales, sales engineer
**Also read:** `b2b-sales-rep`, `sales-manager`, `buyer-purchasing-agent`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Sales Representative, Outside Sales Representative, Territory Sales Representative, Account Representative, Inside Sales Representative, Route Sales Representative, Technical Sales Representative, and Sales Engineer, with the product line named (industrial, building products, food service, medical device, chemicals)
- Adjacent worth searching: Key Account Manager, Distributor or Channel Sales Manager, Manufacturer's Representative (often independent and commission-only), Customer Account Specialist, and Product Specialist

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and industry-specific boards; distributors and manufacturers commonly post territory roles by region
- Many roles are filled through referrals from customers, suppliers, and other reps in the same industry, and specialist sales recruiters are common for medical device and technical sales

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Industry and product knowledge, the customer types you sold to (contractors, plants, hospitals, restaurants), your territory, and your results against quota in plain numbers from your own record
- A valid driver's licence and clean driving record for outside territories; technical roles often ask for a degree in engineering or science; medical device roles commonly need hospital vendor credentialing (background checks, immunizations, training); most roles need no professional licence
- Experience with a CRM (customer relationship management system) such as Salesforce and with quoting or ERP systems

## Story types that land
- Winning a new account from a competitor and how you did it
- Growing an existing account by adding product lines or sites
- Solving a customer's technical or supply problem that kept the business
- Opening or rebuilding a territory

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One to two pages; each role names the products, territory, customer types, and results from your own record such as quota attainment, revenue growth, or new accounts won
- "Proven closer" reads as filler; "grew territory revenue by a fifth in two years by adding three distributor accounts" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter or HR screen, then interviews with the regional or district sales manager and sometimes a ride-along with a current rep
- A role-play ("sell me this product" or a mock customer call), a 30-60-90-day plan presentation, or a review of your past quota results is common, and some employers ask for proof of earnings such as a W-2 or commission statements

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $72,080 a year ($34.65 an hour); most earn between $39,090 and $137,550 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 41-4012, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 127,200 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 3 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 41-4012). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually a base salary plus commission or bonus on sales or gross margin; the split between base and variable pay (the pay mix) varies by company and product, and independent manufacturer's reps are commonly commission-only
- New reps may get a draw against commission while they build the territory; ask whether it is recoverable (paid back from later commission) or non-recoverable
- Commission on repeat orders from existing accounts (residuals) is often paid at a lower rate than on new business, and returns or unpaid invoices are commonly charged back against commission
- Many roles add a car allowance or company car, expense account, and phone; ask whether commission is capped and how accelerators work above quota

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: base salary, the commission rate and quota, guaranteed pay or a draw during ramp-up, the car and expense package, and which accounts come with the territory
- Ask who keeps the house accounts and client list if you leave, whether earned commission on shipped orders is paid after you go, and what the non-compete says

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: commission-only roles described as "base plus commission," quotas raised every year with no territory growth, a territory recently split or taken back, commission caps, and high turnover in the same territory

## Verify before relying on this
- The written commission plan and offer letter for quota, caps, chargebacks, and payout after you leave; your state labor department for rules on paying earned commission; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** 64101; 62100, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Most wholesale sales roles aren't licensed in Canada, but commission and overtime rules for salespeople vary by province under employment standards, and territories that include Quebec often ask for French; check your province's employment standards and the commission plan.
- "Engineer" is a protected title in every province and territory (Alberta exempts only the title "software engineer"; per Engineers Canada and APEGA), so using a sales engineer title without a provincial engineering licence can breach the law, and Canadian postings often use another title for this role (per Engineers Canada); check your provincial engineering regulator before using it.
