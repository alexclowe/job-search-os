# Accountant / Auditor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `accountant` · **Aliases:** staff accountant, senior accountant, auditor, CPA
**Also read:** `bookkeeper`, `financial-analyst`, `financial-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Staff Accountant, Senior Accountant, General Accountant, Cost Accountant, Financial Reporting Accountant, Tax Accountant, Auditor, Internal Auditor, and Audit or Tax Associate at a public accounting firm, with the manager and senior manager versions of each
- Adjacent worth searching: Accounting Manager, Assistant Controller, Revenue or Payroll Accountant, Financial Analyst, Tax Preparer during filing season, and government auditor or financial examiner roles

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages and the general job boards; public accounting firms often recruit graduates through campus recruiting and internships a year or more ahead of the start date
- Accounting and finance staffing agencies commonly fill contract, temp-to-hire, and month-end or audit-season roles, and many experienced hires move through referrals from former colleagues or audit clients

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A bachelor's degree in accounting (or enough accounting coursework), and for public accounting or senior roles, a CPA (Certified Public Accountant) licence or clear progress toward it
- The US CPA path in plain words: accounting education, the Uniform CPA Exam, and supervised experience, licensed by your state board of accountancy; many states now offer a newer pathway (a bachelor's degree plus two years of experience instead of the extra coursework, per NASBA), so check your own board
- The systems named in the posting (for example QuickBooks, SAP, Oracle NetSuite, or other ERP software, meaning the company's main finance system), Excel skill, and the work itself: month-end close, reconciliations, audit, tax, or financial reporting under US GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles)

## Story types that land
- A month-end or year-end close you ran or sped up without losing accuracy
- A reconciliation or audit finding you traced to its cause and fixed for good
- A control, process, or system change that reduced errors or manual work
- Explaining a financial result or an audit issue to someone outside finance in plain words

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page early career, two for experienced; CPA status (licensed with state, or exam sections passed) near the top; each role names the work (close, audit, tax, reporting), the size or type of company or client, and the systems used
- "Detail-oriented and results-driven" reads as filler; "owned the close for four entities and cut close time by two days" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the controller or audit manager and sometimes a partner; technical questions about journal entries, reconciliations, accruals, and how the three financial statements connect are common
- Some employers add an Excel test or a short case, and public accounting firms may run a group or "superday" format for graduates

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $83,680 a year ($40.23 an hour); most earn between $56,020 and $144,090 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-2011). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 115,300 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 5.0% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-2011). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; many employers pay overtime or a busy-season bonus only for non-exempt staff roles, while exempt roles may instead get an annual bonus
- Many employers support the CPA with exam fees, review courses, study leave, or a raise or bonus when you pass or get licensed

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, title or level (staff vs senior), CPA exam support and study time, a pass bonus, remote or hybrid days, and start date often move
- Ask how busy-season hours are handled; comp time or a busy-season bonus may be negotiable where overtime isn't paid

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one person doing the whole accounting function at a staff-level salary, "CPA preferred" with no support for getting it, heavy busy-season language with no mention of time off or bonus, and a "Senior Accountant" title with mostly bookkeeping or data entry duties

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state board of accountancy, NASBA (the National Association of State Boards of Accountancy), and the AICPA (American Institute of CPAs) for current CPA exam, education, and experience rules; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** 11100, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Accounting is regulated provincially: the CPA designation follows CPA Canada's national route (the CPA Professional Education Program, the Common Final Examination, and practical experience), delivered and granted by provincial bodies such as CPA Ontario, and signing audit or review reports for the public usually needs a separate public accounting licence in provinces such as Ontario. A licence from a state board doesn't carry over automatically, and whether a US licence earned through the newer 120-hour route qualifies for US–Canada mutual recognition is worth checking, so check your provincial CPA body.
