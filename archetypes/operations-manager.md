# Operations Manager / General Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `operations-manager` · **Aliases:** general manager, director of operations, business manager
**Also read:** `retail-store-manager`, `production-supervisor`, `logistics-supply-chain`, `project-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Operations Manager, General Manager, Director of Operations, Business Manager, Center Manager, Department Manager, District Manager, and Plant Superintendent, with the industry named (manufacturing, distribution, healthcare, hospitality, services)
- Adjacent worth searching: Operations Director, Site or Branch Manager, Chief Operating Officer at smaller companies, Business Operations Manager, Program Manager, and Continuous Improvement Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and professional networks; many senior roles are filled through executive recruiters or retained search firms and often aren't posted publicly
- Internal promotion is common, so roles may go to a supervisor or assistant manager already inside; networking with owners, private-equity-backed companies, and industry associations often surfaces roles before they are posted

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Scope in plain terms: the size of the team, budget, sites, or revenue you ran, and the industry, because operations experience tends to transfer best within a similar business
- Profit-and-loss (P&L) responsibility, results against targets, and tools such as Excel, an ERP system (the company's main business system), and dashboards; some industries value Lean or Six Sigma training, and a degree may be asked for at larger companies

## Story types that land
- Turning around a site, shift, or department that was missing its targets
- Cutting cost, waste, or cycle time without hurting quality or safety
- Hiring, coaching, or restructuring a team, including handling a hard people decision
- Launching a new site, process, or system and getting people to adopt it

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Two pages is common for experienced managers; a short summary naming industry and scope, then each role with its team size, budget or revenue, and the results you owned
- "Drove operational excellence" reads as filler; "ran a two-shift distribution center and cut mis-picks by a third in a year" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the owner, regional leader, or executive team, and sometimes a panel with the people who would report to you
- Case-style questions ("our site is missing its numbers; what do you do in your first month?"), a site tour, or a short presentation of a plan may be part of later rounds

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $105,770 a year ($50.85 an hour); most earn between $50,090 and $253,390 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-1021). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 285,000 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 5.0% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-1021). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary plus a bonus tied to site, company, or personal targets; the bonus share tends to grow with seniority, and some companies add profit sharing or a car allowance for multi-site roles
- Ask how the bonus is calculated, what share of it has paid out in recent years, and whether it is prorated in your first year

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary, the bonus target and how it is measured, title, sign-on bonus, relocation help, vacation, and start date often move for manager-level hires
- Ask for severance or a first-year bonus paid regardless of results, in writing, when you are leaving a stable role for a turnaround or a company in transition

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a general manager title with no authority over hiring or budget, several recent people in the same seat, "wear many hats" covering operations, sales, HR, and finance alone, and a bonus described as "uncapped" with no target or history

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's own posting and offer letter for bonus terms; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing; any industry licence your sector needs (for example, food safety or facility permits) with the relevant state agency

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** no clear match. No single unit group fits; senior managers are grouped under NOC 00012 to 00015 by industry.
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- There is no single occupation code for this role; senior managers are grouped by industry, so check the senior and middle manager unit groups for your industry on the NOC site from Statistics Canada and on Job Bank before comparing pay.
