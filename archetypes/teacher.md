# Teacher — job-search archetype

**Slug:** `teacher` · **Aliases:** classroom teacher, K-12 teacher, elementary teacher, middle school teacher, high school teacher, special education teacher
**Also read:** `substitute-teacher`, `teaching-assistant`, `early-childhood-educator`, `tutor`, `instructional-designer`, `school-administrator`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Teacher with grade band and subject (Elementary, Middle School Math, High School English, Special Education, ESL/ELL, CTE), Long-Term Substitute, Instructional Coach, Interventionist, Department Chair, Curriculum Specialist, Assistant Principal (with credential)
- Adjacent worth searching: Instructional Designer, Learning Specialist, Education Program Coordinator, Tutor/Academic Coach, EdTech customer success or curriculum roles

## Where the postings live
- Usually district and school career pages, state or regional education job boards and consortia, independent-school associations, charter networks, and the general boards for private and EdTech roles
- Hiring is usually seasonal; postings cluster in spring for the following school year, with a second, smaller wave in late summer

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Certification/licence for the state, grade band, and subject (or a clear path to it), background-check eligibility, student-teaching or classroom experience in the same band, and any endorsements the posting names (SPED, ESL, reading)
- Districts often screen on a standard application system before a human reads the resume; answer the application questions as carefully as the resume

## Story types that land
- A student or class that was behind and what you changed in your instruction (not just "differentiated")
- A unit or assessment you designed and what you learned from the results
- A parent or colleague relationship you repaired or built
- Classroom management: a specific routine and why it worked

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Certifications and endorsements at the top; grade levels, subjects, and class sizes per role; curricula and platforms by name (the district's LMS, assessment tools)
- Avoid "passionate about learning"; prefer what you taught, to whom, and what happened. Portfolios and sample lesson plans are commonly requested at interview

## How interviews usually run
- Often a screening interview, then a panel with the principal and teachers; many schools ask for a demo lesson or a sample unit, and some add a writing sample or a data-use question
- Expect questions on classroom management, differentiation, family communication, and how you use assessment results

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $63,970 a year; most earn between $47,960 and $104,340 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 25-2021, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 240,900 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 8 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 25-2021). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Public schools usually pay from a published salary schedule: a step for years of experience and a lane for education (bachelor's, master's, credits beyond); stipends are added for coaching, extra duties, or hard-to-fill subjects
- A pension or state retirement system is part of total pay in most public districts; private and charter schools may set salary individually

## What's usually negotiable
- The schedule itself rarely moves; step placement (how many prior years are credited, including out-of-state or private-school years) and lane placement for coursework often can
- Stipend roles, start-of-year classroom budget, and mentoring support are worth asking about

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: multiple subjects across grade bands in one role, "must be willing to coach" without stipend language, roles posted repeatedly through the year, vague "other duties as assigned" carrying a heavy load

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state's department of education for certification and reciprocity rules; the district's HR page for salary schedules and application steps; the school's most recent public report for context on the role

- Teachers are certified by the province or territory, not nationally, and most public-school pay follows a grid in the collective agreement set by qualification category and years of experience; French-language and immersion roles usually ask for proof of French proficiency, so check your province's teacher certification body first.
