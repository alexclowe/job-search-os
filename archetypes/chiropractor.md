# Chiropractor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `chiropractor` · **Aliases:** DC
**Also read:** `personal-trainer`, `therapy-assistant`, `medical-assistant`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Chiropractor, Doctor of Chiropractic (DC), Associate Chiropractor, Chiropractic Physician, and Clinic Director
- Adjacent worth searching: Sports Chiropractor, Chiropractor in a multidisciplinary or integrated clinic, Independent Contractor Chiropractor, and roles in hospital, military, or veterans health systems where they exist

## Where the postings live
- Usually private and group chiropractic practices, franchise and membership clinics, multidisciplinary clinics, and chiropractic college and state association job boards
- Many associate roles are filled through school connections, former preceptorships, and practice-sale or practice-transition listings

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A Doctor of Chiropractic degree from a program accredited by the Council on Chiropractic Education (CCE), passed exams from the National Board of Chiropractic Examiners (NBCE), and a licence from the state board; some states add their own exam
- Techniques you use, the patient types you see, and your comfort with the clinic's model (insurance-based, cash, or membership)
- Some roles ask about your willingness to do community screenings or marketing events to bring in new patients

## Story types that land
- A patient whose function improved, told with the assessment and plan
- Building or keeping a patient base: reactivation, referrals, or a new referral source
- Working with physicians, therapists, or trainers on a shared patient
- Clean documentation that held up to an insurance review

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Degree, state licences, and NBCE exams near the top; techniques and certifications listed; each role names the practice model and typical visit volume
- "Wellness warrior" reads as filler; "treated neck and low-back patients in a multidisciplinary clinic, coordinated care with physical therapy" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a conversation with the practice owner, then a working interview or shadow day; some owners ask you to adjust them or a staff member
- Questions commonly cover your techniques, how you explain care plans, your view of marketing and new patient generation, and the contract terms

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $79,200 a year ($38.08 an hour); most earn between $43,460 and $146,080 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 29-1011). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 2,600 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 8.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a doctoral or professional degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 29-1011). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 36% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Associates may be paid an annual salary, a base plus production-based bonus, or production-only pay (a share of what you bill or collect); ask whether "production" means billed or collected amounts
- Independent contractor and practice-owner income depends on patient volume, insurance mix, and overhead, so the employee pay figures above may not describe it

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary, the production percentage and when it starts, malpractice coverage, continuing education money, and a path to buy into or buy the practice tend to move
- Ask about non-compete and non-solicit terms, who keeps the patients if you leave, and whether you are an employee or a contractor

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: production-only pay with no base for a new graduate, large non-compete radius or length, heavy marketing quotas, and pressure to sell long prepaid care plans

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state chiropractic board and the Federation of Chiropractic Licensing Boards (fclb.org) for licensure; NBCE (nbce.org) for exam rules; your contract for pay, production, and non-compete terms
