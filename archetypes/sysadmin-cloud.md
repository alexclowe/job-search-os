# Systems / Network / Cloud Administrator — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `sysadmin-cloud` · **Aliases:** sysadmin, network engineer, cloud engineer, DevOps
**Also read:** `it-support`, `cybersecurity-analyst`, `software-developer`, `it-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Systems Administrator, Network Administrator, Network Engineer, Cloud Engineer or Administrator, Infrastructure Engineer, and the senior and lead versions of each
- Adjacent worth searching: Site Reliability Engineer, DevOps Engineer, Platform Engineer, Network Architect, Microsoft 365 or Identity Administrator, and Linux or Windows Server Engineer

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages and the general job boards; managed service providers, data centres, hospitals, schools, and government post many infrastructure roles
- Many roles are filled through staffing agencies and referrals, and a lot of admins move up from the help desk inside the same employer

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The platforms in the posting matched word for word: Windows Server, Linux, Active Directory, VMware or another virtualization tool, a cloud platform (Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, or Google Cloud), networking gear, and scripting in PowerShell, Bash, or Python
- Automation and infrastructure-as-code tools (Ansible, Terraform) often separate mid-level from junior candidates
- Certifications are often valued: CompTIA Network+ or Security+, the Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA) from Cisco for network roles, Red Hat certifications for Linux, and the cloud providers' own administrator or architect certifications
- On-call availability and, for some government or defence work, whether you can hold a security clearance

## Story types that land
- An outage you handled: how you found the cause, how you communicated during it, and what you changed afterwards
- A migration you ran (to the cloud, to new hardware, to a new identity system) with little or no downtime
- Something you automated that removed repeated manual work or cut errors
- A cost or capacity problem you spotted and fixed

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One to two pages; a skills block grouped by operating systems, cloud, networking, and scripting; certifications with the issuer
- "Maintained a robust infrastructure" reads as filler; "moved file shares to cloud storage over a weekend and retired two aging servers without user downtime" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then a technical interview with scenario questions ("the website is slow; where do you start?") and questions on networking basics, DNS (the system that turns names into addresses), permissions, and backups
- Some employers add a hands-on lab or a take-home script, and senior roles may include a design discussion for a small environment

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $99,130 a year ($47.66 an hour); most earn between $62,640 and $155,050 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 15-1244, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 23,000 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 15-1244). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; on-call pay varies widely, from a per-shift or per-incident payment to nothing extra because it is folded into salary, so ask how on-call is paid and how often you would carry it
- Some employers add a yearly bonus; contract and agency roles tend to pay hourly with fewer benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, title (administrator vs engineer), on-call pay and frequency, remote or hybrid days, and paid certification exams or training tend to move
- Ask what the on-call schedule looks like in practice and how after-hours work is compensated; that often matters as much as the base salary

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one person expected to run networking, servers, cloud, security, and the help desk; "available as needed" with no on-call pay; and senior titles paired with junior pay ranges

## Verify before relying on this
- The certification body's own site (CompTIA, Cisco, Red Hat, or the cloud provider) for current exams; the employer's posting for on-call terms; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** possibly 21222 or 22220. This is an uncertain title match by the maintainers, not an official concordance, so check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site first.
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- "Engineer" is a protected title in every province and territory (Alberta exempts only the title "software engineer"; per Engineers Canada and APEGA), so Canadian postings often say administrator, specialist, or architect instead of network or cloud engineer; check your provincial engineering regulator before putting an engineer title on your resume.
