# Marketing Specialist / Market Research Analyst — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `marketing-specialist` · **Aliases:** marketing coordinator, digital marketer, SEO/search marketing strategist
**Also read:** `marketing-manager`, `social-media-manager`, `data-analyst`, `pr-specialist`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Marketing Specialist, Marketing Coordinator, Digital Marketing Specialist, Content Marketing Specialist, Email Marketing Specialist, SEO or Search Marketing Specialist, Paid Media Specialist, Marketing Analyst, and Market Research Analyst
- Adjacent worth searching: Content Strategist, Communications Specialist, Marketing Operations or Automation Specialist, Customer Insights Analyst, Growth Marketer, and Product Marketing Associate

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, marketing and agency job boards, and professional networks; agencies commonly hire for specialists across many clients
- Many roles are filled through referrals and portfolios seen online; freelance and contract work often turns into full-time offers

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The channels you've run (email, search, paid social, content, events), the tools named in the posting (such as Google Analytics, a marketing automation or CRM platform like HubSpot or Salesforce, Canva or Adobe tools), and results from your own records
- For research and analyst roles: survey design, data analysis in spreadsheets, SQL, or statistics tools, and turning findings into a clear recommendation
- A portfolio or work samples (campaigns, content, dashboards, reports) and a marketing or business degree for many roles, though experience often counts more

## Story types that land
- A campaign you ran: the audience, the offer, what you tested, and what happened
- Using data or research to change a decision (a new segment, a dropped channel, a better message)
- Growing a channel over time (email list, search traffic, organic social) and what drove it
- Working with sales, product, or an agency to hit a launch date

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page for most specialists; a skills line by channel and tool; each role with channels owned and results from your own records, with a link to a portfolio; mark any figure you can't confirm and fill it in before sending
- "Drove brand awareness" reads as filler; "wrote and tested weekly emails to a customer list, moved the welcome series to a shorter sequence, and reported results in Google Analytics" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then an interview with the marketing manager, commonly a take-home exercise (a campaign plan, a writing sample, an analysis of a data set) and a panel with cross-functional partners
- Expect questions on a campaign that didn't work, how you measure success, how you'd prioritize channels with a small budget, and walking through your portfolio

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $78,760 a year ($37.87 an hour); most earn between $43,390 and $155,480 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-1161). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 82,000 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 7.0% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-1161). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some roles add a modest bonus, and agencies and startups vary widely; hourly and freelance roles are common too, so ask whether overtime is paid and how revisions are billed
- Freelance and contract marketing work is common and usually paid hourly, per project, or on retainer, so compare it against salaried pay plus benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: base salary within the band, title (specialist vs senior or manager), remote or hybrid days, training and certification budget, and start date
- Ask how the level was set; a title step up often moves pay more than negotiating within a level

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one "marketing specialist" expected to own every channel, design, and analytics alone; unpaid take-home work that looks like a real client project; "fast-paced" with no budget mentioned; and entry-level titles asking for many years of experience

## Verify before relying on this
- The posting's own tool and channel list; any platform's official certification pages for current requirements; the Federal Trade Commission's guidance on endorsements and email marketing rules for the US

- Commercial email and text marketing falls under Canada's anti-spam legislation (CASL), which asks for consent before sending, and in Quebec, French-language rules apply to much consumer-facing marketing, so bilingual English and French skills are often asked for; check the posting and the CASL guidance before relying on US email practice.
