# Plumber / Pipefitter — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `plumber` · **Aliases:** pipefitter, steamfitter
**Also read:** `hvac-technician`, `electrician`, `welder`, `construction-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Apprentice Plumber, Plumber's Helper, Journeyman Plumber, Master Plumber, Service Plumber, Residential or Commercial Plumber, Pipefitter, Steamfitter, and Sprinkler Fitter, up to Foreman or Lead Plumber
- Adjacent worth searching: Maintenance Plumber for hospitals and campuses, Drain or Sewer Technician, Backflow Tester, Gas Fitter, Medical Gas Installer, Pipe Welder, Plumbing Estimator, and Plumbing Inspector

## Where the postings live
- Usually plumbing and mechanical contractor sites, service company listings, facilities career pages, and the general job boards; union plumbers and pipefitters are commonly dispatched through the local union hall
- Many shops hire through referrals and supply-house contacts, and apprenticeship programs tend to open on set application cycles, so it helps to check dates early

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Your licence level and where it is valid: in the US, plumbing licences are usually issued by the state or city, commonly moving from apprentice to journeyman to master through documented hours, classroom training, and an exam on the plumbing code the jurisdiction has adopted
- The kind of work you have done (new construction, service and repair, commercial, process piping), reading prints, and add-on credentials such as backflow testing, medical gas, or welding certifications; a valid driver's licence is commonly screened for service roles
- Whether you have your own hand tools, can take on-call or emergency calls, and can work in tight or dirty spaces

## Story types that land
- A system you installed or repiped to code that passed inspection first time
- A tough service call: finding a hidden leak or blockage and fixing it without tearing out more than needed
- Explaining a repair and its price clearly to a homeowner or facility manager
- A safety habit on gas, confined spaces, or hot work, or training an apprentice

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Licence type, number, and state near the top, then add-on certifications, then each job with the setting (residential service, new commercial, industrial piping) and the systems you worked on
- "Experienced plumber, all aspects" reads as filler; "service plumber running a solo truck, water heaters, drain cleaning, and repipes, with backflow testing certification" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short interview with the owner, service manager, or foreman, sometimes with code questions or a ride-along; apprenticeship programs usually add an aptitude test and a panel interview
- Expect questions on the work you have done, how you handle an upset customer or an emergency call, and whether you can take on-call shifts

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $30.67 an hour ($63,800 a year); most earn between $21.22 and $52.13 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 47-2152). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 42,000 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 6.8% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 47-2152). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly: union plumbers and pipefitters are commonly paid on the local's published union scale with benefit and pension contributions, and apprentices earn a rising share of the journeyman rate; non-union shops tend to pay a shop rate
- Overtime is common on emergencies and shutdowns, and federal and state rules usually set when it is owed; many service shops add on-call pay or after-hours differentials, and some pay flat-rate per job or a commission on sales, so ask how pay works on a slow week and whether the shop offers guaranteed hours

## What's usually negotiable
- At non-union shops the hourly rate, a take-home truck, on-call pay, tool allowance, and paid training toward the next licence often move; union scale usually doesn't, though overtime opportunity can
- Ask how the on-call schedule works and how often after-hours calls actually come in

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: licensed work offered to someone without the licence, "commission only" service roles, on-call every night with no extra pay, and pay "depending on experience" with no range

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state or city plumbing board (CareerOneStop's License Finder lists them; link, don't copy) for licence levels and exam rules; the Department of Labor's apprenticeship site for registered programs; the union local for current scale

- Job Bank lists plumber certification as compulsory in Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, New Brunswick, Quebec, Ontario, Saskatchewan, and Alberta, and steamfitter-pipefitter certification as compulsory in several provinces, with Red Seal endorsement available for mobility; gas fitting is usually a separate licence, so check your provincial apprenticeship authority for each trade.
