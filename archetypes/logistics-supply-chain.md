# Logistician / Supply Chain Analyst — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `logistics-supply-chain` · **Aliases:** supply chain analyst, logistics coordinator, transportation/distribution manager
**Also read:** `buyer-purchasing-agent`, `warehouse-supervisor`, `operations-manager`, `data-analyst`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Logistics Coordinator, Logistics Specialist or Analyst, Logistician, Supply Chain Analyst, Supply Chain Planner, Demand or Inventory Planner, Transportation Analyst, and Supply Chain or Logistics Manager at the senior end
- Adjacent worth searching: Production Planner, Materials Planner, Import/Export or Trade Compliance Coordinator, Distribution Manager, Procurement Analyst, and Operations Analyst

## Where the postings live
- Usually manufacturer, retailer, distributor, and third-party logistics career pages, freight and transportation company listings, and the general job boards; government and defence contractors post many logistics roles on their own sites
- Many roles also fill through recruiters who specialize in supply chain and through professional association job boards

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The part of the chain you know (planning, inventory, transportation, warehousing, trade compliance), the systems you have used (enterprise resource planning, warehouse or transportation management systems, spreadsheets and reporting tools), and the volume or spend you handled
- Credentials that commonly help: ASCM certifications (Association for Supply Chain Management, which issues CPIM for planning and inventory, CSCP for the wider supply chain, and CLTD for logistics), a degree in supply chain or business, and customs or hazardous-materials training where the role ships regulated goods
- Analytical skill shown through a result, such as a forecast you improved or a freight cost you cut

## Story types that land
- A cost or service improvement: the problem, the data you used, the change, and the result
- Handling a disruption such as a late shipment, a supplier failure, or a port delay, and how you kept customers supplied
- Getting planning, purchasing, and warehouse teams to agree on one plan
- Building a report or model that others kept using

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A skills line near the top listing systems and methods, then each role with the part of the chain you owned and one or two measured results; certifications follow
- "Supply chain guru who optimizes everything" reads as filler; "planned inventory for a regional distribution network, cut stockouts by tightening reorder points in the planning system" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the hiring manager and cross-functional partners; many analyst roles add a spreadsheet exercise or a case about a supply problem
- Expect questions on how you prioritize when two customers need the same stock, how you handled a disruption, and which systems you have used

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $82,320 a year ($39.58 an hour); most earn between $50,890 and $133,160 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-1081, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 44,900 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-1081). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some employers add an annual bonus tied to company or team results, and entry coordinator roles are sometimes paid hourly and eligible for overtime, so ask how the role is classified
- Managers in transportation or distribution may carry on-call duties for after-hours shipment problems, so ask whether that is paid

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: base salary within the band, title or level, bonus target, remote or hybrid days, start date, and paid certification or training
- Ask how the level was decided; a higher level often moves pay more than negotiating within a level

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one role covering planning, purchasing, shipping, and customs alone, "coordinator" pay for analyst or manager scope, constant after-hours availability with no mention of coverage, and tool lists far beyond the role

## Verify before relying on this
- ASCM (ascm.org) for certification details; the Occupational Outlook Handbook from the BLS (Bureau of Labor Statistics) for how the field is changing; the employer's own posting for the systems and scope
