# Medical Assistant / Phlebotomist — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `medical-assistant` · **Aliases:** CMA, clinical medical assistant, phlebotomy tech
**Also read:** `medical-admin`, `nursing-assistant-caregiver`, `billing-collections`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Medical Assistant, Clinical Medical Assistant, Certified Medical Assistant (CMA), Registered Medical Assistant (RMA), Phlebotomist or Phlebotomy Technician, and Lead Medical Assistant
- Adjacent worth searching: Ophthalmic or Optometric Assistant, Chiropractic Assistant, Patient Care Technician, EKG (electrocardiogram) Technician, Specimen Processor, and Patient Service Representative when you want a front-office role

## Where the postings live
- Usually physician practices, urgent care centers, outpatient clinics, and health-system career sites; phlebotomy roles also sit with outpatient labs, blood donation centers, and hospitals
- Staffing agencies commonly fill temporary and contract clinic roles; externship sites often hire the students they trained

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A medical assisting certificate or diploma and a national certification; the ones most often named are the CMA from the American Association of Medical Assistants (AAMA), the RMA from American Medical Technologists (AMT), and the Certified Clinical Medical Assistant (CCMA) from the National Healthcareer Association (NHA)
- Most states don't license medical assistants, but a few set their own rules: Washington, for example, issues its own Medical Assistant-Certified credential, and a few states license phlebotomists, so check your state health department
- Skills named in the posting: vital signs, rooming patients, injections, blood draws, EKGs, and the charting system (for example Epic or eClinicalWorks)

## Story types that land
- Keeping a busy clinic on schedule: rooming patients quickly without skipping the checks
- A blood draw or procedure with an anxious patient, a child, or hard-to-find veins
- Catching an error: a wrong patient, a missing allergy, a mislabeled specimen
- Helping a patient understand the doctor's instructions or a new medication

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Certification and CPR or BLS (Basic Life Support) near the top with expiry dates; a skills list (vitals, injections, venipuncture, EKG, charting system) then each job with the specialty and patient volume
- "Detail-oriented team player" reads as filler; "roomed patients for a busy family practice, gave vaccines, drew labs and scheduled referrals in Epic" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the practice manager or lead medical assistant, sometimes with a provider joining
- Some clinics add a skills check (vital signs, a blood draw on a practice arm, or a charting exercise); questions commonly cover patient privacy under HIPAA (the federal health privacy law), handling a rude patient, and what you do when a provider is running late

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $21.97 an hour ($45,690 a year); most earn between $17.33 and $28.52 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 31-9092, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 127,700 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a certificate or diploma program (not a degree), the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 31-9092). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with overtime paid for extra hours; shift differentials tend to be less common in daytime clinics but may appear for evening, weekend, or hospital phlebotomy shifts
- Ask about guaranteed hours in part-time or float roles that cover several clinics
- Some employers add pay for certification or for bilingual skills; temp agency roles often pay more an hour with fewer benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- The starting rate within the range (often by certification and years of experience), certification pay, schedule, and which locations you float to tend to move
- Tuition help toward nursing or another clinical program and paid certification renewal are often worth asking about

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: front-desk, billing, and clinical duties bundled into one role at a single rate, "floating" between many sites with no mileage pay, and a long list of duties that may be outside what your state lets medical assistants do

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state health department or medical board for what medical assistants may do and whether phlebotomy needs a state licence; the AAMA, AMT, and NHA sites for certification rules; CareerOneStop's License Finder for your state
