# HVAC Technician — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `hvac-technician` · **Aliases:** HVAC-R technician, service tech
**Also read:** `electrician`, `plumber`, `maintenance-technician`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- HVAC Apprentice or Helper, HVAC Installer, HVAC Service Technician, HVAC-R (refrigeration) Technician, Refrigeration Mechanic, Commercial HVAC Technician, and Lead or Senior Service Technician (HVAC stands for heating, ventilation, and air conditioning)
- Adjacent worth searching: Building Automation or Controls Technician, Boiler Technician, Chiller Technician, Facilities or Building Maintenance Technician, Boiler Operator, and HVAC Sales or Comfort Advisor roles for techs who like the selling side

## Where the postings live
- Usually heating and cooling contractor sites, mechanical contractors, facilities and property-management career pages, and the general job boards; union refrigeration and pipe trades are commonly dispatched through the local union hall
- Many shops hire year-round but step up hiring before the summer and winter peaks, and referrals from supply houses and other techs often move faster than a posting

## What a recruiter screens for first
- EPA (Environmental Protection Agency) Section 608 certification: federal rules say technicians who maintain, service, repair, or dispose of equipment that could release ozone-depleting refrigerants must be certified, by type of equipment or universal; many employers screen for it first
- Residential versus commercial experience, electrical troubleshooting, the equipment and controls you know, and any state or city HVAC or refrigeration licence where one applies; optional industry certifications such as NATE (North American Technician Excellence) tend to help
- A valid driver's licence, on-call availability, and whether you have your own hand tools and gauges

## Story types that land
- A no-heat or no-cool call you diagnosed on the first visit, and how you tracked the fault
- An install done clean and to code, from load check to startup
- Handling a stressed customer in the middle of a heat wave or cold snap with honesty about options and price
- A refrigerant-handling or electrical safety habit, or training a newer tech

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Certifications near the top (EPA Section 608 type, any licence, NATE or manufacturer training), then each job with residential or commercial focus, the equipment you service, and whether you ran your own truck
- "Top-notch HVAC tech" reads as filler; "residential service tech running a solo truck, heat pumps and furnaces, maintenance agreements, EPA universal" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often an interview with the service manager, commonly with a troubleshooting scenario, a written test, or a ride-along; some shops add a driving-record check before an offer
- Expect questions on diagnosis, electrical basics, on-call nights, and how you would present repair or replacement options to a homeowner

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $29.33 an hour ($61,010 a year); most earn between $19.25 and $45.78 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 49-9021). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 40,600 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 10.9% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a certificate or diploma program (not a degree), the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 49-9021). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with overtime common in the summer and winter peaks; federal and state rules usually set when overtime is owed, and some shops add on-call pay or after-hours differentials
- Some shops pay flat-rate per job, and some pay commission on sales, such as a spiff for selling a replacement system or a maintenance agreement; ask whether sales commission can be charged back if a customer cancels or financing falls through, and whether anything is paid when an agreement you sold renews
- Hours can drop in the mild seasons, so ask whether the shop offers guaranteed hours year-round

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: the hourly rate (by certifications and experience), a take-home truck, on-call pay, tool allowance, paid training or licence fees, and how commission on sales is calculated
- Clients usually stay with the shop if you leave, along with the maintenance agreements you sold, so ask how commission on a pending sale is handled after you go; licensing costs and renewal help are also worth asking about

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "unlimited earning potential" tied mostly to sales quotas, pressure to sell replacements over repairs, no mention of EPA certification support for new techs, and on-call every week with no extra pay

## Verify before relying on this
- The EPA's Section 608 technician certification pages for certification types and rules; your state or city licensing office for any HVAC or refrigeration licence (CareerOneStop's License Finder lists them; link, don't copy); the Department of Labor for overtime rules
