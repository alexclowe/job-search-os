# Mental Health / Substance Use Counselor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `mental-health-counselor` · **Aliases:** LPC, LMHC, LMFT, therapist, addiction counselor
**Also read:** `social-worker`, `school-counselor`, `human-services-assistant`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Mental Health Counselor, Licensed Professional Counselor (LPC), Licensed Mental Health Counselor (LMHC), Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist (LMFT), Therapist, Clinician, Substance Use or Addictions Counselor, Certified Alcohol and Drug Counselor, and associate or pre-licensed versions of each
- Adjacent worth searching: case manager, intake or crisis clinician, clinical supervisor, program director, and employee assistance program counselor

## Where the postings live
- Usually community mental health agencies, hospitals and health systems, group practices, substance use treatment programs, and the general job boards; many counselors also find roles through their supervisor, internship site, or professional association
- Group practices and telehealth platforms often recruit continuously, and some private-practice work comes through insurance panels and referrals rather than postings

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Your licence level in the state: usually a master's degree in counseling, a national exam (the NCE or the NCMHCE from the National Board for Certified Counselors, depending on the state), and supervised post-degree clinical hours as an associate before full licensure; marriage and family therapists and addiction counselors follow their own state boards; the Counseling Compact is live in some member states and may let a counselor licensed in one member state practise in others, so check whether your state has joined
- The populations and settings you know (adults, youth, couples, substance use, serious mental illness), the approaches you use, and whether you're credentialed with the insurance plans the employer bills
- Whether you can bill independently or still need supervision, and your comfort with the employer's electronic health record

## Story types that land
- A client you helped reach a treatment goal, told without identifying details
- A crisis or safety assessment you handled calmly and documented well
- Keeping documentation and treatment plans current on a full caseload
- Working with psychiatrists, case managers, schools or families to coordinate care

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Licence type, state and number (as the employer asks) near the top, with any associate status and supervisor arrangement; then settings, populations, caseload size and approaches by name
- Never include client details; "provided therapy to diverse clients" reads as filler, while "adult outpatient caseload, anxiety and trauma focus, individual and group sessions" reads as credible (use your own details)

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the clinical director or supervisor; case vignettes ("how would you handle this first session?") and questions about risk assessment are common
- Many employers ask about documentation habits, productivity expectations, supervision needs, and how you manage burnout

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $59,350 a year ($28.53 an hour); most earn between $38,940 and $97,590 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 21-1018, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 57,400 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a master's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 21-1018). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Agencies, hospitals and schools usually pay an annual salary, sometimes with a productivity target of billable sessions a week
- Group practices and telehealth platforms often pay per session or a percentage of what's billed, which can mean no pay for no-shows or denied claims; private practitioners set their own fees and handle their own insurance billing
- Supervision toward licensure is sometimes provided free as part of the job and sometimes charged or deducted, so ask

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: the salary or per-session rate, the productivity target, paid supervision toward licensure, a raise when you become fully licensed, continuing-education money, and a mix of in-person and telehealth days
- Ask how late cancellations, no-shows and paperwork time are paid

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: contractor status with employee-level control of your schedule, high weekly session targets with no paid admin time, pay only on collected claims, supervision fees taken from pay, and caseloads the posting won't describe

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state counseling board (or marriage and family therapy or addiction board) for licence levels, supervised hours and exams; the National Board for Certified Counselors (nbcc.org) for the NCE and NCMHCE and its state board directory; CareerOneStop's License Finder

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 41301, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Job Bank says counselling therapists need registration in Nova Scotia and in New Brunswick, psychotherapy needs a permit in Quebec and registration with the CRPO in Ontario, Prince Edward Island regulates the counselling therapist title through the College of Counselling Therapy of Prince Edward Island, and rules differ elsewhere; many employers also expect membership in a professional association, so check your province's regulator before relying on a US licence.
