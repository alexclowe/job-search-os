# Occupational Therapist — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `occupational-therapist` · **Aliases:** OT, OTR/L
**Also read:** `speech-language-pathologist`, `therapy-assistant`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Occupational Therapist (OT), Registered and Licensed Occupational Therapist (OTR/L), Pediatric OT, Acute Care or Inpatient Rehab OT, Home Health OT, School-Based OT, and Senior or Lead OT
- Adjacent worth searching: Certified Hand Therapist, Early Intervention OT, Assistive Technology Specialist, Driver Rehabilitation Specialist, Ergonomics or Industrial Rehabilitation Consultant, and Rehab Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually hospitals and rehab centers, skilled nursing facilities, home health agencies, outpatient and hand clinics, schools, and early intervention programs; the national OT association's job board and state association boards carry many roles
- Therapy staffing and travel agencies commonly fill short-term contracts; school roles tend to post on district boards on the school calendar

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A degree from an accredited OT program (a master's or doctorate), a passed certification exam from the National Board for Certification in Occupational Therapy (NBCOT), which gives the OTR title, and a state licence
- The setting and population you know (pediatrics, adult rehab, hand therapy, mental health) and experience with that setting's documentation and billing
- Any specialty certification the role names, such as Certified Hand Therapist

## Story types that land
- Helping someone regain an everyday task (dressing, cooking, returning to work or school) and how you adapted the plan
- Adapting equipment or a home to make someone safer
- Managing a caseload or productivity target while keeping documentation current
- Working with the care team or family to plan a safe discharge

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Credentials near the top (OTR/L, state licences, specialty certifications); each role names the setting, population, and caseload
- New graduates list fieldwork placements with setting and population
- "Client-centered therapist" reads as filler; "inpatient rehab caseload after stroke and joint replacement, ran ADL retraining and home safety evaluations" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a screen with a recruiter or rehab manager, then an interview with the lead therapist; some include a case scenario, a tour, or a shadow shift
- Questions commonly cover goal writing, working with a patient who won't participate, productivity expectations, and discharge planning

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $100,330 a year ($48.24 an hour); most earn between $71,690 and $131,950 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 29-1122). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 10,000 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 14.8% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a master's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 29-1122). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary in hospitals, clinics, and schools, and some schools pay on a published teacher-style schedule
- Home health and some skilled nursing and contract roles commonly pay per visit, so compare how many visits fit a day and whether drive time, documentation, and missed visits are paid; per diem roles often pay hourly with no benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary or per-visit rate, sign-on bonus, productivity target, continuing education money, and schedule tend to move
- Ask about paid licence fees, mileage, mentoring for new graduates, and how productivity is counted

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: very high productivity targets, per-visit pay with unpaid documentation time, "flexible" home health roles with no minimum visits, and roles that count group or co-treatment time in ways that feel off

## Verify before relying on this
- NBCOT (nbcot.org) for certification rules; your state OT licensing board for the licence; the American Occupational Therapy Association (aota.org) for state licensure steps
