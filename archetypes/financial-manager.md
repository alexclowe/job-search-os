# Finance Manager / Controller — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `financial-manager` · **Aliases:** controller, treasurer, finance director
**Also read:** `accountant`, `financial-analyst`, `bookkeeper`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Controller, Assistant Controller, Finance Manager, Accounting Manager, Financial Reporting Manager, Financial Planning Manager, Treasurer or Treasury Manager, Director of Finance, and Credit Manager
- Adjacent worth searching: FP&A (financial planning and analysis) Manager, Fractional or Part-Time Controller, Chief Financial Officer at smaller companies, Financial Systems Manager, and bank roles such as Branch Manager or Business Banking Manager when your background is in banking

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and professional networks; many controller and finance director roles are filled through finance and accounting recruiters or executive search
- Referrals from auditors, bankers, and former colleagues often surface roles before they are posted, and private-equity-backed companies commonly hire through their investors' networks

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The scope you ran: company size, number of entities, team size, and whether you owned the close, audit, budgeting, cash, and reporting to owners, lenders, or a board
- A CPA (Certified Public Accountant) licence is often preferred for controller roles, and some FP&A and treasury roles value the CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst) or CMA (Certified Management Accountant); ERP systems (the company's main finance system) such as SAP or NetSuite, and advanced Excel

## Story types that land
- Speeding up the close or getting through an audit with fewer adjustments
- Building a budget, forecast, or cash plan that leaders actually used to decide something
- Fixing controls after an error or finding, or preparing a company for a financing, sale, or system change
- Building or coaching a finance team

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Two pages is common; a summary naming company size, industry, and credentials, then each role with scope (revenue, entities, team) and results you owned
- "Strategic finance leader" reads as filler; "cut the monthly close from twelve days to six and led the first clean audit in three years" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the CFO (Chief Financial Officer), CEO, or owner, and sometimes board members or investors; technical questions about the close, revenue recognition, controls, and forecasting are common
- Some employers ask for a case or a model review, such as walking through a set of financials and saying what you'd look into

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $166,570 a year ($80.08 an hour); most earn between $94,310 and $323,270 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-3031). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 65,600 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 9.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-3031). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary plus a bonus tied to company and personal targets; the bonus share tends to grow with seniority
- Some private companies offer a share of profits or a payout if the company is sold; ask how any such plan works and put the terms in writing
- Fractional or interim controllers are commonly paid a day rate or monthly retainer instead of a salary

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary, the bonus target and how it's measured, title (controller vs director), severance, remote days, and relocation help often move at this level
- Ask whether a sale or change of control would trigger any payout or severance, since finance leaders are often affected first

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a controller title that is really a hands-on bookkeeper role, a history of late or qualified audits, several people in the seat recently, and "fast-paced startup" with no finance team and no budget for systems

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state board of accountancy for CPA status; the AICPA (American Institute of CPAs) and the relevant certification bodies for CFA or CMA requirements; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 10010, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Controller and finance manager roles often ask for the CPA designation, earned through CPA Canada's national route (the Professional Education Program, the Common Final Examination, and practical experience) and granted by provincial bodies such as CPA Ontario; a licence from a state board doesn't carry over automatically, and US licences from the newer 120-hour route may not qualify for mutual recognition, so check your provincial CPA body's rules for accountants trained outside Canada.
