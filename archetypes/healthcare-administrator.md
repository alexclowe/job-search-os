# Medical & Health Services Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `healthcare-administrator` · **Aliases:** practice manager, clinic manager, health administrator
**Also read:** `office-manager`, `operations-manager`, `compliance-officer`, `medical-admin`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Practice Manager, Clinic Manager, Health Services Manager, Department or Service Line Manager, Director of Operations, Health Information Management (HIM) Manager, Nursing Home Administrator, and Administrator or Executive Director
- Adjacent worth searching: Revenue Cycle Manager, Patient Access Manager, Quality or Performance Improvement Manager, Program Manager in public health or behavioral health, and Administrative Fellow for early-career people with a graduate degree

## Where the postings live
- Usually health-system career sites, physician groups and management companies, long-term care operators, and the general boards; healthcare executive and medical practice management associations run job boards too
- Senior roles are often filled through executive search firms and internal promotion; administrative fellowships commonly recruit on a yearly cycle

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The kind of operation you ran (a clinic, a department, a facility), its size in staff and budget, and whether you have been accountable for a profit and loss or a budget
- A degree in health administration, business, or public health is common for larger roles; certifications such as the Fellow of the American College of Healthcare Executives (FACHE) or the Certified Medical Practice Executive (CMPE) can help
- Nursing home administrators are licensed by the state, usually after a national exam from the National Association of Long Term Care Administrator Boards (NAB) plus state requirements; most other management roles have no licence

## Story types that land
- A turnaround: a clinic or department with access, staffing, or financial trouble, and what you changed
- A revenue cycle or cost improvement you can explain plainly (fewer denials, better scheduling, lower overtime)
- A regulatory survey, accreditation visit, or compliance problem handled well
- Recruiting and keeping staff through a hard stretch, or opening a new service or site

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Two pages is normal for experienced managers; a short summary, then each role with the scope (sites, staff, budget, patient volume) before the accomplishments
- "Drove operational excellence" reads as filler; "cut third-next-available appointment wait by adding a same-day template, measured over two quarters" reads as credible, as long as every figure is yours and true

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then panel interviews with executives, physicians, and the staff you would lead; senior roles may add a presentation on a plan for the first months or a case exercise with real data
- Questions commonly cover handling a disruptive provider, a budget shortfall, a staffing crisis, and working with physicians who don't report to you

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $123,860 a year ($59.55 an hour); most earn between $73,390 and $224,340 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-9111). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 62,300 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 24.2% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-9111). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; many employers add an annual incentive bonus tied to quality, patient experience, or financial goals, more often at director level and above
- Retirement plans, paid time off, and relocation matter in the total package; public and nonprofit systems may publish salary bands

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, the bonus target, title and reporting line, sign-on and relocation, and schedule flexibility tend to move
- Ask how the bonus has actually paid out in recent years and what happens to it if you join mid-year

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: several sites with no assistant managers named, "working manager" roles that also cover the front desk or billing, frequent turnover in the role, and a nursing home administrator role with a survey history the employer won't discuss

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state licensing board and NAB (nabweb.org) for nursing home administrator licensure; the certifying association for FACHE or CMPE rules; the employer's posting and salary band where pay-transparency laws apply

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 30010, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.

- Health services management is not usually a regulated profession in Canada, and many roles sit inside public health authorities or hospitals with published pay bands and union rules for the staff you would lead; French is often expected in Quebec and bilingual roles are common in New Brunswick, so check Job Bank and the employer's posting.
