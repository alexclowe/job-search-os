# Medical Office / Front Desk Specialist — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `medical-admin` · **Aliases:** medical secretary, patient access rep, medical receptionist
**Also read:** `medical-assistant`, `billing-collections`, `administrative-assistant`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Medical Receptionist, Front Desk Coordinator, Patient Access Representative, Patient Service Representative, Medical Secretary, Medical Office Specialist, Unit Clerk or Unit Secretary, and Scheduling Coordinator
- Adjacent worth searching: Referral Coordinator, Prior Authorization Specialist, Insurance Verification Specialist, Registration Specialist in hospitals, and Dental Front Office Coordinator

## Where the postings live
- Usually physician practices, dental offices, hospital registration and scheduling departments, urgent care, and specialty clinics; health-system career sites and the general job boards carry most of them
- Many hospitals post central scheduling and patient access roles in batches, and some roles are remote or hybrid; temp-to-hire agencies commonly fill front-desk gaps

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Front-desk or customer-service experience, comfort with phones and a busy lobby, and experience with the scheduling or charting system named in the posting (for example Epic, eClinicalWorks, or Dentrix)
- Insurance know-how: verifying coverage, collecting copays, and prior authorizations; medical terminology and HIPAA (the federal health privacy law) training help
- No licence is usually needed; an optional certification such as the Certified Medical Administrative Assistant (CMAA) from the National Healthcareer Association can help when you are new to healthcare

## Story types that land
- Calming an upset patient about a bill, a wait, or a denied claim
- Fixing a scheduling or registration problem that was causing no-shows or claim denials
- Handling a packed phone queue and lobby at the same time without errors
- Protecting a patient's privacy in a tricky moment

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page is usually enough; a skills line with the scheduling, charting, and billing systems, then each job with the practice type and daily volume (calls, check-ins, providers supported)
- "Excellent people skills" reads as filler; "checked in patients for a four-provider practice, verified insurance and collected copays in eClinicalWorks" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the office or practice manager; hospital patient access roles may add a typing or data-entry test
- Common questions tend to ask how you would handle an angry patient, a double-booked slot, or a caller asking for someone else's information

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $22.08 an hour ($45,930 a year); most earn between $17.27 and $29.10 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 43-6013). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 89,200 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 4.8% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 43-6013). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with overtime for extra hours; hospital registration and emergency department roles on evenings, nights, or weekends may carry shift differentials
- Ask about guaranteed hours for part-time and float roles, and whether remote scheduling roles track productivity
- Some employers add pay for bilingual skills or certification

## What's usually negotiable
- The starting rate within the range, schedule, remote or hybrid days where the role allows it, and certification or bilingual pay tend to move
- Paid training in billing or coding is often worth asking about as a path to a higher-paid role

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: reception, billing, clinical assisting, and office management bundled into one role at a front-desk rate, call-volume targets with no staffing detail, and "fast-paced" paired with "other duties as assigned" and nothing else

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's posting for the systems and schedule; the certifying body's own site for any optional certification; HHS.gov for plain-language HIPAA basics
