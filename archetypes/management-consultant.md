# Management Analyst / Consultant — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `management-consultant` · **Aliases:** management consultant, process improvement analyst
**Also read:** `business-analyst`, `project-manager`, `operations-manager`, `industrial-engineer`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Management Consultant, Business Consultant, Management Analyst, and the consulting firm ladder (Analyst or Associate, Consultant, Senior Consultant, Manager, Principal or Director, Partner)
- Adjacent worth searching: Internal Consultant, Strategy or Transformation Manager, Business Analyst, Organizational Development Consultant, Performance Management or Program Analyst in government, Process Improvement Analyst, and Chief of Staff

## Where the postings live
- Usually consulting firm career pages, the general job boards, and professional networks; the larger firms recruit heavily on campus and through structured MBA (Master of Business Administration) and experienced-hire programs
- Many roles are filled through referrals and alumni networks, government management-analyst roles are posted on government job sites, and independent consultants commonly find work through former clients and freelance platforms

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Your school and degree for campus hires, and for experienced hires your industry or functional expertise (for example supply chain, healthcare, finance, technology) plus evidence you've solved business problems
- Analytical tools such as Excel, PowerPoint, Power BI, SQL, or Visio, and clear results from projects; no licence is usually needed, though some consultants hold a voluntary credential such as the CMC (Certified Management Consultant) or Lean or Six Sigma training

## Story types that land
- A problem you broke down, analyzed, and turned into a recommendation someone acted on
- Winning over a skeptical client or stakeholder and getting a change adopted
- Delivering under a tight deadline with incomplete data and saying what you assumed
- A result you can measure: cost saved, time cut, revenue gained, or risk reduced

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page is the usual convention for campus and early-career consulting applications, two for experienced hires; each bullet leads with the result and the scale, and client names are often anonymized ("a regional hospital system")
- "Leveraged synergies to drive transformation" reads as filler; "redesigned the scheduling process for a regional hospital, cutting overtime by a fifth" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a screen, then several rounds of case interviews (you talk through a business problem live, with estimates and a recommendation) mixed with behavioral or "fit" questions
- Some firms use written cases, online tests, or presentations, and experienced-hire interviews tend to focus more on your past projects and industry depth

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $101,860 a year ($48.97 an hour); most earn between $60,640 and $171,640 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-1111). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 94,100 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 10.1% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-1111). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 14% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary plus a performance bonus, which tends to grow with seniority; many firms add a sign-on bonus for campus and MBA hires, and some add profit sharing or retirement contributions
- Independent and freelance consultants set a day rate or project fee and pay their own costs, so compare income after expenses rather than headline rates

## What's usually negotiable
- At large firms, entry-level base pay is often fixed by class year, while the sign-on bonus, start date, office location, and relocation help may move; experienced hires usually have more room on level, base, and bonus
- Ask how performance ratings drive the bonus and promotion, and how much travel the role usually involves

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "consultant" roles that are mostly staff augmentation or sales, heavy travel with no mention of travel policy, pay described mostly as a bonus with no history, and unpaid case work that looks like real client work

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's posting for level and travel; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing; state rules if you'll work independently (business registration, any licence your specialty needs)

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 11201, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Management consulting isn't a licensed profession in Canada, although some consultants hold a voluntary designation; independent consultants usually register a business and may need to charge GST or HST, so check the CRA's rules and your province's business registration.
