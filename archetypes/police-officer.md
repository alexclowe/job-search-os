# Police Officer — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `police-officer` · **Aliases:** sheriff's deputy, patrol officer
**Also read:** `security-guard`, `compliance-officer`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Police Officer, Patrol Officer, Deputy Sheriff, State Trooper, Police Recruit or Cadet (hired before the academy), Lateral Police Officer (already certified), and specialty roles later on such as K-9 handler, school resource officer, detective, or sergeant
- Adjacent worth searching: campus or transit police, corrections officer, park ranger, emergency dispatcher, and investigator roles in public agencies

## Where the postings live
- Usually the city, county or state civil service or HR site, the agency's own recruiting page, and state law-enforcement job boards; many agencies run testing cycles with set application windows
- Recruiters commonly attend hiring events and colleges, and lateral openings are often shared through professional networks before they're widely posted

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Basic eligibility: age, citizenship or work authorization, a valid driver's licence, and no disqualifying convictions (a felony usually disqualifies, and in many agencies so does a domestic-violence conviction); then usually a written civil service exam, a physical ability test, an oral board, a background investigation, and often a polygraph, psychological evaluation and medical exam
- Academy completion and state certification (often called POST, for Peace Officer Standards and Training) is usually done after hire; lateral candidates are screened on whether their existing certification transfers
- Honesty on the background forms tends to matter more than a spotless past; omissions are a common reason candidates are disqualified

## Story types that land
- Staying calm and de-escalating a tense situation
- A time you made an ethical choice when an easier option was available
- Working with the public or a community group to solve a recurring problem
- Writing a clear, accurate report that held up later

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Many agencies use their own application and a long background packet instead of a resume; where a resume is asked for, keep it plain with certifications, languages, military service, and any first-aid or emergency training
- "Tough and decisive" reads as a red flag; concrete public-service, military, security or customer-facing experience reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a multi-step process over several months: written exam, physical test, oral board with scenario questions, background interview, then conditional offer followed by psychological and medical exams
- Scenario questions often come up ("you find a fellow officer doing something wrong..."), as do questions on why you want the work

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $76,210 a year ($36.64 an hour); most earn between $47,510 and $115,120 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 33-3051). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 51,500 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 3.5% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 33-3051). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually a civil service salary schedule with steps for years of service, often set in a union or association contract; recruits are commonly paid during the academy at a lower step
- Overtime is a big part of pay in many agencies; a special federal rule for law enforcement lets employers count overtime over a longer work period and sometimes give compensatory time off instead of cash, so ask how the agency's contract handles it
- Other common pay: shift differentials, specialty or education incentive pay, court time, off-duty detail work, and a public pension

## What's usually negotiable
- The salary schedule rarely moves; lateral candidates can sometimes get credit for prior years and start at a higher step, and some agencies offer hiring bonuses with a repayment clause
- Ask about take-home vehicle policy, shift bidding, and how overtime is assigned

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: frequent mandatory overtime, very large hiring bonuses with long repayment terms, high turnover mentioned in local news, and vague answers on training or equipment

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state's peace officer standards and training commission for certification and lateral rules; the agency or civil service commission for the current salary schedule and contract; the US Department of Labor's fact sheet on law enforcement under the Fair Labor Standards Act for overtime rules
