# Administrative Assistant / Receptionist / Office Clerk — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `administrative-assistant` · **Aliases:** admin assistant, office assistant, front desk, secretary
**Also read:** `executive-assistant`, `office-manager`, `customer-service-rep`, `medical-admin`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Administrative Assistant, Administrative Specialist, Office Assistant, Secretary, Department Secretary, Administrative Clerk, Receptionist, Front Desk Coordinator, and Office Clerk
- Adjacent worth searching: Staff Assistant, Administrative Support Assistant in government, Office Coordinator, Program or Project Assistant, Data Entry Clerk, and Scheduling Coordinator; medical and legal assistant roles when you have that setting's background

## Where the postings live
- Usually the general job boards, company career pages, and local government, school district, and university job sites, which often post clerical roles on their own portals
- Temp and staffing agencies commonly fill short-term and temp-to-hire office roles, and many small offices hire through word of mouth or a local community board

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The software named in the posting, usually Microsoft Word, Excel, Outlook, and PowerPoint (or Google Workspace), plus any scheduling, phone, or database system; typing speed may be tested for clerk and data-entry roles
- Front-desk and phone experience, the schedule you can work, and for some roles a second language; no licence is usually needed, though some offices value a voluntary credential such as the IAAP (International Association of Administrative Professionals) Certified Administrative Professional

## Story types that land
- Keeping a busy front desk or phone line running smoothly while handling walk-ins, calls, and deliveries
- A filing, scheduling, or database cleanup that made information easier to find
- Calming a frustrated visitor or caller and getting them to the right person
- Juggling requests from several people and still hitting each deadline

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page; a skills line near the top with the software you actually use; each role names the office type, how many people you supported, and the main tasks (phones, scheduling, data entry, records)
- "Excellent communication skills" reads as filler; "front desk for a clinic of twelve providers, about a hundred calls a day" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short phone screen, then an in-person or video interview with the office manager or the person you'd support; questions about handling interruptions, confidentiality, and a difficult caller are common
- Some employers add a typing, Excel, or Word test, or a short scheduling or email-writing exercise, and agencies commonly test skills before sending you to clients

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $21.64 an hour ($45,010 a year); most earn between $14.57 and $31.10 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 43-9061, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 518,000 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 3 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 43-9061). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with overtime paid for hours over the weekly limit because most of these roles are non-exempt (eligible for overtime); some employers pay a salary instead, and a few add a small annual bonus
- Evening, weekend, or bilingual differentials show up in some settings, such as hospital front desks or public-facing government offices; union or civil-service roles usually publish pay steps in the contract or pay plan
- Part-time and temp roles vary a lot in guaranteed hours, so ask how many hours a week are guaranteed and whether agency pay includes any benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- The hourly rate within the posted range (often by years of experience or skills such as a second language), start date, schedule, and part-time or hybrid arrangements may move
- In union or civil-service roles the grade and step are usually fixed, but credit for prior experience sometimes moves your starting step; ask about it

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a receptionist title with bookkeeping, HR, and marketing duties added on, "other duties as assigned" at the top of the list, "fast-paced" with no mention of backup coverage, and salaried pay that looks designed to avoid overtime

## Verify before relying on this
- The US Department of Labor and your state labor department for overtime and exempt-status rules; the employer's posting or union contract for the pay scale; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 13110; 14100; 14101, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Administrative roles aren't licensed, but postings in federal and some provincial public service roles often ask for bilingual English and French, and overtime rules are set by provincial employment standards; check your province's employment standards and the posting's language requirement.
