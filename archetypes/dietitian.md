# Dietitian / Nutritionist — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `dietitian` · **Aliases:** RD, RDN
**Also read:** `food-service-manager`, `nurse`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Registered Dietitian (RD) or Registered Dietitian Nutritionist (RDN), Clinical Dietitian, Outpatient Dietitian, Renal Dietitian, Oncology Dietitian, Pediatric Dietitian, and Dietetic Intern for new graduates
- Adjacent worth searching: Nutrition Manager or Food Service Director, Diabetes Educator, WIC (the federal nutrition program for women, infants and children) Nutritionist, Community or Public Health Nutritionist, Wellness Coach, and Nutrition roles in food companies or telehealth

## Where the postings live
- Usually hospitals, long-term care, dialysis centers, outpatient clinics, public health departments, school nutrition programs, and food service management companies; the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics' career board and state affiliate boards carry many roles
- Telehealth and private practice roles often surface through professional networks and practice groups rather than job boards

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The RD or RDN credential from the Commission on Dietetic Registration (CDR); since January 2024 new exam candidates need a graduate degree, an accredited program, and supervised practice before the exam
- A state licence or certification where the state asks for one, since some states license dietitians or protect the title; check your state
- The setting you know (clinical, renal, pediatrics, food service, community) and any specialty certification the role names, such as diabetes education

## Story types that land
- A nutrition intervention that changed a patient's course (weight loss stopped, a tube feeding adjusted, labs improved), stated only as what you observed
- Counseling a patient or family who was hard to reach
- Improving a menu, a food service process, or a screening workflow
- Working with physicians, nurses, and speech therapists on a care plan

## Resume conventions for this profession
- RD or RDN, state licence, and certifications near the top; each role names the setting, population, and caseload
- New graduates list supervised practice rotations with setting and focus
- "Passionate about wellness" reads as filler; "assessed and followed malnourished inpatients on a mixed medical unit, recommended tube feeds and supplements" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a screen with a recruiter or nutrition manager, then an interview with the clinical nutrition manager and sometimes a physician or nurse leader; some include a case study or a sample assessment
- Questions commonly cover prioritizing a caseload, counseling a resistant patient, and working with food service staff

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $76,400 a year ($36.73 an hour); most earn between $49,090 and $103,720 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 29-1031). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 5,700 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 7.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 29-1031). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary in hospitals, public health, and management roles; part-time, per diem, and weekend coverage are often hourly
- Hourly hospital roles may carry weekend or holiday differentials; ask whether overtime applies and whether part-time or per diem roles come with guaranteed hours
- Private practice and telehealth work may be paid per session or as a contractor, so compare pay after unpaid time and costs

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary or hourly rate within the range, sign-on bonus, continuing education money, schedule, and remote days tend to move
- Paid credential and licence fees and support for a specialty certification are often worth asking about

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one dietitian covering several facilities with no travel pay, "nutritionist" roles with no credential asked for but clinical duties expected, and food service management bundled into a clinical role at a clinical rate

## Verify before relying on this
- CDR (cdrnet.org) for credential and graduate-degree rules; your state licensing board or CareerOneStop's License Finder for state licensure or title rules; the employer's posting for setting requirements
