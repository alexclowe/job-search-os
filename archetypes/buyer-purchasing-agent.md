# Buyer / Purchasing Agent — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `buyer-purchasing-agent` · **Aliases:** procurement specialist, sourcing specialist
**Also read:** `logistics-supply-chain`, `wholesale-manufacturing-sales-rep`, `operations-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Buyer, Purchasing Agent, Purchasing Specialist, Procurement Specialist, Sourcing Specialist, Category Buyer, Senior Buyer, Procurement Officer in government, and Purchasing or Procurement Manager at the senior end
- Adjacent worth searching: Strategic Sourcing Analyst, Supplier Quality or Supplier Development roles, Contracts Specialist, Materials Planner or Buyer-Planner, Merchandise Buyer in retail, and Commodity Buyer for agriculture or raw materials

## Where the postings live
- Usually manufacturer, distributor, retailer, hospital, and university career pages, government job portals for public procurement, and the general job boards; supply chain recruiters fill many senior roles
- Many buyer roles are filled internally from purchasing clerks, planners, and coordinators, so an internal move often opens the first buyer title

## What a recruiter screens for first
- What you bought (direct materials, indirect goods and services, retail merchandise, commodities), the spend or number of suppliers you managed, and the enterprise resource planning or procurement system you used
- Negotiation results, supplier selection and bid processes, and contract basics; public-sector roles commonly screen for knowledge of procurement rules and competitive bidding
- Credentials that commonly help: the CPSM (Certified Professional in Supply Management) from ISM (Institute for Supply Management), ASCM (Association for Supply Chain Management) certifications for planning-heavy roles, and public-procurement certifications for government buyers

## Story types that land
- A negotiation: what you asked for, what you gave, and the savings or terms you secured without hurting quality
- Finding a new supplier fast when one failed, and keeping production or shelves supplied
- A cost-reduction or consolidation project with a result you can explain simply
- Handling a supplier quality problem or a contract dispute fairly and on the record

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Each role names the categories bought, the spend or supplier count as the employer allows you to share it, the system used, and one or two results; certifications follow
- "Savvy negotiator who crushes suppliers" reads as filler; "bought machined parts and packaging for two plants, ran annual bids, moved key parts to dual sourcing after a supplier shortage" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the purchasing manager and people from operations or finance; many employers ask for a negotiation role-play or a sourcing case
- Public-sector interviews commonly follow a structured panel format with scored questions about procurement rules and ethics

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $77,710 a year ($37.36 an hour); most earn between $48,380 and $128,870 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-1020). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 47,200 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 6.0% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-1020). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some private employers add a bonus tied to savings or company results, while public-sector buyers are commonly paid on a published pay grade
- Some entry buyer and purchasing roles are paid hourly and eligible for overtime, so ask how the role is classified

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: base salary within the band, title or level, bonus target, paid certification, remote or hybrid days, and start date; public-sector pay usually moves by grade and step placement rather than individual negotiation
- Ask how savings targets are measured, since a target that counts only price can work against quality and delivery

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a buyer role that is mostly order entry and expediting, savings targets with no clear baseline, pressure to accept supplier gifts or hospitality, and one person covering far too many categories

## Verify before relying on this
- ISM (ismworld.org) and ASCM (ascm.org) for certification eligibility; your government employer's procurement rules and pay grades; the employer's own posting for scope and systems
