# Financial Analyst — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `financial-analyst` · **Aliases:** FP&A analyst, investment analyst, budget analyst
**Also read:** `accountant`, `financial-manager`, `data-analyst`, `business-analyst`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Financial Analyst, Senior Financial Analyst, FP&A (financial planning and analysis) Analyst, Budget Analyst, Investment Analyst, Equity Research Analyst, Credit Analyst, and Finance Manager as the next step
- Adjacent worth searching: Business Finance Partner, Cost Analyst, Pricing Analyst, Treasury Analyst, Real Estate Analyst, Portfolio Analyst, and Revenue or Sales Operations Analyst

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and finance-specific boards; banks and investment firms often hire new graduates through structured analyst programs with fixed application windows
- Finance recruiters and staffing firms commonly fill both permanent and contract analyst roles, and many roles are filled through alumni and professional networks

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Strong Excel (modeling, lookups, pivot tables) and the systems in the posting, such as an enterprise resource planning system like SAP or Oracle, a planning tool, SQL, Power BI, or Tableau
- Budgeting, forecasting, and variance analysis experience for corporate roles; valuation and modeling for investment roles
- A degree in finance, accounting, or economics is commonly listed; the chartered financial analyst (CFA) charter from the CFA Institute is voluntary but often valued in investment roles, and research analysts at broker-dealers usually need FINRA registration exams, so check the posting

## Story types that land
- A forecast or budget you built and how close it came, or a variance you explained to leaders
- An analysis that changed a decision: a price, a cost cut, an investment, or a project you recommended stopping
- Building a model or report that saved the team time each month
- Explaining numbers to people outside finance so they acted on them

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page early career; education and any charter progress near the top for new graduates; for each role, the scope (business unit, budget size from your own record) and the decisions your work supported
- "Leveraged data to drive insights" reads as filler; "rebuilt the monthly forecast model and cut the close-to-report time" reads as credible once your own figures are in

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the finance manager and business partners; technical questions on the three financial statements, forecasting, and valuation are common
- Many employers add an Excel test or a case study (build a quick model or explain a variance), and investment roles may add a stock pitch or modeling test

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $102,740 a year ($49.40 an hour); most earn between $63,720 and $180,860 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-2051, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 27,600 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-2051). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary with a yearly bonus; the bonus tends to be a larger share of pay in banking and investment roles than in corporate finance
- Some employers pay for charter exams or graduate study and give study time, and contract analysts tend to be paid hourly through an agency

## What's usually negotiable
- The base salary within the band, bonus target, title or level, sign-on bonus, exam and tuition support, and remote days tend to move
- Ask how the bonus is decided (company, team, and personal results) and when it's paid, because leaving before the payout date often means losing it

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a "financial analyst" title that is mostly data entry or bookkeeping, a single analyst covering every budget for a large business, and pay described as mostly bonus with no base range shown

## Verify before relying on this
- The CFA Institute for charter requirements; FINRA for registration exams if the role involves securities research; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

- Corporate financial analyst titles aren't regulated, but roles at investment dealers may need registration through CIRO and provincial securities regulators such as the OSC, and many finance roles ask for or value the CPA designation; check the posting and CIRO's proficiency rules.
