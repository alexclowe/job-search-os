# Data Analyst / BI Analyst — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `data-analyst` · **Aliases:** business intelligence analyst, reporting analyst, operations research analyst, statistician
**Also read:** `data-scientist`, `business-analyst`, `financial-analyst`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Data Analyst, Business Intelligence (BI) Analyst, Reporting Analyst, Analytics Analyst, Insights Analyst, and the senior and lead versions of each
- Adjacent worth searching: Operations Analyst, Marketing or Product Analyst, Decision Analyst, Statistical Analyst, Analytics Engineer (more data-pipeline work), and Business Analyst when the role leans toward requirements rather than numbers

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages and the general job boards; analyst roles are often posted under a department (finance, marketing, operations) rather than a data team, so search by the tools as well as the title
- Many contract and temp-to-hire analyst roles come through staffing agencies, and internal moves from an operations or finance seat into analytics are common

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The tools named in the posting, matched word for word: SQL (the language for pulling data from databases), Excel, and a dashboard tool such as Power BI or Tableau; Python or R is often a plus rather than the first filter
- The kind of data you have worked with (sales, marketing, finance, operations, health) and whether you built reports yourself or only read them
- A portfolio or sample dashboard helps when your job title didn't say "analyst"; certificates from software vendors are often valued but rarely replace shown work

## Story types that land
- A question someone asked, the data you pulled, what you found, and the decision that changed because of it
- A report or dashboard you built that replaced a manual spreadsheet and saved people time each week
- A time the numbers looked wrong and you traced the cause (bad data, a changed definition, a broken join) before anyone acted on them
- Explaining a finding to someone who doesn't read charts, and what they did next

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page early career; a tools line near the top (SQL, Excel, Power BI or Tableau, Python); link a portfolio or public dashboard when the data isn't confidential
- "Leveraged data to drive insights" reads as filler; "built a weekly churn report in Power BI that the sales team now uses to pick which accounts to call" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, a hiring-manager conversation, then a skills check: a live SQL exercise, an Excel or spreadsheet task, or a take-home dataset with a short write-up
- Many teams ask you to present a finding back to a mixed audience, so expect questions about how you chose a chart and what you left out

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, nearest occupation only:** there is no federal occupation code for this role, so this is Operations Research Analysts, a rough guide at best: median $88,940 a year ($42.76 an hour); most earn between $57,060 and $159,910 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 15-2031, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings, nearest occupations:** about 9,400 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 15-2031). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some employers add a yearly bonus, and contract or temp-to-hire analyst roles often pay hourly through an agency with fewer benefits
- The figures above are for the nearest federal occupation, not for data analysts as such; compare them with the posted ranges for the exact title and industry before using them

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, title (analyst vs senior analyst), remote or hybrid days, start date, and a training or certification budget tend to move
- Ask how the level was decided; showing you already work at the next level's scope often moves the offer more than asking for a higher number inside the level

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a "data analyst" posting that is mostly data entry, one analyst expected to own every report for the whole company, a long list of engineering tools for an entry title, and take-home tasks that look like real client work

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's own posting for the tools and level; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the nearest occupations are changing

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** possibly 21210. This is an uncertain title match by the maintainers, not an official concordance, so check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site first.
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Canadian postings often split this work across data analyst, business intelligence and statistician titles, and Job Bank may place them in different unit groups; check the unit group definition and search Job Bank by the exact title. Federal and some provincial public-sector postings ask for English and French at set levels, so check the language profile on the posting.
- "Engineer" is a protected title in every province and territory (Alberta exempts only the title "software engineer"; per Engineers Canada and APEGA), so using a data or analytics engineer title without a provincial engineering licence can breach the law, and Canadian postings often use another title for this role (per Engineers Canada); check your provincial engineering regulator before using it.
