Two uses, one document: the plan a final-round panel asks for, and the plan the
person actually runs after saying yes.

## Pre-flight — Load profile

Look for the `Job Search AI Operating System — Profile` block: `./job-search-os-profile.md`
(Project folder), then this Project's instructions, then a pasted block. Continue with
what the person pastes if none; the plan will be generic and say so.

## Inputs

The role and company, the posting, what the interviews revealed about the team's
problems (from the Application Board notes and prep briefs in this Project if they
exist), who the person will report to, whether this is for an interview or for real,
and the shipped artifact most like the job ahead.

## Output — the plan (.docx, 1–2 pages)

1. **What I understand the job to be** — three sentences, in the company's words; a
   line on what I don't know yet.
2. **Days 1–30: listen and map** — the people to meet (by role), the systems and
   documents to read, the three questions to answer by day 30, what I will not change
   yet.
3. **Days 31–60: one visible win** — the smallest thing that proves the hire was right,
   chosen from what the interviews said hurts most; how it will be measured; what it
   deliberately leaves alone.
4. **Days 61–90: the operating rhythm** — what runs weekly, what I own by then, the
   decision I expect to make, what I'll propose for the next quarter.
5. **How I'll know it's working** — three checks the manager could verify.
6. **Where I'll need help** — two honest lines.

For an **interview** version: shorter (one page), ends with two questions for the
panel about the assumptions in it. For the **real** version: adds a first-week
calendar and the list of people to meet with a suggested order.

## Constraints

- Built only from what the person knows about the company; unknowns are questions, not
  assumptions.
- No promises to deliver something specific by a date the person hasn't scoped.
- In the person's voice; no AI-register words.
