A recruiter screen has one purpose: to decide whether to spend the hiring manager's
time on you, and to find out what you cost. Prep for exactly that.

## Pre-flight — Load profile

Look for the `Job Search AI Operating System — Profile` block: `./job-search-os-profile.md`
(Project folder), then this Project's instructions, then a pasted block. If none, say:
"Run `jobsearch-setup-wizard` first — the compensation script needs your minimum pay and the
answers need your wins." Continue only with pasted material.

Older profiles use other labels for the same fields: `Minimum salary:` or `Salary floor:`
mean minimum pay (annual salary unless the value says otherwise), **Shipped artifacts**
means wins, `Positioning challenges:` means things a hiring manager might question,
and `Target level:` means career stage. Read them the same way.

## Inputs

Company, role, the posting if available, the recruiter's name and whether they're
internal or agency, the call length, and anything the recruiter already said about
range or process.

## Company brief first

Before the questions, look in this Project for **"Company brief — [Company].md"**
(under 30 days old → use it). Missing or older → build it with the company-brief
method (`jobsearch-company-brief`): the company's own pages and recent reputable news,
every fact with a source and a date; without web search, say so and ask for the
posting or about page — never fill the gap from memory. The brief feeds "why this
role / why us" and the questions to ask them.

## Output — prep sheet (one page)

1. **What this call decides** — two lines.
2. **The six questions and your answers** (each answer 40–90 words, spoken register,
   built from a named win or the profile):
   - Walk me through your background (the 60-second version: target role first, two
     wins, why now).
   - Why this role / why us (one sourced fact from the company brief, tied to a win).
   - Why are you looking / why did you leave (the profile's approved line, nothing
     more).
   - What are you looking for in your next role.
   - Timeline and other processes (honest, no invented competing offers).
   - Location, remote, start date.
3. **The compensation conversation** — the script:
   - First: ask for their range, in the unit the role is paid in ("What's the hourly
     range for this role, and how are differentials paid?" · "What's the salary range
     you've budgeted?" · "What's the base or draw, the commission split, and what does
     a typical first-year person earn?" · "Where on the schedule would I likely be
     placed, and how are prior years credited?").
   - If they insist: if the profile says share your minimum pay, state it as the lowest
     you'd take, in the same unit ("the lowest I'd take is $44 an hour"), and say the
     rest depends on the package; otherwise give the market range the person supplied,
     with your minimum pay as the bottom, never a single number. Never give pay
     history; if asked, "I'd rather talk about what this role pays than what the last
     one did."
   - If their range is below your minimum pay: say so kindly on the call and ask whether
     there's flexibility (the rate, the step placement, a ramp guarantee, a sign-on);
     if not, thank them and keep the door open.
4. **Questions to ask them** — five, starting from the brief's open questions: the pay (in its unit, plus schedule or hours for
   hourly roles, split and ramp for commission roles), why the role is open, the
   process and timeline, who the hiring manager is, what a strong first year looks
   like.
5. **Red flags to listen for** — a range that moves, "we're still defining the role",
   a process with no dates, agency pressure to commit to exclusivity.
6. **Day-of card** — three lines: lead with, avoid, close with.

## Constraints

- No invented stories; every answer names its source.
- Your minimum pay holds in every branch of the comp script.
- AI-register tells removed; spoken, not written, register.
