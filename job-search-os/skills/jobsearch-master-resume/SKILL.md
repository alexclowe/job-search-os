No resume, an old one, or one that's a mess? This builds the master resume: the one
honest, complete version that every tailored application starts from. It isn't aimed
at one posting — Tailor & apply does that.

## Pre-flight

- **Profile:** `./job-search-os-profile.md`, then this Project's instructions, then a
  pasted block. Use name, current role, target roles, career stage, location, wins,
  things a hiring manager might question, and voice samples. (Older profiles:
  **Shipped artifacts** means wins, `Positioning challenges:` means things a hiring
  manager might question, `Target level:` means career stage.) No profile → the
  interview below collects what's needed; offer "set me up" afterwards.
- **Anything to start from** — an old resume, a LinkedIn profile, a performance review,
  a job description from a past role. Read it first and ask only about gaps.
- **Profession archetype** — `./archetypes/<profession>.md` in this Project, then the
  `archetypes/` folder that ships with the Tailor & apply job; use its resume
  conventions and the titles hiring teams use. Find the file through `archetypes/index.md` first (it ships in the same folder): match the profession against its Name and Also called columns and open only that one file. If more than one row fits ("nurse" could be a registered nurse, an LPN, a nurse practitioner, or a nursing assistant), ask one short question with those rows as options before opening anything.

## The interview (short — three cards at most, then draft)

**Card 1 — Where and what.** Country for this search (US · Canada · both) · the kind of
jobs you're after (one to three titles) · any of these apply: federal or USAJOBS
application · academic or research role (needs a CV, not a resume) · licensed
profession (nursing, teaching, other).

**Card 2 — Your jobs, newest first.** For each: title, employer, city, start and end
month and year (and hours per week if it's a federal application), and in your own
words **what you did and what came of it** — two to four things. Tell them: "Rough
notes are fine. If you don't know a number, say so and I'll leave a spot for it."
Unpaid work, volunteering, caregiving, a career break, or freelance work counts — a
gap gets one plain, factual line, not an apology.

**Card 3 — The rest.** Education (school, credential, year; GPA only if a federal
application asks for it) · licenses and certifications (type, issuing body or state or
province, status and expiry; a license number only if the person wants it listed) ·
skills that are real and job-relevant (tools, systems, languages, clinical or
technical skills) · contact line: name, phone, email, city and region, LinkedIn URL
if they want it.

Anything still unknown goes in the draft as `[add …]`, not another round of questions.

## Write it (current conventions)

- **Format:** reverse-chronological by default. For career changers, offer a
  combination format (a short skills-and-results section on top, then the history).
- **Length:** early career → one page. Experienced in the US → up to two pages. Canada
  → two pages at most. Federal / USAJOBS → **two pages, hard limit** (longer resumes
  are rejected), with month/year dates and hours per week for each job. Academic or
  research roles → a CV instead (no page limit); say so and offer to build that.
- **Contact line:** name, phone, email, city and region, optional LinkedIn URL. **Never**
  a photo, age or date of birth, marital status, religion, a Social Security number, or
  a Social Insurance Number. No full street address needed.
- **Summary, not an objective** — two or three lines naming the kind of role and the two
  strongest results. An objective only for a clear career change, and then one line.
- **Order for licensed professions:** nurses — licensure and certifications near the
  top, then clinical experience (with rotation hours for new grads); teachers —
  certification near the top, education close behind. Otherwise: summary, experience,
  education, licenses and certifications, skills.
- **Bullets:** action, scope, result — in the person's words, with their numbers. No
  number given → the bullet says what changed, or carries `[add the number]`. Never a
  made-up percentage.
- **Readable by applicant-tracking systems:** one column, standard section titles
  (Summary, Experience, Education, Licenses and Certifications, Skills), job title,
  employer, and dates on every role, no tables, text boxes, graphics, or contact
  details in the page header or footer.
- **No self-praise words:** no "results-driven", "passionate", "dynamic", "seasoned",
  "spearheaded", "leverage".

## Save it

- **"Master resume.docx"** and **"Master resume.md"** in this Project (and in the Drive
  root folder if Drive is connected). This is the base every Tailor & apply run reads;
  never overwrite it from a tailored version.
- Offer to update the profile's **Wins** with any result from the interview that isn't
  there yet: "Saved to your profile so I won't ask again — say 'undo' to remove it."
- A **Before you use it** list: every `[add …]` placeholder, any date that doesn't line
  up, and anything a hiring manager might question that the resume should handle.

The oversell, undersell, and source checks apply to the draft like any other.
