A counter-offer is flattering and usually late. This skill separates the money from
the reasons and helps the person decide with the reasons.

## Pre-flight — Load profile

Look for the `Job Search AI Operating System — Profile` block: `./job-search-os-profile.md`
(Project folder), then this Project's instructions, then a pasted block; and the
**Offer Tracker** if it exists. If none, continue with what the person pastes and say
the floor check is off.

## Inputs

Which kind (current employer countering a resignation, or the new company revising),
the terms on each side, the reasons the person started looking (from the profile's
approved line and anything they add now), the timeline, and what has already been
said to each side.

## Output

1. **The two offers, side by side** — base, bonus, equity, title, remote, manager,
   start or continuation, each against the floor and the must-haves. Numbers only
   from what the person pasted.
2. **The reasons test** — the three reasons they started looking, and whether the
   counter fixes any of them beyond the money (a raise doesn't change a manager, a
   roadmap, or a ceiling). Stated plainly; the decision is theirs.
3. **Questions to ask the current employer** — why now and not at the last review;
   what changes besides pay; how this affects the next review and promotion timing;
   whether it's in writing.
4. **Questions to ask the new company** — whether the revised terms are final, what
   moved and why, the start date and the written offer.
5. **Scripts** — for each outcome, 80–150 words, spoken and email:
   - staying: accept in writing, ask for the terms and the review date in the letter,
     tell the new company promptly and kindly;
   - going: thank the current employer, decline the counter without relitigating,
     confirm the notice period; confirm with the new company in writing.
6. **What to get in writing** either way.

## Constraints

- The floor and must-haves are checked first; a counter below the floor is named as
  such.
- No invented figures or tendencies about either company.
- Never resign, accept, or decline for the person — words only.
- Contracts, equity clawbacks, non-competes, and notice obligations: "worth a
  professional's eyes".
