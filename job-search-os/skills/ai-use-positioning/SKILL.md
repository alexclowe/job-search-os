Every hiring manager now asks it, and most answers are either a boast or a shrug. This
skill builds the honest middle from what the person really does.

## Pre-flight — Load profile

Look for the `Job Search AI Operating System — Profile` block: `./job-search-os-profile.md`
(Project folder), then this Project's instructions, then a pasted block. Continue with
what the person tells you now if none.

## Inputs (ask in one card, clickable where possible)

- What they actually use AI for at work today (multi-select: drafting · code ·
  analysis · research · summarizing · automating a recurring task · not much yet).
- One real example with what changed (time saved, a result, a mistake caught).
- What they don't trust it with, and why.
- The target role and what the posting says about AI, if anything.

## Output

1. **The spoken answer** (60–90 seconds, the person's voice): what they use it for
   (specific tasks, not "everything"), the example with its result, the boundary
   (what they check, what they never delegate), and one line on how they'd bring it
   to this team.
2. **The follow-up answers** (two lines each): "what about accuracy or hallucination",
   "what would you not use it for", "how would you help the team adopt it", "what
   happens to your role if the tool does more".
3. **The resume line** — one bullet, action · scope · result, that says what they
   built or changed with AI, with a real figure or none.
4. **The LinkedIn line** — one sentence for the About section.
5. **What to avoid** — "AI-first", "prompt engineer" as a title unless it is one,
   listing tools as skills, claiming outcomes the person didn't measure.

## Constraints

- Nothing invented; if the person hasn't used AI much, the honest answer says so and
  describes what they'd try first.
- No AI-register words; the irony would not be lost on the interviewer.
- Nothing confidential about the current employer's tooling beyond what the profile
  allows.
