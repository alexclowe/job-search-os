# Compliance Officer / Specialist — job-search archetype

**Slug:** `compliance-officer` · **Aliases:** compliance analyst, regulatory specialist
**Also read:** `paralegal`, `lawyer`, `safety-specialist`, `healthcare-administrator`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Compliance Specialist, Compliance Analyst, Compliance Officer, Regulatory Compliance Specialist, Senior Compliance Analyst, and Compliance Manager, usually with the field named: banking, anti-money laundering (AML), healthcare, privacy, trade, or corporate ethics
- Adjacent worth searching: Know Your Customer (KYC) Analyst, Anti-Money Laundering Investigator, Regulatory Affairs Specialist, Risk Analyst, Internal Auditor, Privacy Analyst, and Ethics and Compliance Program Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually bank, insurer, health system, and large-company career pages and the general job boards; many banking compliance roles are also filled through financial-services recruiters and staffing firms
- Industry associations for compliance, anti-money laundering, and healthcare compliance commonly run job boards, and people often move into compliance from operations, audit, or legal roles inside the same employer

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Knowledge of the specific rules the employer lives under (for example the Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money laundering rules for banks, HIPAA for healthcare, or trade and export rules), named the way the posting names them
- Evidence you've done the core work: monitoring and testing, investigations, policy writing, training staff, and preparing for exams or audits by a regulator
- Certifications such as those in anti-money laundering, bank compliance, or healthcare compliance are usually optional but often preferred; a law degree is sometimes preferred for senior roles

## Story types that land
- A gap you found through testing or monitoring, how you raised it, and the fix that followed
- An investigation or alert review you took from first flag to a documented conclusion
- A policy or training you rolled out that people actually followed
- Helping the organization through a regulator's exam or an outside audit

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A short summary naming your field and the rules you know, then certifications; for each role, the programs you supported (monitoring, testing, investigations, training), the size of the review work from your own record, and the outcomes
- "Ensured full compliance with all regulations" reads as inflated; "reviewed flagged transactions, wrote case narratives, and escalated suspicious activity for filing" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the compliance manager and sometimes legal or audit partners; scenario questions such as "a senior leader asks you to skip a control" are common
- Some employers add a written exercise, such as reviewing a sample case or summarizing a new rule for the business

## How pay is usually structured

- **US pay, for comparison:** median $80,730 a year ($38.81 an hour); most earn between $48,220 and $133,720 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 13-1041). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 32,700 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 3.8% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 13-1041). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; banks and larger companies may add a yearly bonus, and contract roles (often during a regulator-ordered cleanup) tend to pay hourly through an agency without benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- The salary within the band, the title or level, certification costs and study time, remote days, and a sign-on bonus tend to move
- Ask whether the role reports to legal, risk, or the business, because that often shapes independence and the promotion path

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a single compliance person expected to cover every rule for a large business, "fast-paced" with a regulator order in the background, and a compliance title whose duties are mainly data entry

## Verify before relying on this
- The regulator's own site for the rules the role names (for example the CFPB, FinCEN, or the Department of Health and Human Services); the certifying body's site for any certification's requirements

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** no clear match. No single unit group fits; search Job Bank by title.
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Compliance titles aren't regulated in Canada, but the rules differ: banks and other federally regulated lenders answer to federal regulators and anti-money laundering law, while investment dealers answer to CIRO and provincial securities regulators, so check which rules the role covers and whether it asks for bilingual English and French skills.
