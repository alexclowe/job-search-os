# Retail Store Manager / Supervisor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `retail-store-manager` · **Aliases:** assistant store manager, department supervisor, key holder
**Also read:** `retail-sales-associate`, `food-service-manager`, `operations-manager`, `inventory-shipping-clerk`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Store Manager, Assistant Store Manager, Co-Manager, Department Manager or Supervisor, Shift Manager or Shift Lead, Key Holder, and managers of a named department (grocery, bakery, deli, apparel, electronics)
- Adjacent worth searching: District or Area Manager, Merchandising Manager, Visual Merchandiser, Loss Prevention Manager, Inventory or Receiving Manager, Operations Manager in a distribution center, and Customer Experience Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually retailer career pages, the general job boards, and retail-focused staffing firms; chains often post management roles on their own sites and fill many of them from their own associates and key holders
- Internal promotion is common, so telling your current store leader you want the next step often moves faster than applying outside; district managers commonly recruit from nearby stores

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The store size and type you've run (sales volume band, team size, departments), your title and scope, and results from your own records on sales, shrink (loss), labour budget, and customer scores
- Scheduling, inventory, and point-of-sale systems you've used; availability for nights, weekends, and holidays; and willingness to relocate or cover other stores for multi-unit tracks

## Story types that land
- Lifting sales or conversion in a department and what you changed (staffing, layout, training)
- Cutting shrink or fixing an inventory problem
- Hiring and developing a team, including promoting someone from within
- Handling a difficult customer, a safety incident, or a holiday rush without losing the store

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page is common for supervisors and two for multi-unit managers; for each role name the store type, team size, and results from your own records, and mark any figure you can't confirm and fill it in before sending
- "Responsible for store operations" reads as filler; "ran a mid-size apparel store with a team of hourly associates, rebuilt the schedule to cover evening peaks, and trained two new key holders" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen with a recruiter, then an interview with the store or district manager, commonly with a store walk where you point out what you'd fix
- Expect behavioural questions on coaching a low performer, handling a theft or safety situation, meeting a labour budget, and a time you missed a sales goal

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $23.33 an hour ($48,520 a year); most earn between $15.92 and $37.06 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 41-1011). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 113,500 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to shrink 3.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 41-1011). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 21% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Supervisors, key holders, and some assistant managers are commonly paid hourly; store managers and many assistant managers are commonly on an annual salary; a bonus tied to sales, profit, shrink, or audit results is common
- Hourly supervisors usually earn overtime under federal and state law, while salaried managers may be classified exempt; ask how the role is classified, since a salaried role with long weeks can pay less per hour than an hourly one
- Shift differentials are uncommon in retail, though some stores pay more for overnight or inventory shifts; ask whether hourly supervisor roles come with guaranteed hours, since hours often follow sales
- Employee discounts and paid time off are common extras; compare them after base pay and bonus

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: starting pay within the range, the bonus target or tier (tied to the store's volume), the store you're assigned, schedule, relocation help, and the timeline to a store of your own
- Ask how the bonus has actually paid out at stores of similar size; bonus formulas tend to depend on things outside your control

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: salaried "manager" roles that mostly cover cashier or stocking shifts, "open availability" with no schedule, high manager turnover at the same store, a bonus with no written plan, and multi-store coverage with no travel pay

## Verify before relying on this
- The Department of Labor and your state labour department for overtime and exempt-status rules; the retailer's written bonus plan and handbook before accepting
