# Real Estate Agent — job-search archetype

**Slug:** `real-estate-agent` · **Aliases:** realtor, real estate salesperson, broker associate (real estate), associate broker (real estate), buyer's agent, listing agent
**Also read:** `property-manager`, `loan-officer`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Real Estate Agent / Salesperson (licensed, affiliating with a brokerage rather than being "hired"), Associate Broker, Team Buyer's Agent, Listing Specialist, Inside Sales Agent (ISA), Transaction Coordinator, Showing Assistant
- Adjacent worth searching (salaried): New-Home Sales Consultant (builders), Leasing Consultant, Property Manager, Relocation Coordinator, Brokerage Operations or Marketing roles

## Where the postings live
- Brokerage and team career pages, the general boards for team and salaried roles, builder career sites, and direct recruiting by brokers; most agent "hires" are affiliation decisions made on split, support, and culture rather than a posting
- Team roles (buyer's agent, ISA) are the most posting-driven and the most structured

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Active licence in the state and MLS/association membership, transaction history (sides and volume, which will be checked), lead sources, CRM and marketing tools used, and whether you have worked on a team or solo
- For salaried adjacent roles, systems and customer-service experience matter more than licence

## Story types that land
- A transaction saved: inspection, appraisal, or financing problem and what you did
- How you generate business (a sphere, a niche, a channel) in specific, honest terms
- A listing you priced or marketed well and what the evidence was
- A fair-housing or disclosure moment handled correctly

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Licence and designations; production by year (sides, volume) only with records to back it; lead sources; tools; team versus solo made explicit
- Inflated production is the fastest way to lose an interview in this industry; say "team volume" when it was team volume

## How interviews usually run
- Joining a brokerage is usually a conversation with the managing broker or team lead about your license, sphere, marketing plan, and production, more than a formal interview
- Salaried roles (transaction coordinator, showing agent, inside sales) usually run more like a standard interview

- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 40,400 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 41-9022). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 54% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: the split and any cap, who pays for leads and marketing, fees, training and mentoring, and whether a team provides a guaranteed lead flow
- Ask what a typical first-year agent on that team actually closes, not the top producer's numbers
- Worth asking in writing: who keeps the leads and past clients (the book) if you leave the team or brokerage

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "Unlimited earning potential" with desk fees and no leads, mandatory lead-purchase programs, splits that change with undefined "caps", team roles that require you to bring your own database

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state real estate commission for licensing; the brokerage's independent-contractor agreement and comp schedule; the local association for MLS rules
