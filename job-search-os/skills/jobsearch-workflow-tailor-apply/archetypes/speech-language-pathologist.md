# Speech-Language Pathologist — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `speech-language-pathologist` · **Aliases:** SLP, CCC-SLP
**Also read:** `occupational-therapist`, `therapy-assistant`, `teacher`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP), Speech Therapist, Clinical Fellow (CF) or CF-SLP for new graduates, Pediatric SLP, Medical or Acute Care SLP, School SLP, and Bilingual SLP
- Adjacent worth searching: Early Intervention Provider, Feeding and Swallowing Specialist, Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) Specialist, Telepractice SLP, Clinical Supervisor, and Rehab Director

## Where the postings live
- Usually school districts, hospitals and rehab centers, skilled nursing and home health, outpatient pediatric clinics, and early intervention programs; ASHA's own job board and state association boards carry many roles
- School roles are commonly posted on district and state education job boards on the school calendar; staffing and contract agencies often fill school and teletherapy roles

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A master's degree from an accredited program, a state SLP licence (or a clinical fellowship licence while you finish your supervised year), and often the Certificate of Clinical Competence (CCC-SLP) from the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (ASHA)
- The CCC path usually runs through the graduate program, the Praxis exam in speech-language pathology, and a supervised clinical fellowship; school roles may also ask for a state education credential
- The populations and settings you know (pediatric language, swallowing and dysphagia, voice, AAC) and your caseload experience

## Story types that land
- A student or patient's progress toward a goal you set, described in plain words
- Managing a large caseload or a demanding productivity target while keeping documentation current
- Working with teachers, parents, nurses, or physicians to carry therapy into the classroom or home
- A swallow study or evaluation that changed a care plan

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Credentials near the top (CCC-SLP, state licence, school credential); each job names the setting, age range, and caseload or productivity expectations
- New graduates list clinical placements with setting and population; bilingual skills go near the top when you can assess or treat in that language
- "Passionate about communication" reads as filler; "school caseload across two elementary schools, wrote IEP goals, ran push-in language groups" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a screen with a recruiter or rehab director, then an interview with the clinical lead or special education director; some add case questions or a sample evaluation report
- Questions commonly cover writing measurable goals, handling a large caseload, working with a resistant family, and documentation for insurance or school plans

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $97,870 a year ($47.05 an hour); most earn between $62,900 and $134,160 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 29-1127). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 12,500 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 16.6% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a master's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 29-1127). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary in hospitals, clinics, and schools; school districts often pay on a published salary schedule with steps for years and lanes for education, and some add a stipend for the CCC
- Home health, skilled nursing, and contract roles commonly pay per visit or hourly, so compare how many visits fit a day and whether travel and paperwork are paid

## What's usually negotiable
- In clinical settings, base salary, sign-on bonus, productivity targets, continuing education money, and schedule tend to move; in schools, placement on the schedule (years and lanes credited) usually moves more than the rate
- Ask about clinical fellowship supervision, paid licence and ASHA dues, and caseload caps

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: no mention of clinical fellowship supervision for a CF role, per-visit pay with unpaid documentation time, very high productivity targets, and school roles spread across many buildings with no travel pay

## Verify before relying on this
- ASHA (asha.org) for CCC-SLP and clinical fellowship rules; your state licensing board and state education department for licence and school credentials; the school district's published salary schedule
