# Billing & Collections Specialist — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `billing-collections` · **Aliases:** billing specialist, collections agent, AR specialist
**Also read:** `bookkeeper`, `medical-admin`, `customer-service-rep`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Billing Specialist, Billing Coordinator, Billing Clerk, Accounts Receivable (AR) Specialist, Collections Specialist, Collector, and Collections Representative, plus senior and team-lead versions
- Adjacent worth searching: Medical Biller or Patient Account Representative, Revenue Cycle Specialist, Credit and Collections Analyst, Cash Application Specialist, and Accounting Clerk

## Where the postings live
- Usually the general job boards, employer career pages (hospitals, clinics, utilities, software and service companies), and staffing agencies that place accounting and back-office temps
- Third-party collection agencies often hire continuously and in groups; healthcare billing roles are commonly posted by health systems and billing companies, sometimes as remote roles

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The billing or accounting system named in the posting (for example QuickBooks, an enterprise resource planning system, or a medical system such as Epic or MEDITECH), Excel skills, and accuracy with invoices and payment posting
- For healthcare billing: familiarity with insurance claims, denials, and patient privacy rules; a billing or coding certificate is sometimes asked for but is usually optional for entry roles
- For collections: phone comfort, a calm manner, and knowing the consumer debt rules; third-party collectors fall under the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) and the CFPB's Regulation F, while a creditor's own staff collecting in the creditor's own name generally aren't covered by the FDCPA (under Regulation F a creditor counts as a debt collector if it collects under another name), and some states license collection agencies, so check your state's rules
- Knockout questions often cover typing or data-entry speed, shift availability, and a background or credit check for roles handling payments

## Story types that land
- Finding and fixing a billing error before the invoice went out, or tracing why a batch of payments didn't post
- Bringing an old account up to date through a payment plan the customer could keep
- Working a list of denied or overdue claims down in a way the team noticed
- Handling an angry caller while staying within the rules on what you can say

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A one-page resume with a systems line near the top; for each role, the kind of accounts (consumer, business, medical), the systems used, and results from your own record, such as accounts worked or a drop in overdue balances
- "Detail-oriented team player" reads as filler; "posted daily payments and worked the overdue list for business accounts in QuickBooks" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short phone screen, then an interview with the billing or collections supervisor; collections roles may include a mock call with an upset customer
- Some employers add a data-entry, Excel, or ten-key test, and many ask early about schedule, remote setup, and comfort with call quotas

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $23.32 an hour ($48,500 a year); most earn between $17.93 and $32.55 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 43-3021, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 48,200 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 43-3021). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, and most of these roles are non-exempt, so overtime pay applies when you work past the weekly limit; month-end and year-end often bring extra hours
- Collections roles commonly add an incentive or bonus tied to money collected, and call centres that run evening or weekend shifts may pay a shift differential
- Temp and agency roles tend to pay hourly with fewer benefits and no guaranteed hours; ask whether a full-time role guarantees a set weekly schedule

## What's usually negotiable
- The hourly rate within the range (often by experience with the same system or the same type of claims), schedule and remote days, and the collection incentive plan's targets tend to move
- Ask how the incentive is calculated, how often targets change, and whether it's capped

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: pay described mainly as "uncapped bonus" with a low base, pressure-heavy language about call counts, collection scripts that sound like threats, and a "billing specialist" role that is really a full bookkeeping or office-manager job

## Verify before relying on this
- The CFPB (consumerfinance.gov) for the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act and Regulation F; your state's regulator for any collection agency licence; the posting and the employer's incentive plan in writing

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 14200; 14202, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Collection agencies are regulated by each province's consumer protection rules (in Ontario, agencies register with the province, and individual collectors no longer register separately), and the rules usually cover the agency rather than a creditor's own billing staff; check your province's consumer protection office.
