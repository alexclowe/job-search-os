# Marketing Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `marketing-manager` · **Aliases:** brand manager, growth marketing manager, advertising manager
**Also read:** `marketing-specialist`, `product-manager`, `sales-manager`, `social-media-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Marketing Manager, Senior Marketing Manager, Brand Manager, Product Marketing Manager, Growth Marketing Manager, Demand Generation Manager, Marketing Communications Manager, Advertising or Promotions Manager, and Marketing Director
- Adjacent worth searching: Head of Marketing at a small company, Content Marketing Manager, Lifecycle or CRM Marketing Manager, Business Development Manager, and Account Director at an agency

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, professional networks, and executive recruiters for director-level roles; agency roles often post on agency and creative job boards
- Many senior roles are filled through referrals, former colleagues, and recruiters before or instead of a public posting

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The size of the budget and team you've managed, the channels and markets you've owned, and business results from your own records (pipeline, revenue influenced, customer growth, retention)
- Your industry and model (consumer, B2B, e-commerce, nonprofit) and tools such as marketing automation, CRM, and analytics platforms; a degree in marketing or business is common, and an MBA is sometimes preferred for brand and director roles

## Story types that land
- A launch or campaign you led from plan to results, including how you set the budget
- Changing strategy based on data: a channel you cut or a segment you added
- Building or growing a team, or fixing a relationship between marketing and sales
- A campaign that underperformed and what you learned and changed

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One to two pages; a short summary naming industry, model, and scope; each role with team size, budget range, channels, and results from your own records; mark any figure you can't confirm and fill it in before sending
- "Spearheaded integrated campaigns" reads as filler; "planned and ran the spring product launch across email, paid search, and events, worked with sales on follow-up, and reported pipeline to leadership" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then the hiring manager, then a panel with sales, product, or leadership; many companies ask for a case presentation (a go-to-market plan, a budget split, a critique of their current marketing)
- Expect questions on how you set and measure goals, how you'd use the first months in the role, working with sales, and managing an underperforming agency or team member

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $166,790 a year ($80.19 an hour); most earn between $90,260 and $293,610 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-2021, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 36,300 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-2021). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary with a bonus that's commonly tied to company or marketing goals; agency and startup pay tends to vary more
- Some roles tie part of the bonus to pipeline or revenue, which works a little like sales pay; ask how targets are set and what happened to the bonus in recent years

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: base salary within the band, bonus target, title (manager vs senior manager vs director), team and budget ownership, remote or hybrid days, and start date
- Ask how the level was decided; getting placed a level higher usually moves pay more than negotiating inside a level

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "marketing manager" roles with no team or budget that are really specialist jobs, a long list of channels owned alone, revenue targets with no sales support described, and high turnover in the role

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's posting for scope and team size; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing
