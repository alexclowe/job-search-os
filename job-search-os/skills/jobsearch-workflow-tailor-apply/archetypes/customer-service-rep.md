# Customer Service Representative — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `customer-service-rep` · **Aliases:** call center agent, customer support, client service rep
**Also read:** `customer-success-manager`, `administrative-assistant`, `billing-collections`, `insurance-sales-agent`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Customer Service Representative, Customer Service Specialist, Customer Care Representative, Customer Support Representative, Call Center Representative, Client Services Representative, Member Services Representative, and Guest Service Agent
- Adjacent worth searching: Account Representative, Chat or Email Support Agent, Technical Support Representative, Order Entry or Customer Operations Specialist, Team Lead in a contact center, and Customer Success Associate

## Where the postings live
- Usually the general job boards and company career pages; large contact centers often hire in waves for a training class, and outsourcing firms that run support for other companies post many roles at once
- Remote and work-from-home roles are commonly posted but attract heavy competition and more scams, so staffing agencies and direct company sites tend to be the safer route

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The schedule you can work (evenings, weekends, holidays), whether you can work on site or have a quiet home setup and reliable internet for remote roles, and experience with phones, chat, or email support
- Tools such as a CRM (customer relationship management system) or ticketing system, typing speed, and for some roles a second language or product-specific knowledge; insurance, banking, or collections support may need a licence or background check

## Story types that land
- Turning an angry customer around and keeping their business
- Solving a problem that took some digging, not just reading from a script
- Hitting quality and speed targets on a busy queue without cutting corners
- Spotting a repeat complaint and passing it on so the root cause got fixed

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page; the channels you handled (phone, chat, email), the kind of customers and products, and your results on the center's own measures, such as customer satisfaction, first-contact resolution, or quality scores
- "People person" reads as filler; "handled billing calls for a utility, quality score above team average most months" reads as credible when it's yours; mark any figure you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often an online application with an assessment (typing, a customer-service scenario test, or a personality questionnaire), then a phone or video interview; group interviews are common in high-volume hiring
- Role-plays such as handling an upset caller or explaining a policy the customer doesn't like are usual, and interviewers may ask about attendance and schedule flexibility early

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $21.53 an hour ($44,770 a year); most earn between $15.27 and $30.57 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 43-4051). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 289,500 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to shrink 5.3% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 43-4051). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with overtime for hours over the weekly limit; many centers add shift differentials for evenings, nights, and weekends, and a bilingual differential for a second language
- Some employers add monthly or quarterly incentive pay tied to quality, attendance, or sales of add-ons; training pay can differ from the regular rate
- Part-time and seasonal roles vary in guaranteed hours, so ask how many hours a week are guaranteed and how schedules are bid or assigned

## What's usually negotiable
- The starting rate is often fixed for a hiring class, but the shift, differential eligibility, remote days after training, and a move to a specialized queue may be open
- Experience in a similar industry or a second language sometimes earns a higher starting rate; ask directly

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "work from home" roles asking you to buy equipment or pay for training, pay described mostly as commission or bonuses, mandatory overtime during "peak" with no end date, and strict attendance points with no mention of paid sick time

## Verify before relying on this
- The US Department of Labor and your state labor department for overtime rules; the FTC (Federal Trade Commission) guidance on job scams for remote postings; the employer's own posting for schedule and pay
