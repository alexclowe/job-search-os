# Software QA / Test Engineer — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `qa-engineer` · **Aliases:** QA analyst, SDET, test automation engineer
**Also read:** `software-developer`, `business-analyst`, `it-support`, `sysadmin-cloud`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- QA (quality assurance) Analyst, QA Engineer, Software Test Engineer, Software Quality Engineer, Test Automation Engineer, SDET (software development engineer in test), and Senior or Lead QA Engineer
- Adjacent worth searching: Automation Tester, Performance Test Engineer, Quality Assurance Tester, Release Engineer, Validation Engineer in regulated industries, and Software Developer for people who code a lot of their tests

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and tech-focused boards; many QA roles, especially contract and manual testing roles, are filled through IT staffing agencies
- Referrals from developers you've worked with often matter, and a public repository with a test framework you built may help people find you

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Automation skills named in the posting (for example Selenium, Cypress, Playwright, or Appium), the programming language used for tests (Java, Python, JavaScript, or C#), and API testing tools
- Manual testing fundamentals: test plans, test cases, regression, exploratory testing, and clear bug reports in a tracker such as Jira; plus Git and continuous integration tools such as Jenkins or GitHub Actions
- Testing certifications (such as ISTQB) are usually optional; the kind of product tested (web, mobile, data, embedded) and domain knowledge like healthcare or finance often matter more

## Story types that land
- A serious bug you caught before release: how you found it, how you reported it, and what the fix prevented
- Building or fixing an automation suite so tests ran faster or broke less often
- Pushing back on a release, with evidence, and how the team decided
- Working with developers and product to agree on what "done" meant and how to test it

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A skills line grouped by tools, languages, and test types near the top; for each role, the product, what you tested, and results from your own record such as coverage added or test time cut
- "Ensured zero defects" reads as inflated; "wrote API and end-to-end tests for the checkout flow and added them to the release pipeline" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then a technical screen on testing concepts and some coding for automation roles, then a round with the engineering team
- Exercises often include "how would you test this" for an everyday object or a login page, writing test cases from a short spec, and a live or take-home automation task

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $104,300 a year ($50.14 an hour); most earn between $61,440 and $167,010 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 15-1253). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Stock or equity:** grants aren't in these pay figures, so compare them separately.
- **Openings:** about 10,700 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 5.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 15-1253). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary for full-time roles, sometimes with a yearly bonus, and at some larger tech employers an equity grant; contract and agency testing roles tend to pay hourly, often without benefits, so compare total pay rather than the rate alone
- Automation and test engineering roles tend to sit higher than manual testing roles in the same company, so the title and level named in the offer matter

## What's usually negotiable
- The salary within the band, the level or title (analyst vs engineer vs senior), sign-on bonus, the size of any equity grant, remote days, and training or certification costs tend to move
- Ask how the role's level was decided; moving from a manual title to an automation or engineer title often changes the pay band

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one tester for several development teams, "QA engineer" titles whose duties are only manual clicking, release weekends with no mention of time off, and postings that list every automation tool ever made

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's posting for the tools and level; the certifying body's site for any testing certification; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada

<!-- canada:end -->
- "Engineer" is a protected title in every province and territory (per Engineers Canada), so a quality assurance engineer or test engineer title needs a provincial engineering licence; Alberta exempts only the title "software engineer" (per APEGA), not testing titles, so Canadian postings often say "quality assurance analyst" or "tester", and you should check APEGA or your provincial engineering regulator before using the title.
