# Social / Human Service Assistant — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `human-services-assistant` · **Aliases:** case aide, community support worker
**Also read:** `social-worker`, `mental-health-counselor`, `nursing-assistant-caregiver`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Human Services Assistant, Social Services Assistant, Case Aide, Case Manager Assistant, Community Support Worker, Residential Counselor or Group Home Worker, and Family Support Worker
- Adjacent worth searching: Peer Support Specialist, Direct Support Professional, Youth Worker, Eligibility Worker, Outreach Worker, and Intake Coordinator

## Where the postings live
- Usually nonprofit and social service agency sites, the general job boards, nonprofit job boards, and state or county government job sites
- Many agencies hire relief or on-call staff first and promote from within, and referrals from current staff or field placements commonly help

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Experience with the population served (youth, older adults, people with disabilities, mental health or addiction, housing), plus availability for the posted shifts
- A background check, a child-abuse or vulnerable-adult registry check where required, and often a valid driver's licence with insurance for home visits
- Some roles ask for a state peer specialist or similar certification, or a related degree or certificate; check the posting and your state

## Story types that land
- Connecting a client to a service they needed (housing, benefits, treatment) and following up
- Calming a crisis or a tense situation safely
- Keeping case notes accurate and on time while carrying a full caseload
- Building trust with a client who was hard to reach

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One to two pages; list each role with the population served, caseload or unit size, and any training such as crisis de-escalation, first aid, or medication administration
- "Passionate about helping people" reads as filler; "supported a caseload of adults in supportive housing, connected clients to benefits, wrote timely case notes" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then a panel interview with a supervisor and staff, with scenario questions about a crisis, a boundary issue, or a client refusing help
- Some agencies add a writing sample such as a mock case note, and a background check before the offer

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $22.08 an hour ($45,930 a year); most earn between $16.44 and $30.43 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 21-1093). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 48,900 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 7.4% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 21-1093). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with shift differentials at many residential programs for evenings, overnights, and weekends
- Most assistants are non-exempt, so overtime pay applies after a full workweek under federal law; ask how overnight or sleep shifts are paid, since rules for those vary
- Guaranteed hours vary: relief and on-call positions often have none, while regular positions usually set weekly hours, so ask which one you're being offered
- Government and unionized agencies commonly publish pay schedules with steps, and some agencies reimburse mileage for home visits

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: the starting rate by related experience or degree, the shift, mileage and phone reimbursement, and training toward a certification or degree
- Where a public or union pay schedule applies, the step you start on usually matters more than the rate itself

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: very high caseloads, "flexible" roles that seem to cover several jobs, using your own car without mileage pay, and constant reposting of the same role

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state's human services or behavioral health agency for certification rules; the Department of Labor's overtime pages for sleep time and overtime; the posting and offer letter for pay and caseload
