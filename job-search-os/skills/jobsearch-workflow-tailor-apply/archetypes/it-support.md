# IT Support / Help Desk Specialist — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `it-support` · **Aliases:** help desk, desktop support, technical support specialist, network support
**Also read:** `sysadmin-cloud`, `customer-service-rep`, `cybersecurity-analyst`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Help Desk Technician or Analyst, IT Support Specialist, Desktop Support Technician, Technical Support Specialist, Service Desk Analyst, and Network Support Specialist, with first-line, second-line, and senior versions
- Adjacent worth searching: IT Technician, Field Service Technician, Deskside Support, Endpoint or Device Management Specialist, Junior Systems Administrator, and Technical Support at a software company

## Where the postings live
- Usually the general job boards, school district, hospital and government job sites, and company career pages; managed service providers (companies that run IT for other businesses) post many entry roles
- Many help desk jobs are filled through staffing agencies as contract or contract-to-hire, and internal moves from customer service or retail tech counters are common

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Hands-on work with the systems in the posting: Windows and macOS, Microsoft 365, Active Directory or Entra ID (for user accounts and passwords), a ticketing system such as ServiceNow, and basic networking
- Certifications are often valued at entry level, not usually a hard gate: CompTIA A+ (two exams covering hardware, operating systems, and troubleshooting), then CompTIA Network+ or Security+; ITIL Foundation (the IT service management framework, from PeopleCert) is common on service desk postings
- Shift availability (nights, weekends, on-call), a driver's licence for field roles, and whether you can lift and move equipment

## Story types that land
- A ticket you solved that others had given up on, and how you found the cause
- A time you calmed a frustrated user and fixed the problem at the same time
- Something you documented or automated so the same issue stopped coming back to the desk
- Handling a busy day or an outage: how you decided what to fix first

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page; a skills block near the top (operating systems, Microsoft 365, ticketing tool, networking basics, hardware) and certifications listed with the issuer; home-lab or volunteer IT work counts when you are new
- "Provided world-class support" reads as filler; "cleared the password-reset backlog by writing a self-service guide the whole office now uses" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the desk lead that mixes behavioral questions with troubleshooting scenarios ("a user can't print; walk me through what you check")
- Some employers add a short practical: reset an account, image a laptop, or talk a mock caller through a fix

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $29.74 an hour ($61,860 a year); most earn between $19.70 and $48.34 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 15-1232, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 48,700 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** some college, without a degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 15-1232). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Entry help desk roles are often hourly, and many move to an annual salary at the desktop support, network support, or senior level
- Hourly roles are commonly eligible for overtime, and night or weekend coverage may carry a shift differential; ask how on-call and after-hours calls are paid
- Contract and agency roles tend to pay a higher hourly rate with fewer benefits, and may not come with guaranteed hours, so compare total pay rather than the rate alone

## What's usually negotiable
- The hourly rate or salary within the range, shift choice, differential eligibility, guaranteed hours on contract roles, and paid exam fees or study time for certifications tend to move
- Ask how people move from first-line to second-line support and what the pay step looks like; a clear path often matters more than a small raise now

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "IT support" that also covers the building, phones, and security cameras with no extra pay, one-person IT for a whole company at entry pay, unpaid on-call, and contract roles with no stated hours

## Verify before relying on this
- CompTIA (comptia.org) and PeopleCert for current exam versions before buying study material; the employer's posting for shifts and on-call terms; your state labour department for overtime rules
