# School / Career Counselor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `school-counselor` · **Aliases:** guidance counselor, academic advisor, career counselor
**Also read:** `mental-health-counselor`, `teacher`, `school-administrator`, `social-worker`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- School Counselor (elementary, middle or high school), Guidance Counselor, College and Career Counselor, Academic Advisor or Academic Counselor at colleges, Career Counselor, and lead or department-chair counselor roles
- Adjacent worth searching: admissions counselor, college access program coordinator, student success coach, career services advisor, and workforce-program career coach

## Where the postings live
- Usually district career pages and state education job boards for K-12 roles, and college and university job sites for advising and career-services roles; workforce agencies and nonprofits post career-coach roles on the general boards
- K-12 hiring usually follows the school calendar, with most postings in spring and summer

## What a recruiter screens for first
- For K-12: a state school counselor certificate or licence, which usually means a master's in school counseling, a school-based internship, and a state or national exam; a few states also look at teaching experience
- For colleges: a master's in counseling, higher education or a related field is common, plus experience with the student system and degree audits
- Background-check eligibility, the grade levels you know, and experience with scheduling, college applications, or crisis response

## Story types that land
- A student you helped get back on track academically or through a crisis
- Improving a school-wide measure such as attendance, course completion, or college applications
- Running classroom lessons or small groups and how you knew they worked
- Working with teachers, families, and outside services on a hard case

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Certification and state near the top, then each role with school level, caseload described in words, programs you ran, and systems you used (student information system, college-application platforms)
- "Passionate about student success" reads as filler; "counselor for a senior class, ran the college-application program and small anxiety groups" reads as credible (use your own details)

## How interviews usually run
- Often a screening interview, then a panel with the principal, counselors and sometimes a parent or student; some ask for a sample lesson or a written response to a scenario
- Expect questions on a student disclosing harm, balancing scheduling duties with counseling, and working with families who disagree with you

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $64,330 a year ($30.93 an hour); most earn between $45,020 and $104,770 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 21-1012). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 27,800 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 2.9% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a master's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 21-1012). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- In public K-12, pay usually follows the teacher salary schedule, with steps for years and lanes for education; counselors sometimes work extra contract days and are paid for them
- College and nonprofit roles usually pay an annual salary set by the institution; public roles typically include a pension

## What's usually negotiable
- The schedule itself rarely moves; step placement for prior years (including out-of-state or counseling years outside schools) and lane placement for coursework often can
- Ask about extra contract days, summer work, and stipends for testing coordination or other duties

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: counselors used mainly as test coordinators or substitutes, caseloads the district won't state, one counselor covering several schools, and "other duties" that crowd out counseling

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state department of education for school counselor certification and reciprocity; the American School Counselor Association's state requirements page; the district HR page for salary schedules

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 41320; 41321, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- School counsellors in many provinces are certified teachers with added counselling qualifications and are paid on the teacher grid in the collective agreement, while college and career counsellors are usually unregulated; check your province's teacher certification rules and Job Bank's requirements.
