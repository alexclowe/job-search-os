# Shipping, Receiving & Inventory Clerk — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `inventory-shipping-clerk` · **Aliases:** inventory specialist, production planner, expediter
**Also read:** `warehouse-associate`, `logistics-supply-chain`, `buyer-purchasing-agent`, `warehouse-supervisor`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Shipping Clerk, Receiving Clerk, Shipping and Receiving Clerk, Inventory Clerk, Inventory Control Specialist, Materials Control Associate, Order Fulfillment Specialist, and Production Planner or Expediter
- Adjacent worth searching: Shipping Coordinator, Traffic Assistant, Cycle Counter, Parts Clerk, Stock Control Clerk, and Warehouse Lead

## Where the postings live
- Usually the general job boards, company career sites with quick-apply forms, and staffing agencies; manufacturers, distributors, retailers, and hospitals often post these roles on their own sites
- Many clerks are promoted from warehouse or production floor roles, so an internal move is often faster than an outside application

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Accuracy with counts and paperwork (packing slips, bills of lading, purchase orders), comfort with spreadsheets, and experience with an inventory or warehouse management system or an enterprise system such as SAP
- Carrier and shipping software experience, and for some roles forklift or pallet jack experience; employers usually train and evaluate forklift operators on their own equipment
- Availability for the posted shift, and whether you can handle the lifting and standing in the posting

## Story types that land
- Finding and fixing an inventory count that didn't match the system
- Catching a wrong or short shipment before it went out, or getting a vendor to fix one
- Setting up a simpler receiving, labeling, or cycle-count routine
- Keeping orders moving during a busy season or a system change

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page; list each job with the systems used, the kind of goods, and results from your own record, such as count accuracy or orders shipped per day
- "Detail-oriented" reads as filler; "received and checked in vendor shipments against purchase orders in SAP, ran weekly cycle counts, printed carrier labels" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an in-person interview with the warehouse or materials manager, with questions about how you handle discrepancies and deadlines
- Some employers add a short spreadsheet, math, or data-entry test, a site tour, and a background check or drug screen

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $21.76 an hour ($45,260 a year); most earn between $16.66 and $29.90 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 43-5071, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 93,900 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 43-5071). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, sometimes with a differential for second or third shift; lead and planner roles may carry a higher rate
- Clerks are usually non-exempt, so overtime pay applies after a full workweek under federal law, and month-end or peak season often brings extra hours
- Guaranteed hours are uncommon but most clerk roles are full-time; ask how hours change in slow periods
- Unionized warehouses and plants commonly publish wage steps in the contract

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: the starting rate by systems experience (especially an enterprise system), shift choice, and a path to planner, coordinator, or lead roles
- Where a union scale applies, the step you start on usually matters more than the rate itself

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a clerk title that is mostly loading trucks, "other duties as assigned" covering several roles, and planner or coordinator titles paid at a clerk rate

## Verify before relying on this
- OSHA's powered industrial trucks pages for forklift training; the Department of Labor's overtime pages and your state labor department; the posting and offer letter for shift and rate
