# Business / Systems Analyst — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `business-analyst` · **Aliases:** systems analyst, business systems analyst, health informatics specialist
**Also read:** `data-analyst`, `project-manager`, `product-manager`, `management-consultant`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Business Analyst, Business Systems Analyst, Systems Analyst, IT Analyst, Applications Analyst, and the senior and lead versions of each
- Adjacent worth searching: Requirements Analyst, Functional Analyst or Consultant (for a named business system), Process Analyst, Product Owner, Health Informatics Specialist, and Programmer Analyst when the role includes some coding

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages and the general job boards; many analyst roles sit inside IT, finance, or operations teams, and consulting and staffing firms post a large share of contract work
- Internal moves are common: people who know a business process well are often moved into the analyst seat for a system change

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Evidence you have gathered requirements and turned them into something a team built: user stories, process maps, functional specs, test cases
- The systems and domains named in the posting (an enterprise resource planning system such as SAP, a customer system such as Salesforce, a health record system, banking or insurance processes) plus tools such as Jira, Excel, SQL, and Power BI
- Certifications are often valued, not usually a gate: the International Institute of Business Analysis (IIBA) offers the Entry Certificate in Business Analysis (ECBA), the Certification of Capability in Business Analysis (CCBA) for analysts with some experience, and the Certified Business Analysis Professional (CBAP) for experienced analysts

## Story types that land
- A requirement that was vague or disputed, how you pinned it down, and what the team built as a result
- A process you mapped that exposed waste or a gap, and what changed after
- A system rollout you supported: testing, training, go-live, and what you did when something broke
- Translating between a business team and developers when each thought the other was wrong

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One to two pages; name the systems, the business process, and the delivery method (agile or plan-driven) in each role; certifications near the top
- "Liaised with stakeholders" reads as filler; "wrote the approval-flow requirements for the new purchasing system and cut the back-and-forth between finance and IT" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, a hiring-manager interview, then a panel with the business and IT people you would sit between
- Many interviews include a case: elicit requirements from an interviewer playing a stakeholder, write a user story, or map a process on a whiteboard; some add a short SQL or Excel check

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $105,850 a year ($50.89 an hour); most earn between $67,340 and $167,710 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 15-1211). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 32,900 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 7.9% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 15-1211). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some employers add a yearly bonus, and contract analyst roles are commonly paid hourly or by day through a consulting or staffing firm, often without benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, title or level, remote or hybrid days, start date, and paid training or certification fees tend to move
- Ask whether the role supports one system or several; wider system scope often supports a higher level

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a business analyst posting that is really report writing or data entry, "analyst" duties plus full project management at analyst pay, and a long list of unrelated systems expected at expert level

## Verify before relying on this
- IIBA (iiba.org) for current certification rules; the employer's posting for the systems and domain; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply

- Business analysis isn't a regulated profession in Canada. Public-sector analyst roles often sit on published classification and pay grids, and federal roles may ask for English and French at set levels, so check the classification and the language profile on the posting.
