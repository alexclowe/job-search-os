# Bank Teller / Universal Banker — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `bank-teller` · **Aliases:** personal banker, universal banker, loan processor/clerk
**Also read:** `customer-service-rep`, `loan-officer`, `retail-sales-associate`, `financial-advisor`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Bank Teller, Teller, Part-Time Teller, Head Teller, Member Service Representative (at credit unions), Universal Banker, Personal Banker, and Financial Services Representative
- Adjacent worth searching: Loan Processor or Loan Clerk, New Accounts Representative, Branch Operations Specialist, Call Center Banking Representative, and Assistant Branch Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually bank and credit union career sites, the general job boards, and banking-specific job boards; credit unions and community banks often post on their own sites only
- Many branches hire part-time tellers regularly, and a referral from a current employee commonly helps; internal moves from teller to universal banker are common

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Cash-handling accuracy (retail cashier experience counts), customer service, and availability, including Saturdays at many branches
- A background check and often a credit check; under a federal rule, people with certain past convictions involving dishonesty or breach of trust may need consent from the FDIC (the bank deposit insurance agency) before a bank can hire them, and credit unions have a similar rule
- For universal and personal bankers, comfort talking about accounts and products; roles that sell investments may ask for the SIE exam and securities licences, and bank employees who take mortgage applications are commonly registered in the federal loan-originator registry

## Story types that land
- Balancing your drawer accurately over time, or catching a counterfeit or suspicious transaction
- Spotting possible fraud or elder financial abuse and escalating it properly
- Helping a customer with a problem that led to them opening a new account or product
- Handling a long line or an upset customer calmly

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page; list cash-handling volume, balancing accuracy, and referrals or product results from your own record, plus the banking systems you used
- "Excellent customer service" reads as filler; "part-time teller, balanced drawer daily, trained on fraud alerts, top branch referrer for new accounts" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the branch manager, with behavioral questions about accuracy, handling an upset customer, and spotting fraud
- Some banks add an online assessment, a math or cash-counting test, and a role-play of a customer conversation

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $20.69 an hour ($43,030 a year); most earn between $16.52 and $23.88 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 43-3071, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 37,300 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 43-3071). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, often with small incentives for referring customers to products; universal bankers may earn a higher rate or a sales bonus
- Tellers are usually non-exempt, so overtime pay applies after a full workweek under federal law; ask how Saturday or extended hours are paid
- Shift differentials are less common in branches, though some banks pay extra for bilingual skills or call-center evening shifts
- Guaranteed hours vary: some part-time teller roles have a set minimum, so ask how many hours are guaranteed and whether you can move to full-time

## What's usually negotiable
- The starting rate often moves with prior cash-handling or banking experience; hours, branch location, and a path to universal banker or head teller may also be open
- Ask how sales or referral goals are measured, since they tend to affect bonuses and reviews

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: heavy sales quotas in a teller role, "universal banker" that means doing several jobs at a teller rate, and part-time roles with very few guaranteed hours

## Verify before relying on this
- The FDIC's pages on its consent rule for past convictions; FINRA's pages for the SIE exam and securities licences; the Department of Labor's overtime pages; the posting for the hours and pay
