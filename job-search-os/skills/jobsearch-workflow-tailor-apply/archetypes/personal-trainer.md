# Personal Trainer — job-search archetype

**Slug:** `personal-trainer` · **Aliases:** fitness coach, strength and conditioning coach, strength coach, group fitness instructor
**Also read:** none

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Personal Trainer (gym-employed or independent-contractor), Fitness Coach, Strength and Conditioning Coach, Group Fitness Instructor, Fitness Manager, Head Coach (studio), Online Coach
- Adjacent worth searching: Corporate Wellness Coordinator, Health Coach, Exercise Physiologist (with credential), Studio Manager, Athletic Performance roles (with credential), Physical Therapy Aide

## Where the postings live
- Usually gym-chain and studio career pages, fitness-industry job boards, certifying bodies' job boards, and the general boards; independent work is built through referral, a local presence, and online platforms rather than postings
- Studio hiring is often by audition or trial session; the resume gets you the audition

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Current certification from a body the employer recognises (NASM, ACE, ACSM, NSCA, ISSA, or a specialty credential), CPR/AED, insurance for contractors, specialties (populations, sports, rehab-adjacent), retention and client counts you can substantiate, and sales comfort where the gym expects trainers to sell
- Availability windows (early mornings, evenings, weekends) are screened before skills

## Story types that land
- A client outcome with the honest arc: starting point, what you changed, what they achieved, and how long it took
- Retention: how you keep clients month to month
- A programming or injury-management decision where you referred out or adjusted well
- How you filled your schedule from scratch

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Certifications with issuing body and expiry near the top; per role: client load, session types, populations, retention in relative terms if that is all you can document; sales targets met only with records
- Avoid "transform lives" language; describe the work and the results you can show

## How interviews usually run
- Often a conversation with the fitness manager plus a practical session: coaching a mock client or a staff member through a short workout
- Expect questions on certifications, client retention, and how you'd build a client base

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: the per-session rate or commission share, paid floor or onboarding time, any guaranteed hours, continuing-education support, schedule, and whether the gym provides leads
- Worth asking: whether clients stay with you or with the gym if you leave (who keeps the book), and whether commission on a package is charged back if the client asks for a refund

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: commission-only with floor-time requirements and no client pipeline, sales quotas stated as the primary metric, "must purchase branded apparel", non-competes that follow you to your own clients

## Verify before relying on this
- The certifying bodies' own sites for credential status and continuing-education rules; state requirements for insurance and contractor status
