# Preschool Teacher / Childcare Worker — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `early-childhood-educator` · **Aliases:** ECE, preschool teacher, daycare teacher, nanny
**Also read:** `teacher`, `teaching-assistant`, `substitute-teacher`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Preschool Teacher, Pre-K Teacher, Toddler Teacher, Infant Teacher, Lead Teacher or Assistant Teacher in a childcare center, Montessori Teacher, Child Care Worker, and Early Head Start or Head Start Teacher
- Adjacent worth searching: Center Director or Assistant Director, Family Child Care Provider (running a licensed home program), Nanny or Family Assistant, Before- and After-School Program Staff, Early Intervention Assistant, and Teaching Assistant roles in public pre-K

## Where the postings live
- Usually childcare center and preschool websites, Head Start and Early Head Start agencies, public school district pages for pre-K, faith-based and community programs, and the general job boards
- Many centers hire continuously and fill roles by walk-in, referral from current staff, or a call to the director; nanny work is often found through family referrals, local parent groups, and care-matching sites

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Whether you meet your state's childcare licensing rules for the role (lead teacher and assistant often differ), plus the background check, fingerprinting, health screening, and CPR and first aid the state asks for
- Credentials that commonly move you to lead teacher: the Child Development Associate (CDA) credential from the Council for Professional Recognition, or an associate or bachelor's degree in early childhood education; Head Start lead roles and public pre-K often ask for a degree, and public pre-K may ask for a state teaching licence
- The age group you have worked with (infants, toddlers, preschoolers), the hours you can cover (early opens and late closes), and how soon you can start

## Story types that land
- A child who struggled to settle, share, or communicate, and the routine or approach that helped
- Planning activities around a curriculum or learning standards and noticing what each child was ready for next
- Building trust with a family, including a hard conversation about behaviour or development
- Keeping children safe: supervision, ratios, allergies, a health or safety incident handled calmly and reported properly

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Credentials first (CDA, degree, state registry or training hours, CPR and first aid with expiry dates); for each role, the age group, group size, and the curriculum or assessment tools you used
- "Loves children" reads as filler; "led a room of toddlers with one assistant, ran daily observations and parent updates in the center's app" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short phone call, then an interview with the director, and commonly a working interview or classroom observation where you spend part of a day with the children
- Expect questions on handling a biting or tantrum situation, talking with an upset parent, supervision and ratios, and how you plan activities for mixed ages

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $16.82 an hour ($34,980 a year); most earn between $11.66 and $22.12 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 39-9011, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 217,000 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 39-9011). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 24% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, and commonly on the lower end for the education asked; lead teachers, degree holders, and CDA holders often earn a higher rate, and public pre-K teachers may be paid from the district's salary schedule instead
- Shift differentials are uncommon in childcare, though some centers pay a little more for early opens, late closes, or split shifts; overtime rules follow federal and state law for hourly staff, so ask how extra hours after closing or at events are paid
- Guaranteed hours can vary: many centers send staff home early when attendance is low, so ask whether your hours are guaranteed or cut with enrollment; free or reduced childcare for your own children is a common benefit worth pricing in
- Nannies are commonly paid hourly by the family, who may owe employer taxes and overtime like any household employer; many self-employed home providers set their own rates

## What's usually negotiable
- The hourly rate within the center's range (often tied to credentials and years of experience), guaranteed hours, which room and age group you take, and paid time for planning or training
- Many employers will pay for or reimburse CDA fees, college credits, or state scholarship programs; ask about these and about any wage bonus tied to earning a credential

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "flexible hours" that means hours cut at short notice, ratios the posting won't discuss, no paid planning time, lead-teacher duties at an assistant's rate, and constant re-posting of the same room, which may signal turnover

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state's childcare licensing agency and early childhood workforce registry for staff qualifications; the Council for Professional Recognition for the CDA; Head Start rules through the federal Office of Head Start; CareerOneStop's License Finder for state requirements (link, don't copy)

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 42202, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Early childhood educators are certified or registered by each province, and the title and levels differ: in Ontario only registered members of the College of Early Childhood Educators may use the title early childhood educator, while British Columbia certifies educators through its BC Early Childhood Educator Registry. Many provinces also run wage-enhancement programs for certified staff, so check your provincial early childhood educator certification office before relying on a US credential.
