# Software Developer — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `software-developer` · **Aliases:** software engineer, full-stack developer, front-end developer, back-end developer, web developer, programmer, application developer
**Also read:** `qa-engineer`, `sysadmin-cloud`, `data-scientist`, `ux-designer`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Software Developer, Software Engineer, Software Development Engineer, Application Developer, Web Developer, Full-Stack / Front-End / Back-End Engineer, and the senior, staff, and lead versions of each
- Adjacent worth searching: Application Integration Engineer, DevOps or Infrastructure Engineer, Systems Engineer, Software Architect, Solutions Engineer, and Developer Advocate when the person likes explaining as much as building

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages first, then the general job boards and startup-focused boards; many roles are also filled through referrals and recruiters who reach out on professional networks
- Open-source contributions, a public portfolio, or a technical blog often surface people before a posting does

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The languages, frameworks, and platforms named in the posting, matched word for word (for example C#, C++, CSS, AWS, Docker, Kafka), plus version control (Git, GitHub) and a ticketing tool such as Jira
- Years of experience at the level asked, the kind of systems built (web, mobile, embedded, data), and whether work authorization or clearance is needed

## Story types that land
- Something you shipped end to end: the requirement, the design choice you made, and what happened after release
- A bug or outage you traced and fixed, and what you changed so it didn't come back
- A performance, reliability, or cost improvement you can explain in plain words
- Working with product, design, or other engineers to agree on scope and interfaces

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page early career, two for experienced; a skills line near the top grouped by languages, frameworks, and tools; links to GitHub or a portfolio when the work is public
- "Built a scalable, robust platform" reads as filler; "moved the order service to a queue so checkout stopped timing out at peak" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then a technical screen (live coding or a take-home), then a loop of coding, system design for mid-level and up, and behavioral rounds
- Many teams ask you to talk through your reasoning while you code; a few use a paid trial project instead of live coding

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $135,980 a year ($65.38 an hour); most earn between $82,460 and $214,670 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 15-1252, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Stock or equity:** grants aren't in these pay figures, so compare them separately.
- **Openings:** about 105,000 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 3 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 15-1252). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; some employers add an annual bonus, stock or equity grants that vest over several years, and a sign-on bonus
- Contract roles tend to pay hourly through an agency, often without benefits, so compare total pay and benefits rather than the rate alone

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, level or title, the equity grant and its vesting schedule, sign-on bonus, start date, and remote or hybrid days tend to move
- Ask how the level was decided; getting placed one level higher usually moves more than negotiating inside a level

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a long list of unrelated stacks for one role, "rockstar" or "ninja" language, unpaid take-home projects that look like real client work, on-call with no mention of rotation or pay, and titles that say senior with junior pay ranges

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's own posting for the stack and level; published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 21231; 21232; 21234, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- In most provinces "engineer" is a protected title, so calling yourself a software engineer without a provincial engineering licence can breach the law; Alberta has been an exception for software engineers since 2023 (per Engineers Canada). Canadian postings often say "developer" for this reason, so check your provincial engineering regulator before using the title.
