# Instructional Coordinator / Designer — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `instructional-designer` · **Aliases:** instructional designer, curriculum specialist, instructional coach
**Also read:** `training-specialist`, `teacher`, `professor`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Instructional Designer, Learning Experience Designer, Curriculum Developer, Curriculum Specialist or Coordinator, Instructional Technologist, eLearning Developer, Instructional Coach, and senior or lead versions of each
- Adjacent worth searching: learning and development specialist, training specialist, content developer for education companies, and director of curriculum and instruction in schools

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages and the general boards for corporate roles, university job sites for online-learning teams, and district pages for K-12 curriculum and coaching roles
- Contract and freelance work is common and often comes through agencies, professional networks, and learning-industry communities

## What a recruiter screens for first
- A portfolio: sample modules, storyboards, or course designs, with your part in each made clear; many hiring managers look at this before the resume
- Tools the posting names, such as authoring tools (Articulate Storyline or Rise, Adobe Captivate), video and graphics tools, and a learning management system; plus design models such as ADDIE or backward design
- For K-12 coordinator and coaching roles, a teaching licence and classroom years are commonly expected, and some states ask for an administrator licence

## Story types that land
- A course or program you designed from a needs analysis through launch, and how you measured whether it worked
- Turning a subject expert's dense material into something learners finished
- Moving in-person training online, or cutting a long course down without losing what mattered
- Coaching teachers or trainers to use a new curriculum well

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A portfolio link at the top, then roles with audiences, formats (eLearning, instructor-led, blended), tools, and outcomes from your own records such as completion or assessment results
- "Created engaging learning experiences" reads as filler; "built a blended onboarding course for new call-center staff and cut time to first solo call" reads as credible (use your own details)

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then a hiring-manager interview and a portfolio walkthrough; many employers add a design exercise, such as storyboarding a short module from a given topic
- Expect questions on working with subject experts, handling tight timelines, and how you'd evaluate a program

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $77,440 a year ($37.23 an hour); most earn between $47,980 and $121,670 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 25-9031). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 23,100 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 1.6% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a master's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 25-9031). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary, with a bonus at some corporate employers; school-district coordinators and coaches are often paid on the teacher or administrator salary schedule
- Contract designers are commonly paid hourly or per project, so compare benefits and gaps between contracts, not just the rate

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: base salary, title level, remote or hybrid days, professional development and conference budget, and software licences for your own work
- In school districts, step placement for prior teaching years tends to move more than the salary figure

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one designer expected to build, film, edit, administer the learning system and train, large unpaid design tests, and "instructional designer" titles that are really slide-formatting roles

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's posting for tools and portfolio expectations; your state department of education for any licence K-12 coordinator roles need; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing
