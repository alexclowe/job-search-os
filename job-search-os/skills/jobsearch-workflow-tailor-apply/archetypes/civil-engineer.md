# Civil Engineer — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `civil-engineer` · **Aliases:** structural engineer, transportation engineer, EIT
**Also read:** `construction-manager`, `project-manager`, `industrial-engineer`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Civil Engineer, Engineer in Training (EIT) or Engineer Intern, Staff Engineer, Project Engineer, Design Engineer, and specialty titles: Structural, Geotechnical, Transportation or Traffic, Water Resources, Municipal, and Site or Land Development Engineer; plus senior, project manager, and principal levels
- Adjacent worth searching: construction project engineer, city or county engineer, plan reviewer, estimator, and civil engineering technician or designer roles

## Where the postings live
- Usually consulting and design firms, construction contractors, and public agencies (city, county, state transportation departments), plus the general boards and engineering society job boards
- Public-agency roles often run through the civil service system with set application windows, and many consulting roles are filled through professional networks

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Licensing path in plain words: an accredited engineering degree (often ABET-accredited), the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam from NCEES to become an engineer intern, several years of supervised experience, then the Principles and Practice of Engineering (PE) exam and a state licence
- For senior roles, a PE licence usually matters most because only a licensed engineer can sign and seal engineering plans, including plans for public work; some roles ask for a licence in a particular state
- Software and specialty: AutoCAD Civil 3D, MicroStation, Revit, and analysis or modeling tools named in the posting; plus design standards the employer works to

## Story types that land
- A project you designed or managed from concept through construction, and a problem you solved along the way
- Catching an error in plans or calculations before it went to construction
- Working with permitting agencies, contractors, or the public to keep a project moving
- Balancing cost, schedule and safety on a design decision

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Licences (EIT or PE, with states) near the top, then a project list with type, your role, and the scope in plain words; software and standards grouped in a skills line
- "Managed large projects" reads as filler; "designed stormwater systems for a residential subdivision and handled county permitting" reads as credible (use your own details)

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then interviews with the engineering manager and senior engineers; technical questions about past projects, design codes, and calculations are common
- Public agencies commonly use a structured panel interview with scored questions, and some firms add a short technical exercise or a walk-through of your work samples

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $100,840 a year ($48.48 an hour); most earn between $68,240 and $163,220 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 17-2051). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 22,700 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 6.4% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 17-2051). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; consulting firms may add a bonus, and some pay straight-time or overtime for hours beyond the standard week, so ask
- Earning the PE licence commonly brings a raise or promotion; public agencies usually pay from a salary schedule with a pension

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: base salary, a raise or bonus on passing the PE, exam and licence fees, overtime or comp-time rules, professional development hours, and remote days
- At public agencies, the step you start on tends to move more than the salary figure

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "PE required" for a junior salary, heavy unpaid overtime described as "dedication," and roles that ask you to seal work you won't be able to supervise

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state engineering licensing board and NCEES (ncees.org) for exam and licensure rules; the employer's posting for software and standards; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for the field's outlook

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 21300, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Engineer is a protected title in every province and territory, so you need a licence from your provincial engineering regulator (such as APEGA in Alberta) to call yourself a civil engineer or to seal plans; graduates commonly start as engineers-in-training, and a US professional engineering licence doesn't transfer on its own, so check Engineers Canada and your provincial regulator first.
