# Office Manager / Admin Supervisor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `office-manager` · **Aliases:** office administrator, administrative supervisor
**Also read:** `administrative-assistant`, `executive-assistant`, `medical-admin`, `healthcare-administrator`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Office Manager, Office Administrator, Administrative Supervisor, Office Supervisor, Office Coordinator, and Staff Services Manager, often with the setting named (dental, medical, law firm, construction, nonprofit)
- Adjacent worth searching: Practice Manager, Operations Coordinator, Facilities Coordinator, Customer Service Supervisor, Accounts Payable or Receivable Supervisor when you ran billing, and Administrative Services Manager at larger employers

## Where the postings live
- Usually the general job boards, company career pages, and local networks; small offices often hire through referrals, their accountant, or a local business association
- Industry boards (dental, medical, legal, construction) commonly carry practice and office manager roles, and staffing agencies may fill interim coverage

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The breadth of what you ran: vendors, supplies, facilities, scheduling, payroll inputs, billing, onboarding, and how many people you supervised or supported
- The systems named in the posting, such as Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, QuickBooks, a practice-management or billing system, and a payroll tool; industry experience often matters, for example medical billing or dental scheduling

## Story types that land
- Getting a messy office organized: vendors renegotiated, supplies controlled, files cleaned up
- Supervising or training front-desk or clerical staff and fixing a coverage problem
- Moving the office to a new system, space, or process with little disruption
- Catching a billing, payroll, or compliance issue before it cost money

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page, or two with long experience; each role names the office type, headcount supported, staff supervised, and the areas you owned (facilities, vendors, bookkeeping, HR paperwork)
- "Wore many hats" reads as filler on its own; list the hats plainly, with results you can confirm; mark any figure you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the owner, managing partner, or practice lead; small offices may ask you back to meet the whole team
- Questions about handling a difficult employee, a vendor problem, or competing requests from partners are common, and some employers give a QuickBooks, Excel, or scheduling exercise

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $69,500 a year ($33.41 an hour); most earn between $45,610 and $104,710 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 43-1011). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 127,400 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 0.1% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 43-1011). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Either an annual salary or hourly pay, depending on the size of the office and whether the role is classed as exempt from overtime; hourly office managers usually earn overtime for hours over the weekly limit
- Some offices add a year-end bonus or profit sharing, especially in small medical, dental, and law practices
- Part-time office manager roles are common in small practices; ask about guaranteed hours each week
<!-- pay-na: differentials (office manager roles usually run on a daytime office schedule, so shift differentials rarely apply) -->

## What's usually negotiable
- The salary or hourly rate, exempt or non-exempt status (which decides overtime), bonus, paid time off, schedule, and title may move, especially when the job has grown beyond the posting
- Ask whether billing, bookkeeping, or HR duties come with extra pay or a title change, since those duties tend to grow over time

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: office manager, bookkeeper, HR, and receptionist rolled into one at an entry-level rate, a salaried role with long hours that looks designed to avoid overtime, "family atmosphere" with no mention of backup coverage, and frequent turnover in the same seat

## Verify before relying on this
- The US Department of Labor and your state labor department for exempt-status and overtime rules; the employer's offer letter for bonus terms; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada

- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.

- Office manager roles aren't licensed, but overtime eligibility for managers varies by province under employment standards, and some offices ask for bilingual English and French; check your province's employment standards before accepting a salaried offer.
