# Dental Assistant — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `dental-assistant` · **Aliases:** DA, expanded-function dental assistant
**Also read:** `medical-assistant`, `medical-admin`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Dental Assistant, Registered Dental Assistant (RDA), Certified Dental Assistant (CDA), Expanded Function Dental Assistant (EFDA) or Expanded Duty Dental Assistant, Orthodontic Assistant, Oral Surgery Assistant, and Lead Dental Assistant
- Adjacent worth searching: Sterilization Technician, Treatment Coordinator, Dental Front Office Coordinator, and Dental Office Manager for experienced assistants

## Where the postings live
- Usually private dental practices, group practices and dental service organizations, specialty offices (orthodontics, oral surgery, pediatric), and community health centers
- Many small offices hire through referrals from dentists and hygienists, dental temp agencies, and local dental society boards; working a few temp days often leads to an offer

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The duties your state lets you do and the credential you hold for them: each state dental board sets its own rules, and many ask for a separate radiography (X-ray) permit or certificate; expanded functions usually need extra training and a state credential
- The Dental Assisting National Board (DANB) runs the national Certified Dental Assistant exam and other exams that many states accept or ask for
- Chairside experience in the office's specialty, infection control, CPR or BLS (Basic Life Support), and the practice software (for example Dentrix or Eaglesoft)

## Story types that land
- Keeping the schedule on track: fast room turnover and instrument prep without cutting infection-control corners
- Helping an anxious patient or a child through a procedure
- Catching something the dentist needed to know (a medical history change, a broken instrument, a missed X-ray)
- Taking on new duties once you earned the credential, such as coronal polishing or sealants where your state allows

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Credentials near the top with the state named (RDA, CDA, radiography, expanded functions), then CPR or BLS; each job names the practice type and the procedures you assisted on
- "Excellent chairside manner" reads as filler; "four-handed assisting for crowns and implants, took and mounted digital X-rays, charted in Dentrix" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short phone screen, then an interview with the office manager and the dentist; many offices ask for a working interview (a paid or unpaid trial day chairside)
- Questions commonly cover infection control, how you handle a nervous patient, and which procedures you have assisted on

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $23.11 an hour ($48,070 a year); most earn between $17.85 and $29.93 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 31-9091). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 53,000 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 7.4% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a certificate or diploma program (not a degree), the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 31-9091). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly; overtime applies for extra hours, and shift differentials are uncommon in dental offices because most work daytime hours, though some offices pay more for evening or Saturday shifts
- Ask about guaranteed hours: some offices send assistants home early on slow days or when patients cancel, which cuts pay
- Some offices add a production or collections bonus shared by the team; temp agency days often pay a higher rate with no benefits

## What's usually negotiable
- The hourly rate within the office's range (often by credential and experience), pay for a working interview day, guaranteed hours, and paid continuing education or exam fees tend to move
- Help paying for a radiography or expanded-function course is often worth asking for

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: an unpaid working interview that runs longer than a single trial, duties your state doesn't let assistants do, "front and back" roles at a single assistant's rate, and offices that describe being "short-handed" as a selling point

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state board of dentistry and DANB's state-by-state requirements pages (danb.org) for what you may do and which credentials count; CareerOneStop's License Finder for your state
