# Bookkeeper — job-search archetype

**Slug:** `bookkeeper` · **Aliases:** full-charge bookkeeper, accounting clerk, staff accountant (adjacent), accounting associate
**Also read:** `accountant` (if present)

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Bookkeeper, Full-Charge Bookkeeper, Accounting Specialist/Associate, Accounts Payable or Receivable Specialist, Payroll Specialist, Staff Accountant (often the next step), Accounting Manager (small company)
- Adjacent worth searching: Client Accounting Services Associate (at firms), Billing Specialist, Office Manager with bookkeeping, Controller (small business, with experience)

## Where the postings live
- The general boards carry most volume; accounting-firm career pages for client-accounting roles; local staffing agencies specialising in accounting; software-vendor advisor directories and communities for freelance and part-time work
- Small businesses often hire through referral or a local firm; a firm relationship can be worth more than a board

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Software by name and depth (QuickBooks Online or Desktop, Xero, NetSuite, Sage, Bill.com, Gusto/ADP), month-end close ownership, multi-entity or multi-client experience, and whether you have run payroll and sales tax filings
- Certification (a QuickBooks or Xero advisor credential, or a bookkeeping certificate) helps at the first pass; an accounting degree is often preferred for staff-accountant titles

## Story types that land
- A close you shortened or cleaned up (what was wrong, what you changed, how long it takes now)
- A reconciliation or error you caught and what it would have cost
- A client or owner you kept informed in plain language
- Systems work: a migration, an integration, an automation you set up and maintained

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Software list near the top; per role: entity count, revenue scale in relative terms if confidential, what you owned in the close; one to two pages
- Avoid "detail-oriented" as a claim; show it with a specific catch. Numbers only where you actually know them

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the owner, controller, or accounting manager; a short practical test (a reconciliation, journal entries, or a software exercise) is common
- Expect questions on month-end close, the specific accounting software, accuracy habits, and confidentiality

## How pay is usually structured
- Hourly is common for part-time and small-business roles; annual salary for full-time roles in larger firms; contract and fractional bookkeepers often bill hourly or a monthly fee per client

## What's usually negotiable
- Base rate or salary, hours and remote days, software certification support, and for contract work the scope and monthly fee

## Red flags in postings
- "Bookkeeper/office manager/HR/receptionist" in one title, full-charge duties at clerk pay, "must be available for month-end weekends", no mention of software

## Verify before relying on this
- The software vendors' own certification pages; state requirements for payroll and sales tax; AIPB/NACPB for bookkeeping credentials
