# Professor / Postsecondary Instructor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `professor` · **Aliases:** lecturer, adjunct, assistant professor
**Also read:** `instructional-designer`, `teacher`, `school-administrator`, `training-specialist`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Assistant Professor, Associate Professor, Professor (tenure-track), Lecturer or Senior Lecturer, Instructor, Clinical or Teaching Professor, Professor of Practice, Visiting Assistant Professor, Postdoctoral Fellow, and Adjunct or Part-Time Faculty
- Adjacent worth searching: Community College Faculty, Academic Program Director, Instructional Designer, Research Scientist, Academic Advisor, and Director of Teaching and Learning Centers

## Where the postings live
- Usually college and university HR sites, higher-education job boards, and your discipline's professional association job list; many fields run a hiring season in the fall for positions starting the next academic year
- Adjunct and visiting roles are often filled close to term start and through department contacts; many community colleges keep standing adjunct pools

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The terminal degree for the field (usually a doctorate, sometimes a master's or professional degree, especially at community colleges and for practice-based fields), a match to the advertised subfield, and teaching experience at the right level
- For research-focused roles, publications, grant history, and a research agenda that fits the department; for teaching-focused roles, courses you can teach from day one and evidence of teaching quality
- Some public colleges and accreditors ask that instructors hold graduate credits in the subject they teach; licensure in the field (nursing, law, counseling) is commonly asked for professional programs

## Story types that land
- Redesigning a course and what changed in student learning or completion
- Your research question in plain words, why it matters, and what comes next
- Mentoring a student through a thesis, project, or career step
- Service: committee work, an accreditation review, or building a program

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A curriculum vitae (CV), not a short resume: education, appointments, publications, grants, presentations, teaching (courses by title and level), service, and references; length grows with the career
- Applications commonly add a cover letter tailored to the department, a teaching statement, a research statement, a diversity or student-success statement where asked, and sample syllabi or teaching evaluations

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short first-round video or conference interview with the search committee, then a campus visit of one or two days with a job talk or research presentation, a teaching demonstration, and meetings with faculty, students, and a dean
- Expect questions on how you would teach the department's core courses, your research plans for the next few years, mentoring students, and how you'd fit the institution's mission

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $107,310 a year; most earn between $59,270 and $322,020 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 25-1071, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 123,800 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 37 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a doctoral or professional degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 25-1071). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Full-time faculty are commonly paid an annual salary on a nine- or ten-month academic contract, with summer teaching, grants, or administrative duties paid separately; some roles are on twelve-month contracts
- Adjunct and part-time faculty are usually paid per course, often without benefits, with no guarantee of courses next term; ask about the per-course rate and any course-cancellation pay
- Many public institutions and unionized faculties publish salary scales or ranges by rank; startup packages (research funds, lab space, equipment) often matter as much as salary for research roles

## What's usually negotiable
- For tenure-track offers, often open: starting salary within the band, startup funds, a reduced teaching load in early years, moving costs, summer support, start date, and credit toward tenure for prior years
- Adjunct terms tend to move less; ask about multi-course or multi-term contracts, office space, and a path to a full-time line

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: visiting or lecturer roles renewed year to year with no path to a longer contract, heavy teaching loads with research expectations, adjunct roles where courses may be cancelled late without pay, and vague startup or tenure criteria

## Verify before relying on this
- The institution's faculty handbook and union contract for rank, tenure, and pay scale; the discipline's professional association for field-specific hiring norms; any accreditor or state rules on instructor credentials for the subject

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 41200; 41210, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Faculty hiring is commonly unionized with salary scales in the collective agreement, and many roles are sessional (per-course) appointments; federal rules ask institutions to give priority to Canadian citizens and permanent residents, and many job ads say so, so check the posting and the institution's collective agreement.
