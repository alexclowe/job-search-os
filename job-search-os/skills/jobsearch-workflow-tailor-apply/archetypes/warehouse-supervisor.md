# Warehouse / Logistics Supervisor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `warehouse-supervisor` · **Aliases:** warehouse lead, operations supervisor (warehouse)
**Also read:** `warehouse-associate`, `inventory-shipping-clerk`, `logistics-supply-chain`, `operations-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Warehouse Supervisor, Shift Supervisor, Operations Supervisor, Shipping or Receiving Supervisor, Inventory Control Supervisor, Distribution Center Supervisor, Area Manager, and Warehouse Manager at the senior end
- Adjacent worth searching: Warehouse Lead, Fulfillment Supervisor, Transportation or Dock Supervisor, Inventory Control Manager, Logistics Coordinator, and Operations Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually distribution center and third-party logistics career pages, retailer and manufacturer supply-chain listings, staffing agencies, and the general job boards
- Many warehouses promote leads from within, so internal postings and referrals from current supervisors often fill roles before outside ads run

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Team size and shift you have led, the kind of operation (e-commerce fulfillment, cold storage, cross-dock, retail distribution), and the numbers you ran, such as units per hour, pick accuracy, on-time shipping, and safety incidents
- Warehouse management system experience, inventory control and cycle counts, and equipment knowledge; US rules put forklift and powered-truck training and evaluation on the employer, so screeners often ask what you are trained on rather than for an outside certificate
- Shift and peak-season availability, including nights, weekends, and mandatory overtime periods

## Story types that land
- Hitting a peak season or a surge: staffing, labour planning, and what happened to service levels
- Raising accuracy or cutting damage with a process change you can explain
- A safety fix around forklifts, docks, or racking that lowered incidents
- Coaching a struggling team member, or handling attendance and discipline fairly

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Each role names the building type, shift, headcount led, and the systems used, with one or two measured results; a short skills line covers the warehouse system, inventory methods, and equipment
- "Dynamic leader in a fast-paced environment" reads as filler; "supervised a second-shift outbound team, raised pick accuracy and cut mis-ships by fixing slotting" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the operations or site manager, commonly with a building walk and behavioral questions about attendance, safety, and missed targets
- Some employers ask you to read a productivity report or plan staffing for a sample shift

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $30.23 an hour ($62,890 a year); most earn between $20.85 and $46.23 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 53-1047). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 54,900 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 3.0% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 53-1047). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Either hourly pay or an annual salary: many first-line supervisors and leads are paid hourly and are owed overtime under federal and state rules, while others are salaried and classified exempt, so ask how the role is classified
- Night and weekend shifts commonly carry a shift differential, peak season often brings heavy overtime, and some sites add a bonus tied to productivity or safety; ask about guaranteed hours in slow seasons for hourly roles

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: the base rate or salary, shift assignment, the shift differential, sign-on bonus, and start date; overtime expectations during peak are worth pinning down before accepting
- Ask how supervisors are measured and whether productivity targets have changed recently

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: salaried supervision with long mandatory peak hours and no extra pay, targets described only as "aggressive", no mention of equipment training, and frequent re-posting of the same supervisor role

## Verify before relying on this
- The powered industrial truck pages of OSHA (the Occupational Safety and Health Administration) for forklift training rules; the Department of Labor's overtime guidance for how supervisors are classified; the employer's own posting for shift and headcount

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** 12013, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site). Statistics Canada lists warehouse supervisor, shipping supervisor and receiving supervisor among this unit group's example titles.
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Forklift training rules and overtime rules for supervisors are set provincially (federally for some transport employers), so check your province's workplace safety and employment standards rules, and use Job Bank for regional wages rather than US figures.
