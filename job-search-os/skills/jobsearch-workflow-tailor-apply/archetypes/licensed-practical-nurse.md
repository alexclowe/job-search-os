# Licensed Practical / Vocational Nurse — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `licensed-practical-nurse` · **Aliases:** LPN, LVN, licensed vocational nurse, practical nurse, RPN (Ontario only: registered practical nurse)
**Also read:** `nurse`, `nursing-assistant-caregiver`, `medical-assistant`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- LPN or LVN with the setting named: long-term care, home health, clinic or office nurse, pediatric LPN, triage LPN, private duty nurse, and charge nurse in long-term care
- Adjacent worth searching: clinic or office nurse roles in outpatient practices, MDS (Minimum Data Set) coordinator, infusion or dialysis LPN where the state allows it, and school nurse roles where the state lets LPNs fill them

## Where the postings live
- Usually long-term care and rehab facilities, home health and hospice agencies, physician practices, and health-system career sites; staffing agencies commonly fill per-diem and contract shifts
- Many facilities hire continuously, so a direct call to the director of nursing or the staffing coordinator can move faster than the online form

## What a recruiter screens for first
- An active LPN or LVN licence in the state (or a multistate compact licence), CPR or BLS, and any setting-specific certification the state requires, such as IV therapy
- The setting and shift you can work, experience with the facility's charting system (for example Epic, MEDITECH, or eClinicalWorks), and medication-pass experience

## Story types that land
- Noticing a change in a patient's condition, charting it, and escalating in time
- Running a safe medication pass on a heavy assignment, or catching a medication error before it reached the patient
- Wound care, catheter care, or IV work done well and documented clearly
- Keeping a family informed during a hard stretch, or de-escalating a difficult situation

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Licence type, state, and number near the top (as the employer asks), then certifications with expiry dates; each role names the setting, unit size or caseload, and patient population
- "Provided excellent patient care" reads as filler; "25-resident unit, two medication passes a shift, wound care and G-tube feeds" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short phone screen, then an in-person or video interview with the director or assistant director of nursing; scenario questions about a change in condition or a medication issue are common
- Some facilities add a skills check, a unit tour, or a shadow shift, and many ask early about weekend and holiday availability

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $30.96 an hour ($64,400 a year); most earn between $23.91 and $40.12 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 29-2061). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 51,800 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 3.0% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a certificate or diploma program (not a degree), the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 29-2061). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with differentials for evenings, nights, weekends, and sometimes charge duty; overtime and extra-shift incentives often matter as much as the base rate
- Agency and per-diem shifts tend to pay a higher hourly rate with fewer or no benefits; unionized facilities publish wage steps in the contract

## What's usually negotiable
- The base rate within the facility's range (often by years of experience), shift and differential eligibility, guaranteed hours, sign-on bonus and its repayment terms, and tuition help toward an RN
- Where a union scale exists, placement on the scale usually moves more than the rate itself

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: "flexible" with no set schedule, a sign-on bonus with a long clawback period, mandatory overtime language, charge duties described as a perk without extra pay, and a resident or patient count the posting won't state

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state board of nursing and the NCSBN (ncsbn.org) and its Nursys licence lookup for licence status, compact rules, and what LPNs may do in your state, including IV therapy; the employer's posting for setting requirements

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** 32101, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Practical nurses are regulated by each province (in Ontario the title is registered practical nurse, or RPN), and scope of practice varies by province; check your provincial nursing regulator before relying on a US licence or scope. In the western provinces RPN usually means registered psychiatric nurse, a different profession. Internationally educated practical nurses usually start with NNAS (outside Quebec), but check your provincial regulator first because the steps can change.
