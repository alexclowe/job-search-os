# Automotive / Diesel Technician — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `automotive-technician` · **Aliases:** auto mechanic, diesel mechanic
**Also read:** `maintenance-technician`, `hvac-technician`, `welder`, `truck-driver`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Automotive Technician, Automotive Service Technician, Auto Mechanic, Diesel Technician or Diesel Mechanic, Lube or Quick Service Technician for entry roles, Line Technician, Drivability or Diagnostic Technician, Transmission Technician, and Master or Shop Foreman roles at the senior end
- Adjacent worth searching: Fleet Technician or Fleet Mechanic, Heavy Line Technician, Heavy Equipment or Mobile Equipment Mechanic, Electric Vehicle or Hybrid Technician, Service Advisor or Service Writer, and Parts Specialist

## Where the postings live
- Usually dealership group career pages, independent and chain repair shop listings, fleet and municipal job boards, and the general job boards; trade-school and apprenticeship programs often feed entry roles
- Many shops hire by word of mouth, so walking in with a resume, or asking a service manager or parts counter you already know, can move faster than an online form

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Diagnostic range (engine performance, electrical, brakes and steering, heating and air conditioning), the makes you have worked on, and scan-tool and service-information experience; a clean driving licence is commonly screened because techs road-test vehicles
- Credentials that commonly help: ASE certifications (National Institute for Automotive Service Excellence tests, which pair a test with qualifying work experience, plus entry-level tests for students), and manufacturer training levels at dealerships
- EPA (Environmental Protection Agency) Section 609 certification: federal rules say anyone who repairs or services a vehicle air-conditioning system for payment must be trained and certified through an EPA-approved program, so shops that do A/C work usually screen for it
- Whether you bring your own tools and toolbox, and whether you are comfortable with flat-rate pay

## Story types that land
- A hard diagnosis: the symptom, what you tested first, what you ruled out, and the fix that held
- A comeback you prevented or cleaned up, and what you changed in your process
- Turning jobs efficiently on a busy day without cutting corners on safety or quality
- Explaining a repair clearly to a service advisor or customer, or training a newer tech

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A short skills block near the top (systems, makes, diagnostic tools), then certifications with the test areas you hold, then each job with the shop type and the kind of work you did
- "Expert mechanic, can fix anything" reads as filler; "line tech at a busy import shop, electrical and drivability diagnosis, ASE certified in brakes and engine performance" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short conversation with the service manager or shop foreman, sometimes followed by a shop tour and a hands-on diagnostic check or a paid working interview
- Expect questions on your diagnostic process, which systems you are strongest in, your tool set, and how you handle a comeback or a job that runs over the flag time

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $24.34 an hour ($50,620 a year); most earn between $16.66 and $39.32 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 49-3023, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 90,600 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a certificate or diploma program (not a degree), the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 49-3023). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 15% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, or flat-rate (also called flag hours): each job pays a set number of labour hours from a time guide, whatever time it actually takes, so fast techs on steady work can out-earn the clock and slow weeks or unpaid comebacks can pull pay down
- Some shops pay a mix, such as an hourly base plus a production bonus, and some flat-rate shops add a guaranteed hours minimum each week; shift differentials are more common at fleets and large service departments than at small shops
- Overtime rules vary: some dealership mechanics may be exempt from federal overtime, so check your state's rules and ask how overtime is handled at a flat-rate shop
- Federal rules generally require at least the minimum wage for every hour a non-exempt employee works, so ask how a slow flat-rate week is topped up if flagged hours fall short

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: the flat-rate or hourly rate (by certifications and experience), guaranteed hours in slow weeks, a tool allowance or help financing tools, paid manufacturer training, and which work you are dispatched
- Ask how work is dispatched between techs; who gets the good-paying jobs often moves take-home pay more than the rate itself

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a flat-rate posting with no word on guaranteed hours, "unlimited earning potential" with no typical-week example, unpaid comebacks on work you didn't do, and a shop that re-posts the same role every month

## Verify before relying on this
- ASE (ase.com) for current test areas and experience rules; the EPA's Section 609 page for vehicle air-conditioning certification; the Department of Labor and your state labour office for overtime rules at your kind of shop
