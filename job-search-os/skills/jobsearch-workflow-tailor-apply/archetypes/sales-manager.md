# Sales Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `sales-manager` · **Aliases:** regional sales manager, sales director, sales supervisor
**Also read:** `b2b-sales-rep`, `wholesale-manufacturing-sales-rep`, `marketing-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Sales Manager, Regional Sales Manager, District Sales Manager, National Sales Manager, Sales Director, VP (Vice President) of Sales, and Sales Supervisor, with the channel named (inside, field, channel, retail)
- Adjacent worth searching: Sales Operations Manager, Business Development Director, Head of Sales at smaller companies, Fractional Sales Executive, Sales Enablement or Training Manager, and Key Account Director

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and professional networks; many director and VP roles are filled through sales recruiters and executive search
- Internal promotion from top reps is common, so roles often aren't posted, and referrals from former reps and customers often surface openings

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Your team's results against quota over several years, team size, the territory and deal type, and what you did to hire, coach, and keep reps
- CRM (customer relationship management) and forecasting experience, sales process or methodology training, and whether you've carried a quota yourself; most sales manager roles need no licence, unless the team sells regulated products such as insurance or securities

## Story types that land
- Turning around a team or region that was missing its number
- Hiring and developing reps, including a rep you coached from struggling to hitting quota
- Improving forecast accuracy or the sales process
- Handling a hard people call: letting go of a low performer, or keeping a top one

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Two pages is common; each role names team size, territory, and team results by year from your own record (attainment, growth, rep retention)
- "Inspirational leader" reads as filler; "took a six-rep team from well below plan to above plan in a year, promoted two reps" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the sales VP or CEO, peers in marketing or operations, and sometimes reps who would report to you
- A 30-60-90-day plan, a pipeline or forecast review exercise, a coaching role-play, or a presentation on how you'd hit the number is common, and some employers ask for proof of past results

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $148,270 a year ($71.28 an hour); most earn between $73,170 and $290,540 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-2022, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 69,000 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-2022). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually a base salary plus variable pay: commission or an override on the team's sales, a bonus tied to team quota, or both; the base share tends to be higher than for reps
- Plans commonly include accelerators above target and sometimes caps; some managers are paid on renewals or repeat business, and managers who also sell their own accounts may earn commission on those too
- Commission or bonus paid on deals that later cancel may be clawed back, and new managers sometimes get a guaranteed bonus or draw for the first period; ask whether it is recoverable (paid back from later earnings) or non-recoverable

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: base salary, the variable target and how it's measured, guaranteed pay during the first period, team size and hiring budget, territory, title, severance, and relocation
- Ask whether you keep any personal client list or pipeline commission if you leave, how quota is set for your team, and what the non-compete says

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a manager title that is really a player-coach carrying a full personal quota, several recent managers in the same seat, team quotas far above past results, variable pay with no history of payout, and "build the team from scratch" with no hiring budget

## Verify before relying on this
- The written compensation plan for targets, caps, clawbacks, and payout after you leave; your state labor department for rules on earned commission; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada

- **NOC 2021:** 60010, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.

- Sales management isn't licensed in Canada, though teams selling insurance or securities work under provincial licensing; commission and overtime rules vary by province under employment standards, so check your province's rules and the written compensation plan.
