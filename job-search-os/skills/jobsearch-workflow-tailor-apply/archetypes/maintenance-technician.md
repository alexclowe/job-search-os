# Maintenance / Industrial Maintenance Technician — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `maintenance-technician` · **Aliases:** maintenance mechanic, millwright, facilities technician
**Also read:** `electrician`, `hvac-technician`, `welder`, `production-supervisor`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Maintenance Technician, Maintenance Mechanic, Industrial Maintenance Technician, Industrial Machinery Mechanic, Millwright, Multi-Craft or Multi-Skilled Technician, Facilities or Building Maintenance Technician, and Maintenance Lead or Planner
- Adjacent worth searching: Reliability Technician, Controls or Automation Technician, Electromechanical Technician, Field Service Technician for equipment makers, Stationary Engineer or Building Engineer, and Maintenance Supervisor

## Where the postings live
- Usually manufacturer, warehouse, hospital, property-management, and school-district career pages, the general job boards, staffing agencies for skilled trades, and union halls where the trade is organized
- Many plants hire through referrals from current technicians and through temp-to-hire agencies; community college and apprenticeship programs often feed entry roles

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Hands-on range across mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, and pneumatic systems; reading prints and schematics; programmable logic controller (PLC) troubleshooting for industrial roles; and welding or fabrication where the posting names it
- Credentials that commonly help: a completed apprenticeship or journeyman card, EPA Section 608 refrigerant certification for HVAC work, a boiler or stationary engineer licence where the city or state has one, lockout/tagout and forklift training, and an OSHA safety card; some states licence electrical work separately
- The shift you can work, on-call availability, and whether you bring your own tools

## Story types that land
- A breakdown you diagnosed under pressure, what you checked first, and how long the line was down
- A preventive or predictive maintenance habit that cut repeat failures
- A safety catch: a lockout gap, a guard missing, a hazard you reported and fixed
- Training a newer tech or improving a work-order or parts system

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A skills block near the top by system (electrical, mechanical, fluid power, controls) with the equipment and controls brands you've worked on, then certifications, then each job with the site type and what you maintained
- "Handy and hardworking" reads as filler; "troubleshot conveyor drives and PLC faults on a three-shift packaging line, kept work orders in the maintenance system" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the maintenance manager, commonly with a hands-on or written skills test (reading a schematic, a meter test, a troubleshooting scenario) or a walk through the plant
- Expect questions on your troubleshooting method, which equipment you've owned, lockout/tagout, and working nights, weekends, or on-call

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $23.84 an hour ($49,590 a year); most earn between $17.00 and $37.11 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 49-9071, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 193,100 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 49-9071). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with shift differentials for second, third, or weekend shifts; overtime is common and often a large part of total pay, so ask how much is typical and whether it's voluntary or mandatory
- Some employers pay extra for skill levels or certifications (a pay-for-skills ladder), plus on-call pay; union plants publish wage steps in the contract
- Ask about guaranteed hours in any temp-to-hire or agency placement, and about tool allowances

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open: the starting rate or step (by skills test and years of experience), shift assignment, tool allowance, training or certification pay, and sign-on bonus; union scales tend to move by classification rather than individual negotiation
- Ask how skill-level raises are earned and how often reviews happen

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one "maintenance tech" expected to cover electrical, HVAC, plumbing, and machine repair across many buildings alone, mandatory overtime with no limit, no mention of lockout/tagout training, "must have own tools" with no allowance, and the same role re-posted often

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state or city licensing office for electrical, boiler, or refrigeration licences (CareerOneStop's License Finder lists them; link, don't copy); the EPA for Section 608 certification; the Department of Labor's apprenticeship site for registered programs

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** 73201; 72400, matched by title by the maintainers (not an official concordance; check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site).
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Industrial mechanic (millwright) certification is voluntary across Canada (per Job Bank), though employers commonly prefer a provincial certificate or the Red Seal endorsement; related trades such as industrial electrician can be compulsory in some provinces, so check your provincial apprenticeship authority for your trade.
- Stationary or building engineer roles in Canada usually mean a provincially certified power engineer (called an operating engineer in Ontario, certified through TSSA; in Alberta, ABSA issues the power engineer certificates of competency), and the certificate class often decides the plant size you can run; check your province's boiler and pressure vessel authority.
