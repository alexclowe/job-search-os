# Retail Sales Associate / Cashier — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `retail-sales-associate` · **Aliases:** cashier, sales associate, store associate
**Also read:** `retail-store-manager`, `customer-service-rep`, `bank-teller`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Sales Associate, Retail Sales Associate, Cashier, Store Associate, Customer Assistant, Sales Consultant, Stock Associate, and Key Holder or Shift Lead
- Adjacent worth searching: commissioned sales roles in furniture, appliances, electronics, mattresses, jewelry, or cars; Visual Merchandiser; Customer Service Desk Associate; Pharmacy or Photo Clerk; and Assistant Store Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually the store's own career site with a quick-apply form, the general job boards, and "now hiring" signs in the store; many chains hire heavily before the holiday season
- Walking in and asking for the manager, or applying through a hiring event, often moves faster than an online form, and referrals from current staff commonly help

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Availability, above all evenings, weekends, and holidays, and how many hours you want
- Cash-handling and register experience, comfort talking to customers, and for commissioned roles, a sales history in plain numbers from your own record
- Some jobs have extra screening: many retailers run a background check, and car sales roles in some states need a salesperson licence from the state, so check your state

## Story types that land
- Helping a customer find the right product, or turning a complaint into a sale
- Handling cash accurately and balancing the drawer
- Hitting a sales, credit-card sign-up, or add-on goal from your own record
- Keeping the floor stocked and organized during a rush or a big delivery

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page or the online form alone; list each store with its type, your duties (register, stocking, sales), and any results from your own record
- "People person" reads as filler; "closing cashier and key holder, balanced drawers, trained new cashiers, top in store for loyalty sign-ups" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short interview with a store or department manager, sometimes at a group hiring event, with questions about availability, customer situations, and teamwork
- Many chains add an online assessment before the interview, and commissioned roles may include a role-play such as "sell me this"

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $17.03 an hour ($35,410 a year); most earn between $13.08 and $23.02 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 41-2031, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 1,041,900 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** no formal credential, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 41-2031). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly; some stores pay a differential for overnight stocking or holiday shifts, and some add small bonuses for sales or sign-up goals
- Some retail sales jobs (furniture, appliances, electronics, jewelry, cars) pay commission on sales, often with an hourly base or a draw against commission; ask whether any draw is recoverable (paid back from later commission) or non-recoverable
- Commission on returned or cancelled sales is commonly charged back from later pay, so ask how chargebacks work and for how long after the sale
- Overtime: hourly retail workers usually earn overtime after a full workweek, but a federal rule exempts some commissioned retail employees from overtime when commissions make up most of their pay and their rate is high enough, so ask whether that applies to you
- Guaranteed hours are uncommon; part-time schedules often change week to week, and some cities and states have scheduling laws requiring advance notice or pay for last-minute changes

## What's usually negotiable
- The starting hourly rate often moves little at entry level, but the number of hours, the shift, and a key holder or lead role may be open
- For commissioned roles, the commission rate, the base or draw, and who keeps your client list if you leave (common in luxury and furniture selling) may be open to discussion; ask for the pay plan in writing

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: commission-only pay with no base, "unlimited earning potential" with no typical figures, draws that get paid back if you leave, "open availability" demanded for part-time hours, and sales quotas tied to discipline

## Verify before relying on this
- The Department of Labor's retail commission overtime fact sheet and your state labor department for overtime and scheduling rules; your state motor vehicle dealer board for car sales licences; the written pay or commission plan before accepting
