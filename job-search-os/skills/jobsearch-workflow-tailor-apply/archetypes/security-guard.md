# Security Guard — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `security-guard` · **Aliases:** security officer, loss prevention
**Also read:** `police-officer`, `retail-sales-associate`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Security Guard, Security Officer, Unarmed or Armed Security Officer, Patrol Officer, Campus or Hospital Security Officer, Concierge or Front Desk Security, and Loss Prevention Officer or Associate
- Adjacent worth searching: Security Supervisor or Site Supervisor, Alarm or Security Monitoring Operator, Event Security, Access Control Officer, and Transportation Security Screener

## Where the postings live
- Usually the general job boards, contract security company career sites (which hire continuously and staff many sites), and in-house security roles at hospitals, universities, hotels, and large buildings
- Contract security companies often hold open hiring events and hire fast, while in-house roles tend to pay more and move slower, so it's commonly worth applying to both

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The state guard licence or registration where your state asks for one, or willingness to get it: often a short state-approved training course, a background check with fingerprints, and an application to the state licensing agency; armed roles usually add firearms training and a separate permit
- A clean background, availability for nights, weekends, and holidays, and reliable transport; some sites ask for first aid and CPR
- Report writing, calm communication, and any experience with access control, camera systems, or patrol software

## Story types that land
- Handling a tense situation by talking someone down rather than escalating
- Spotting something wrong on patrol (an open door, a hazard, a stranger) and reporting it clearly
- Writing an incident report that held up later
- Responding to a medical emergency or evacuation and working with police or paramedics

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Guard licence and state, armed permit if any, and first aid or CPR near the top; for each job, the site type (hospital, retail, office, event), the post duties, and the systems you used
- "Protected property" reads as filler; "overnight patrol of a hospital campus, access control and camera monitoring, wrote incident reports" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a short interview with a site supervisor or recruiter, with scenario questions such as "what would you do if someone refused to leave"
- Many employers add a background check, a drug screen, and licence verification; in-house and armed roles may include a panel interview or a firearms qualification

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $18.28 an hour ($38,020 a year); most earn between $14.52 and $28.29 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 33-9032). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 156,000 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 0.9% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 33-9032). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually hourly, with differentials at some employers for overnight shifts, armed posts, or supervisory duties
- Security guards are usually non-exempt, so overtime pay applies after a full workweek under federal law, and overtime is common where sites are short-staffed; some states add daily overtime rules
- Guaranteed hours vary: contract security often offers part-time or on-call shifts, so ask about minimum weekly hours and how often posts change
- Unionized guard jobs and some government contracts publish wage rates, and some contracts set a prevailing wage for the site

## What's usually negotiable
- The hourly rate often moves with an armed permit, supervisory duties, or specialized sites; shift choice and a steady post may also be open
- Ask who pays for licence renewals, uniforms, and firearms training, since those costs tend to vary by employer

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: fees for training or uniforms deducted from pay, "no licence needed" in a state that licenses guards, constant mandatory overtime, and unarmed posts that seem to expect use of force

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state's security guard licensing agency, or CareerOneStop's License Finder, for guard and armed licence rules; the Department of Labor's overtime pages and your state labor department

- Many provinces license security guards (Ontario, British Columbia and others), Quebec requires a permit from the Bureau de la sécurité privée for security officers, and armed guard work is rare in Canada and needs a federal firearms licence plus an Authorization to Carry; check your province's private security licensing office and Job Bank's requirements before applying.
