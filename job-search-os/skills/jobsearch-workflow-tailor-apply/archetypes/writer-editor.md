# Writer / Copywriter / Editor — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `writer-editor` · **Aliases:** copywriter, content writer, technical writer, editor
**Also read:** `pr-specialist`, `marketing-specialist`, `social-media-manager`, `instructional-designer`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Copywriter, Content Writer, Content Marketing Writer, Technical Writer, Editor, Copy Editor, Managing Editor, Senior Writer, and Content Strategist or Content Lead
- Adjacent worth searching: SEO (search engine optimization) Writer, UX Writer or Content Designer, Proposal Writer, Grant Writer, Documentation Specialist, Communications Specialist, and Proofreader

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages (marketing, product, and documentation teams), agencies, publishers, and media outlets, plus the general job boards and writing-specific boards
- Much of the work is freelance and comes through referrals, past editors and clients, freelance marketplaces, and creative staffing agencies, so a portfolio site and a network often matter more than any one posting

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Writing samples that match the job: ads and landing pages for copywriters, articles for content writers, manuals or help content for technical writers, edited pieces with your changes for editors
- The subject matter and audience you know (for example software, healthcare, finance), the style guide you've used, and tools named in the posting such as a content management system or documentation tools
- A degree in English, journalism, or communications is commonly listed, but samples usually count for more

## Story types that land
- A piece you wrote or edited and the result it drove: sign-ups, fewer support tickets, a published story, or a clearer policy
- Turning a hard technical or legal topic into something a general reader understood
- Working with subject experts, legal reviewers, or editors to get a piece approved on time
- Building or cleaning up a style guide, content calendar, or documentation set

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A one-page resume with a portfolio link near the top; for each role, the content types, audiences, and results from your own record; the resume itself is treated as a writing sample, so tight and error-free matters
- "Wordsmith" and "passionate storyteller" read as filler; "wrote the product help center and cut repeat support questions" reads as credible once your own figures are in

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then a conversation with the content or editorial lead about your samples, then a round with the teams you'd write for
- A writing or editing test is common; a short test is typical, while a long unpaid assignment that looks like real work is worth questioning

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $76,910 a year ($36.98 an hour); most earn between $44,310 and $139,870 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 27-3043, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 23,000 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 3 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a bachelor's degree, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 27-3043). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 65% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Staff roles usually pay an annual salary; contract roles through agencies tend to pay hourly
- Freelance writers and editors are commonly paid per project, per word, per hour, or on a monthly retainer; a freelance rate has to cover self-employment tax, health insurance, tools, and unbilled time spent pitching and doing admin, so it isn't comparable to a salaried rate at face value
- Many writers combine a staff job with freelance work, so check the employment contract for rules on outside work and who owns what you write

## What's usually negotiable
- For staff roles, the salary, title (writer vs senior vs lead), remote days, and a training or tools allowance tend to move
- For freelance work, the rate, the number of revision rounds, kill fees if a project is cancelled, payment timing, and the rights you're granting are often worth settling in writing

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: pay per word far below local norms, "exposure" instead of pay, unpaid test pieces that look like publishable content, and a writer role that also expects design, video, and social media management

## Verify before relying on this
- Writers' and editors' associations for published rate surveys and sample contracts; the contract's rights and payment clauses; your tax adviser or the IRS small business pages for self-employment tax if you freelance

- Writing and editing titles aren't regulated in Canada; freelancers must register for GST/HST with the CRA once their taxable sales pass the small-supplier threshold (and may register voluntarily before that), and roles serving Quebec or federal audiences often ask for French or bilingual English and French skills, so check the posting and the CRA's guidance.
