# B2B Sales Representative / Account Executive — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `b2b-sales-rep` · **Aliases:** account executive, SDR/BDR, inside sales, outside sales
**Also read:** `wholesale-manufacturing-sales-rep`, `sales-manager`, `customer-success-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Account Executive, Sales Representative, Business Development Representative (BDR), Sales Development Representative (SDR), Inside Sales Representative, Outside Sales Representative, Enterprise or Mid-Market Account Executive, and National Sales Executive
- Adjacent worth searching: Account Manager (existing customers), Customer Success Manager, Partner or Channel Manager, Solutions Consultant or Sales Engineer for technical sellers, and Business Development Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages, the general job boards, and professional networks; sales-specific job boards and communities often carry software and services roles
- Many roles are filled through referrals from other reps and sales leaders, and specialist sales recruiters commonly place experienced account executives

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Results against quota in plain numbers from your own record, the deal size and sales cycle you're used to, and who you sold to (small businesses, mid-market, enterprise, a specific industry)
- Tools such as a CRM (customer relationship management system) like Salesforce or HubSpot, and prospecting or call tools; most B2B sales roles need no licence, though selling regulated products such as insurance or securities may

## Story types that land
- A deal you found, worked, and closed, including how you handled the main objection
- Building pipeline from scratch through cold outreach or a new market
- Recovering a stalled or lost deal
- Expanding an existing customer to more users, products, or sites

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One page early career, two for experienced; each role names the product, customer segment, deal size, and quota results year by year from your own record (attainment, rank on the team, awards)
- "Crushed quota" reads as filler; "reached quota in six of eight quarters selling to mid-market manufacturers" reads as credible when the figures are yours; mark any number you can't confirm and fill it in before sending

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, then interviews with the hiring manager and peers; a mock discovery call, a cold-call or email exercise, or a full mock pitch to a panel is common for account executive roles
- Some employers ask for a 30-60-90-day plan or proof of past quota results such as a W-2 or commission statements, and SDR interviews often focus on persistence and handling rejection

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $69,990 a year ($33.65 an hour); most earn between $37,980 and $148,840 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 41-3091). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 115,700 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 2.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 41-3091). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually a base salary plus commission, described as on-target earnings (OTE: what you'd earn at exactly full quota) and a pay mix, the split between base and variable pay; SDR roles often have a higher base share, account executive roles a higher commission share
- Commission plans commonly include accelerators above quota, and sometimes caps; some plans pay commission on renewals or expansions, often at a lower rate than new business
- Commission paid upfront is commonly clawed back if the customer cancels or fails to pay within a set window, and new reps may get a draw or guaranteed commission during ramp-up; ask whether a draw is recoverable (paid back from later commission) or non-recoverable

## What's usually negotiable
- Often open to discussion: base salary, OTE and the pay mix, quota and ramp time, guaranteed pay during ramp-up, territory or named accounts, title, and sign-on bonus
- Ask what share of reps hit quota last year, whether commission on deals in your pipeline is paid if you leave, and how territory changes affect your quota
- Worth asking in writing: who keeps your named accounts and client relationships if you leave, and what any non-solicit or non-compete covers

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: OTE with no base stated, quotas few reps reach, territories that change every quarter, commission caps, "commission only" framed as uncapped opportunity, and very high turnover on the team

## Verify before relying on this
- The written commission plan for quota, clawbacks, caps, and what happens to earned commission after you leave; your state labor department for rules on paying earned commission; the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook for how the field is changing

## In Canada
<!-- canada:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **NOC 2021:** possibly 64101. This is an uncertain title match by the maintainers, not an official concordance, so check the unit group's definition on Statistics Canada's NOC site first.
- **Outlook and wages:** Job Bank publishes a three-year outlook and wage ranges by NOC and region; check your province there rather than using US figures.
<!-- canada:end -->
- Business-to-business sales roles usually aren't licensed in Canada, but commission, overtime, and vacation pay on commission vary by province under employment standards, and national roles often value French; check your province's employment standards and the written commission plan.
- "Engineer" is a protected title in every province and territory (Alberta exempts only the title "software engineer"; per Engineers Canada and APEGA), so using a sales engineer title without a provincial engineering licence can breach the law, and Canadian postings often use another title for this role (per Engineers Canada); check your provincial engineering regulator before using it.
