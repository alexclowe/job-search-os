# Nurse — job-search archetype

**Slug:** `nurse` · **Aliases:** RN, registered nurse, nurse practitioner (NP), LPN/LVN (adjacent), staff nurse, charge nurse
**Also read:** none

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Staff Nurse / Registered Nurse (unit named: med-surg, ICU, ED, L&D, peds, oncology, periop), Charge Nurse, Nurse Manager, Clinical Nurse Specialist, Nurse Educator, Case Manager, Utilization Review Nurse, Infection Prevention Nurse, Informatics Nurse, Nurse Practitioner (specialty named)
- Adjacent worth searching: Clinical Documentation Specialist, Quality/Patient Safety Nurse, Telehealth/Triage Nurse, Occupational Health Nurse, Legal Nurse Consultant, Clinical Research Nurse

## Where the postings live
- Health-system career sites (most volume), hospital association boards, specialty-society job boards, travel/contract agencies for short assignments, staffing agencies, and the general boards
- Many unit openings are filled through internal transfer or referral before they are posted externally; a recruiter contact at the system matters

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Active licence in the state (or compact licence), BLS/ACLS/PALS or the unit's required certifications, specialty certification where the unit expects it, degree level (many acute-care systems prefer or require a BSN), and years in the *same* unit type
- EHR experience by name (Epic, Cerner/Oracle Health, Meditech) is often a filter; shift and unit fit is asked before skills

## Story types that land
- A patient-safety catch: what you noticed, what you did, what it prevented
- Handoff, throughput, or documentation improvements on your unit (a process you changed, not a metric you invented)
- Precepting, charge, or committee work that shows scope beyond your assignment
- A hard family conversation or de-escalation handled well

## Resume conventions for this profession
- Licence and certifications near the top with numbers/expiry where the employer expects them; unit type, bed count, and patient population per role; one page for early career, two for experienced
- "Provided compassionate care" reads as filler; "12-bed ICU, 1:2 ratio, vents and drips" reads as credible. Keep the specifics; drop the adjectives

## Red flags in postings
- "Must be flexible with floating" plus no unit named, unrealistic ratios stated as a positive, sign-on bonus with an unusually long clawback, "self-scheduling" paired with mandatory overtime language

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state board of nursing and the NCSBN for licence and compact status; the certifying body (ANCC, AACN, etc.) for credential rules; the employer's own posting for unit requirements
