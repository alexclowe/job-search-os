# Customer Success Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `customer-success-manager` · **Aliases:** account manager, client success, implementation manager
**Also read:** `b2b-sales-rep`, `customer-service-rep`, `project-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Customer Success Manager (CSM), Senior or Enterprise CSM, Customer Success Lead, and Director of Customer Success
- Adjacent worth searching: Account Manager, Client Success Manager, Implementation or Onboarding Manager, Renewals Manager, Technical Account Manager, and Customer Experience Manager

## Where the postings live
- Usually company career pages and the general job boards; the role is most common at software and subscription companies, so startup and tech boards often list many
- Many roles are filled through referrals and internal moves from support, account management, or implementation, and some are posted under account manager titles

## What a recruiter screens for first
- The size and type of accounts you managed (number of customers, small business vs enterprise), described in words you can back up, and whether you owned renewals or expansion
- Retention results you can explain: renewals, churn (customers who leave), adoption, and customer health scores
- Tools named in the posting, such as Salesforce or HubSpot, and customer success platforms; industry knowledge often counts as much as tools; customer success certificates from training providers exist but are rarely a deciding factor

## Story types that land
- An at-risk customer you kept: the warning signs, what you did, and the outcome
- An onboarding or adoption push that got customers using more of the product
- An expansion or upsell you found by understanding the customer's goals, not by pushing
- Feedback you carried back to product or support that led to a fix

## Resume conventions for this profession
- One to two pages; each role names the book you managed (customer count or segment) and results such as retention or expansion in terms you can back up
- "Passionate about customer delight" reads as filler; "ran quarterly reviews with my largest accounts and caught two at-risk renewals early enough to save them" reads as credible

## How interviews usually run
- Often a recruiter screen, a hiring-manager interview, then a panel with sales, support, or product partners
- Many employers add a role play or presentation: a mock business review with a customer, handling an unhappy client, or a plan for an at-risk account

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, nearest occupation only:** there is no federal occupation code for this role, so this is Sales Representatives of Services, Except Advertising, Insurance, Financial Services, and Travel, a rough guide at best: median $69,990 a year ($33.65 an hour); most earn between $37,980 and $148,840 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 41-3091, the largest of the occupations this archetype covers). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings, nearest occupations:** about 214,400 a year projected for 2025–35 across the 2 occupations it covers (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 41-3091). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary; many employers add variable pay tied to renewal, retention, or expansion targets, paid quarterly or yearly, and some treat senior CSMs almost like account managers with a larger variable share
- The figures above are for the nearest federal occupations, not for customer success managers as such; compare them with posted ranges for the exact title and company size
- Ask what the variable pay is measured on, whether it is capped, whether it can be reduced if a customer later cancels, and how the targets have paid out for the team in recent years

## What's usually negotiable
- Base salary within the band, the split between base and variable pay, the book of accounts you are assigned, title or level, remote days, and start date tend to move
- Ask whether targets are set by you, your manager, or the company; unrealistic targets often matter more than the headline variable figure

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: a customer success role that is really a sales quota in disguise, a very large account list with no support, "fix churn" with no say over product or pricing, and variable pay described only as "uncapped"

## Verify before relying on this
- The employer's posting and the offer letter's variable-pay plan (get it in writing); published salary ranges where pay-transparency laws apply; talk with current CSMs at the company about how targets usually work
