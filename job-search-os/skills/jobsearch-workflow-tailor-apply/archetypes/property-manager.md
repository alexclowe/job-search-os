# Property / Community Association Manager — job-search archetype

<!-- uses: onet -->

**Slug:** `property-manager` · **Aliases:** leasing manager, HOA manager, community manager (real estate)
**Also read:** `real-estate-agent`, `office-manager`, `construction-manager`

## Target titles (and the adjacent ones)
- Property Manager, Assistant Property Manager, Community Manager, Apartment Manager, Commercial Property Manager, Community Association Manager, Leasing Manager, and Regional or Portfolio Manager
- Adjacent worth searching: Leasing Consultant or Leasing Agent (a common entry point), Resident or Onsite Manager, Lease Administrator, Facilities Manager, and Asset Manager for people who like the financial side

## Where the postings live
- Usually property management company and apartment owner career pages, the general job boards, and apartment, commercial real estate, and community association industry boards
- Many roles are filled by promoting leasing staff and assistant managers, so a leasing job at a firm you like is often a practical way in

## What a recruiter screens for first
- Licensing: many states treat managing rentals for someone else as real estate brokerage, so property managers there may need a real estate licence or to work under a licensed broker, though on-site staff employed directly by the owner are exempt in some states; a few states have a separate property management licence, and several license community association managers on their own track, so check your state's real estate commission
- Industry credentials such as those from the apartment, real estate management, or community association industry groups are usually optional but often mentioned in postings
- The property type and size you've run (units or square footage from your own record), occupancy and rent-collection results, budget ownership, and the property management software named in the posting (for example Yardi, AppFolio, or RealPage)

## Story types that land
- Raising occupancy or cutting turnover: what you changed and what happened
- Handling a big repair, a vendor problem, or an emergency at the property and keeping residents informed
- Building or sticking to an operating budget, including a cost you cut or a capital project you managed
- A difficult resident or board situation you settled fairly and within the lease or the association's rules

## Resume conventions for this profession
- A short summary with licence and property types near the top; for each role, the portfolio (type, units or square footage, number of sites), who owned it, and results from your own record such as occupancy, collections, or renewals
- "Oversaw all aspects of property operations" reads as filler; "managed a garden-style community, ran the budget, led leasing and maintenance staff, raised renewals" reads as credible once your own figures are in

## How interviews usually run
- Often a phone screen, then an interview with the regional or portfolio manager, sometimes with a property tour; scenario questions about a late-paying resident, an emergency repair, or an angry board member are common
- Some firms ask you to read a property's financial report or build a quick budget, and leasing-focused roles may include a mock tour

## How pay is usually structured
<!-- facts:start (generated from the evidence pack by scripts/job-search-os/render-archetype-facts.py; do not edit by hand) -->
- **US pay, for comparison:** median $69,990 a year ($33.65 an hour); most earn between $41,010 and $139,680 a year (BLS OEWS May 2025, SOC 11-9141). Ten percent earn less than the low figure and ten percent more than the high one.
- **What those pay figures cover:** employees only, not the self-employed; they include commissions, production bonuses and tips, and leave out overtime, shift differentials and non-production bonuses (BLS OEWS technical note).
- **Openings:** about 36,900 a year projected for 2025–35; employment projected to grow 3.7% over the decade (BLS Projections 2025–35).
- **Typical entry education:** a high school diploma or equivalent, the level BLS lists as typical for entry (BLS Projections 2025–35, SOC 11-9141). Individual employers may ask for more or less.
- **Self-employed:** about 31% of people in this work are self-employed, and the pay figures above leave them out (BLS Projections 2025–35).
<!-- facts:end -->
- Usually an annual salary, often with a bonus tied to occupancy, collections, renewals, or budget results; leasing managers and agents commonly earn a commission or leasing bonus per signed lease on top of base pay
- Leasing commissions may be clawed back or charged back if the lease falls through or the resident leaves early, so ask how that works
- Renewal bonuses are common in apartment management, paid when a resident renews
- Onsite roles sometimes include a free or reduced-rent apartment, which may be treated as part of pay, so check how it's taxed and what happens to it if you leave the job
- If you manage owner accounts at a firm, the owner clients usually stay with the firm when you leave, and non-solicit terms may apply

## What's usually negotiable
- The base salary, the bonus plan's targets, the leasing commission rate, the housing benefit, and licence or credential costs tend to move
- Ask for the bonus and commission plan in writing, including clawback terms and what happens to earned bonuses if you leave mid-year

## Red flags in postings
- Often worth a second look: one manager for several distant sites, "on call" with no mention of rotation or pay, a housing benefit presented as most of the pay, and "assistant manager" duties that match the manager's job

## Verify before relying on this
- Your state's real estate commission for whether managing rentals needs a licence; your state's community association manager licensing board where one exists; the employer's bonus and housing terms in writing

- Licensing varies by province: in British Columbia, rental and strata property managers are licensed by BCFSA, in Alberta condominium managers are licensed through RECA, and Ontario licenses condominium managers through the CMRAO; check your provincial real estate or condo regulator before relying on a US licence.
