# Your Jobs — Job Search AI Operating System

Your **command center** is home base. Say **"open my command center"** (or "what should I work on") in any conversation inside your Project. It shows five jobs as cards, a one-line summary of active applications, interviews this week, offers in play, and follow-ups due, and runs whichever job you pick. It also keeps **Your Search This Week** up to date in Artifacts: the same numbers at a glance, this week's interviews, what's due in the next seven days, and your three moves for the week.

**First time?** Pick **Try it on a sample** — pick a nurse moving to a clinic job (paid hourly) or a data engineer (paid a salary), and it tailors one application in about two minutes: the check card (is it real, the pay, the fit, who you know there), a tailored resume and cover letter, the posting terms that made it in, and an Application Board row, so you see exactly what you'll get before you paste anything real.

**Connecting your tools is optional.** Say **"connect my tools"** to link Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Drive — **personal accounts only, never an employer's.** Connected, jobs put replies and notes in your Drafts folder, interview holds and focus blocks on your calendar, and tailored files in a per-company Drive folder. Not connected, you get the same work copy-paste-ready, with files saved in your Project. **Nothing is ever sent, submitted, accepted, or declined without your click.**

Every job keeps a **living tracker** — an Application Board, a Story Bank, an Offer Tracker, a Weekly Search Log, and a People board for who you know where — so the next job picks up where the last one left off. Find them anytime in the **Artifacts** section of the sidebar.

**The Application Board is a board you can move.** Each stage is a column (Interested, Applied, Screening, Interviewing, Onsite, Offer, Closed), and every card says its stage in words, not just colour. When the board can save moves, each card has a **Stage** menu: move a card, and the next job you run picks the move up, fills in the date, and tells you in one line. To change anything else on a card, tell Claude. A board that saves moves stays private to your organization (no public link), and anyone you share it with as a viewer can see it but can't move cards. Where the board can't save moves, say "move Northwind to Interviewing" and the job does it. A spreadsheet copy of the board is kept either way.

Under everything: **never below your minimum pay**, and **nothing goes on a resume that your own record can't back**.

## 1. Tailor & apply

**When to use it:** every time a posting is worth more than a glance.
**Say:** "tailor and apply" · "apply to this job" · "here's a posting"
**Give it:** the posting (text or link), your resume (the master in your Project, or paste it — no resume at all? it offers to build one from scratch in about ten minutes), how you're applying (site, recruiter, referral, email), and anything about this one you'd rather get ahead of.
**You get:** **one check card first**, before any drafting:
- **Is this real?** — the caution signals official consumer-protection guidance names for job scams (pay to start, a check to send back, ID or bank details before an interview, an unexpected text about a job you never applied for), plus softer signs a posting may not be an open role, and how to check. Signals, never verdicts.
- **Pay** — your minimum against the posted pay, like with like (hourly against hourly, salary against salary, a commission role's base and realistic total against yours).
- **Fit** — strong, partial, or stretch, with the two biggest gaps, and whether each is a real gap or just missing from your resume.
- **People** — who you know there, from your People board or your LinkedIn connections export.

Then one choice: **tailor it**, **skip it**, or **get the intro first** (it drafts the intro request for you to send). After that, a **tailored resume (Word)** that leads with what you did and what came of it, in the posting's vocabulary where your record honestly supports it, with a change log; a **cover letter (Word, 250–350 words)** that couldn't be sent anywhere else; the **posting terms** your resume now uses — and the ones your record can't support yet, as questions ("Did you do X? Tell me and I'll add it") that never get added on their own; **application-form answers** (why us, why this job, desired pay in your pay unit, how you heard) — never the voluntary demographic questions; and a short **application note** for a referral or hiring manager.
**It knows your line of work:** name your profession in setup and it reads the built-in guide for it (108 professions and trades): the titles hiring teams use, what a recruiter screens for, how pay is usually structured. Nothing from the guide goes on your resume unless your own record backs it.
**Where it lands:** a per-company folder in Drive with both files; the note as a Gmail draft; the row on your **Application Board**. Otherwise the files in your Project and the note paste-ready.
**It never:** invents a figure, title, or date (every claim traces to your resume or profile — anything else becomes `[confirm]`), adds a posting term you haven't told it you have, overwrites your master resume, mentions pay in the letter, answers a voluntary self-ID question, or drafts into an employer's inbox.

## 2. Work my inbox

**When to use it:** twice a week, or whenever the inbox has piled up.
**Say:** "work my inbox" · "reply to this recruiter" · "they want to schedule a call" · "I got a rejection"
**Give it:** the messages — pasted, or "check my inbox" with Gmail connected (it looks for recruiters, hiring teams, and anyone on your board, shows you what it found, and you confirm before anything is used) — and the window.
**You get:** every message sorted (outreach · scheduling · next-round news · rejection · offer), a **reply drafted** for each one you choose in your voice — interested replies lead with a win and ask the three qualifying questions before a call; declines are specific and kind; scheduling confirms the slot you picked — a two-line **rejection read** for any rejection from a human, and your board moved to the right stage.
**Where it lands:** replies as Gmail drafts in the original thread; confirmed interviews on your calendar with a prep hold the day before; the **Application Board** updated; any offer added to the **Offer Tracker**.
**It never:** sends, accepts, declines, or promises anything; gives salary history; proposes a time it hasn't checked; or reads an employer's mailbox.
**Make it automatic:** say **"schedule this"** for Tuesdays and Fridays. A scheduled run works from messages saved in your Project and prepares replies for your review — it doesn't read your inbox or draft into Gmail on its own.

## 3. Prep for an interview

**When to use it:** as soon as a round is on the calendar.
**Say:** "prep me for an interview" · "recruiter screen Tuesday" · "hiring manager round tomorrow" · "final round with the VP"
**Give it:** which application (picked from your board), which round, who you'll meet if you know, and what worries you about this one. If your Story Bank has fewer than five stories, it builds it first from your wins.
**You get:** a **company brief first** (one page, every fact with a source and a date — it says so plainly if it can't search the web, and asks you to paste their about page instead), which feeds your "why us" answer and the questions you ask; then a **prep brief (Word, 2–4 pages)** for that round only — what it decides, their three to five likely worries with the story that answers each, the questions to expect for a recruiter screen, a hiring-manager round, a panel loop, a final round, or a technical exercise, **your answers** in your voice built from named Story Bank entries, the paragraph that gets ahead of your what a hiring manager might question, five questions to ask back, the honest answer to "how do you use AI in your work", and a half-page **day-of card**. Then, if you want it, a **practice round**: one question at a time, in the format your profession's interviews usually take (behavioral for everyone, clinical scenarios for nurses, a demo lesson and panel for teachers, a sell-me-this role-play for sales and lending, technical plus behavioral for tech), five lines of feedback after each answer, and a debrief that adds new stories to your Story Bank.
**Where it lands:** the company brief and the prep brief in the company's Drive folder; a prep hold on your calendar if you want one; the **Story Bank** and **Application Board** updated; during a practice round, the **Practice Round** view: the question you're on with an answer timer, and how many of your answers used a Story Bank story.
**It never:** invents a story — a question with no matching story gets "no story yet, pick from these" — or gives salary history in the compensation answer.

## 4. Follow up & negotiate

**When to use it:** the day after any interview, when a process goes quiet, or when an offer lands.
**Say:** "write my thank-you note" · "I haven't heard back" · "I got an offer" · "help me counter"
**Give it:** which application and what happened; for an offer, the written terms and the two things you want most.
**You get:** after an interview, **thank-you notes** per interviewer (no two alike) and a **follow-up cadence** with dates — two touches, each with one new piece of information, never a third nudge; for an offer, an **offer worksheet (Excel)** with each component as stated in its own unit (hourly rate and differentials, salary and bonus, base or draw and commission, or salary-schedule placement), the annual value as formulas, any competing offer side by side, and the **salary check** in the first line; a **counter script** with the exact words for the call and the email; a short **decision memo** against your must-haves; and a **decline note** only if you choose it.
**Where it lands:** notes as Gmail drafts in the interview thread; the worksheet in the company's Drive folder; follow-up dates and the decision deadline on your calendar; the **Offer Tracker** and **Application Board** updated; with an offer in hand, the **Offer Comparison**, your open offers side by side (base, differentials or bonus or commission, schedule, benefits, the salary check), every figure as the company stated it and "not stated" where you still need to ask.
**It never:** accepts, declines, resigns, or gives notice for you; asks for less than your minimum pay; invents a market rate (figures only with a source you supplied); or answers an equity-tax, draw-recovery, union-contract, or non-compete question — those get "worth a professional's eyes."

## 5. Run my weekly review

**When to use it:** Monday morning, or Sunday evening for the week ahead.
**Say:** "run my weekly review" · "plan my search week" · "how is my search going"
**Give it:** which of last week's three moves got done, how you're feeling about it (steady, stalled, stretched, close), your hours this week, and any new targets.
**You get:** a **week plan (Word)** — last week in five lines from the board, the board honestly (anything untouched for ten business days named as stale with a decision each: nudge, park, or close), **where your search stalls** — reply rate by source (referral, job board, recruiter reached out, applied direct) and the stage where applications stop (no reply, screens that stall, interviews without a final, finals without an offer), with one fix for that stage; under 10 applications it says "too few to tell yet" instead of guessing — **exactly three moves** each with "done looks like", hours, and a calendar block, a **not-doing list**, up to five **outreach drafts** in your voice for the moves that need them, and a Monday note that matches the mood you picked without pretending.
**Where it lands:** three focus blocks on your calendar at the times you picked; the plan in a Drive "Weekly reviews" folder; outreach as Gmail drafts; the **Weekly Search Log**; the **Search Funnel**, a read-only view of the same numbers (each step as a bar, per source, rebuilt from the board every time). Otherwise the plan in your Project and the blocks as a list.
**It never:** gives you a fourth move, books time you didn't pick, invents a count, or lets "apply to twenty more" stand as a plan — one volume move gets swapped for a positioning move, because the product's philosophy is fewer, better, and never below your minimum pay.
**Make it automatic:** say **"schedule this"** for every Monday. A scheduled run reads your board and log and saves the plan for review — it doesn't touch your calendar or draft outreach on its own.
